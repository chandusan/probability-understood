Order, groups & constraints · Slide 19

Keep related books together

Imagine fastening a removable band around every subject's books. A shelf arrangement now has two layers: the order of the subject bundles, and the order of the individual books inside each bundle. Counting those layers separately turns a difficult-looking restriction into ordinary permutations.

~2 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

Translate the shelf into four blocks

Mr. Jones has ten books: four mathematics, three chemistry, two history, and one language book. All books of the same subject must be adjacent. We treat the ten books as distinct individual books, even when they share a subject.

Represent the subject blocks by $M,C,H,L$. There are

$$ 4!=24 $$

ways to order these four blocks. For example, $H,M,L,C$ means the two history books come first, then the four mathematics books, then the language book, then the three chemistry books. The different block lengths do not change the number of block orders.

Open each bundle

For every one of those 24 block orders, there are independently:

Inside a block Possible orders
Four distinct mathematics books $4!=24$
Three distinct chemistry books $3!=6$
Two distinct history books $2!=2$
One language book $1!=1$

The multiplication rule gives

$$ \boxed{4!\times4!\times3!\times2!\times1!=6912.} $$

The two $4!$ factors do different jobs. The first orders four subject blocks; the second orders four mathematics books.

Why every valid shelf is counted exactly once

See the one-to-one correspondence

Given a block order and an internal order for each subject, concatenate the blocks. You get one valid shelf.

Conversely, look at any valid shelf. Since every subject's books are together, its sequence of subject blocks is uniquely determined. Reading each block from left to right also uniquely determines its internal order. Thus two different specifications cannot produce the same shelf, and every valid shelf has a specification.

This proves that the multiplication counts precisely the shelves we want: nothing omitted and nothing repeated.

Counting $10!$ would allow subjects to be mixed. Counting only $4!$ would forget that swapping two mathematics books changes the shelf. If books within a subject were genuinely indistinguishable, only the block order would remain visible and the answer would be 24; that is a different model.

Check your understanding

Suppose the language block must be at the far left, but every other requirement stays the same. How many arrangements remain?

Reveal the count

Only the three remaining subject blocks can be reordered, giving $3!$ block orders. The internal orders are unchanged:

$$ 3!\times4!\times3!\times2!\times1!=1728. $$

This is one quarter of 6912, consistent with fixing one of four possible positions for the language block.
