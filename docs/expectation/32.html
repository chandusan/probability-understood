Shape & complete moment examples · Slide 32

Verify the mean and spread, then finish the third moments

For $P(X=1)=1/2$, $P(X=2)=1/3$, and $P(X=3)=1/6$, the source works through the mean and standard deviation. We will check those calculations and then complete both third-moment quantities from the preceding problem.

~2 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

Mean and variance directly from their definitions

$$ \mu=1\frac12+2\frac13+3\frac16 =\frac36+\frac46+\frac36=\frac53. $$

Subtract the mean before squaring:

$$ \begin{aligned} \sigma^2&=\left(1-\frac53\right)^2\frac12 +\left(2-\frac53\right)^2\frac13 +\left(3-\frac53\right)^2\frac16\\ &=\frac29+\frac1{27}+\frac8{27} =\frac{15}{27}=\frac59. \end{aligned} $$

Therefore $\sigma=\sqrt5/3$. The square root applies to the entire variance $5/9$, so $\sqrt{5/9}=\sqrt5/3$.

A shorter variance check

Compute $E(X^2)=1/2+4/3+9/6=10/3$. Then

$$ E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2 =\frac{10}{3}-\frac{25}{9}=\frac59. $$

The two methods must agree. The shortcut is especially convenient when raw moments have already been tabulated.

Derive a third-central-moment shortcut

Expand the entire cube before averaging:

$$ (X-\mu)^3=X^3-3\mu X^2+3\mu^2X-\mu^3. $$

Because $E(X)=\mu$, this gives

$$ \mu_3^0=E(X^3)-3\mu E(X^2)+2\mu^3. $$

With $E(X^3)=23/3$,

$$ \begin{aligned} \mu_3^0 &=\frac{23}{3}-3\frac53\frac{10}{3}+2\left(\frac53\right)^3\\ &=\frac{207}{27}-\frac{450}{27}+\frac{250}{27} =\boxed{\frac7{27}}. \end{aligned} $$

This agrees with directly cubing the three deviations. Both approaches distinguish $E(X^3)=23/3$ from $E[(X-\mu)^3]=7/27$: the first measures powers around zero, while the second measures powers around the mean.
