Average transformed variables · Slide 19

Solve the reciprocal-cost expectation exercise

Let $X$ have density $f(x)=x^2(2x+3/2)$ for $0<x<1$, zero elsewhere. Find $E(2/X+3)$. We will state the full integral, separate the transformed quantity into useful pieces, and show why the behavior near zero causes no divergence.

~2 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

Confirm the density first

Expand $f(x)=2x^3+(3/2)x^2$. It is nonnegative on its support, and

$$ \int_0^1f(x)\,dx =\left[\frac{x^4}{2}+\frac{x^3}{2}\right]_0^1=1. $$

The fraction in the source is $3/2$ inside the parentheses, so the density is $x^2(2x+3/2)$.

Write the complete expectation

$$ E\left(\frac2X+3\right) =\int_0^1\left(\frac2x+3\right)x^2\left(2x+\frac32\right)dx. $$

We may separate a sum and factor out fixed constants:

$$E\left(\frac2X+3\right)=2E(1/X)+3.$$

The second term is $3$, because the expectation of a constant is that constant.

Calculate the one nonconstant piece

$$ \begin{aligned} E(1/X)&=\int_0^1\frac1x\,x^2\left(2x+\frac32\right)dx\\ &=\int_0^1\left(2x^2+\frac32x\right)dx\\ &=\left[\frac23x^3+\frac34x^2\right]_0^1\\ &=\frac23+\frac34=\frac{17}{12}. \end{aligned} $$

Therefore

$$ \boxed{E\left(\frac2X+3\right)=2\frac{17}{12}+3 =\frac{35}{6}\approx5.8333.} $$

Check by expanding the original integral

Multiplying out its integrand gives

$$ \left(\frac2x+3\right)\left(2x^3+\frac32x^2\right) =6x^3+\frac{17}{2}x^2+3x. $$

Its integral is $3/2+17/6+3/2=35/6$, confirming the result. The simplified integrand remains integrable near zero, even though the transformed value $2/x+3$ is unbounded there.

Since $0<X<1$, we always have $2/X+3>5$. The resulting expectation $35/6>5$ passes this basic range check.
