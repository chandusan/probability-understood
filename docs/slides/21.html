Understanding spread · Slide 21

Two expensive diamonds change the story

Add two prices far above the original group and the endpoints move dramatically. The middle ranks barely shift. This is a concrete demonstration of what we mean when we call IQR and the median more resistant to extremes.

~2 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

Add the two observations

The original 42 diamonds are accidentally mixed with two higher-quality diamonds priced at $7,393 and $8,979. The resulting 44-observation sample has these supplied summaries:

Statistic New value
Number of diamonds $n$ 44
Minimum $416
First quartile $Q_1$ $1,429
Median $1,899
Mean $2,209
Third quartile $Q_3$ $2,537
Maximum $8,979
Sample standard deviation $s$ $1,497

In the histogram, the original observations remain clustered below roughly $3,200. Two isolated observations now sit far to the right, near $7,400 and $9,000. The price axis expands to accommodate them.

Calculate the new spread

$$ \operatorname{IQR}_{\text{new}}=2537-1429=1108\text{ dollars}, $$

$$ R_{\text{new}}=8979-416=8563\text{ dollars}. $$

Now compare every supplied summary:

Statistic Original 42 Mixed 44 Change
Minimum $416 $416 $0
$Q_1$ $1,416 $1,429 $13
Median $1,882 $1,899 $17
Mean $1,924 $2,209 $285
$Q_3$ $2,497 $2,537 $40
Maximum $3,154 $8,979 $5,825
IQR $1,081 $1,108 $27
Range $2,738 $8,563 $5,825
Sample SD $703 $1,497 $794

The two exercise answers are therefore

$$ \Delta\operatorname{IQR}=1108-1081=27, $$

$$ \Delta R=8563-2738=5825. $$

Range more than triples. IQR increases by only about 2.5%.

Why the summaries react differently

Range follows the endpoints. The minimum did not change, so the range increases by exactly the increase in the maximum.

IQR follows middle ranks. The new observations lie at the top, but adding them changes the sample size and can change which ranks determine the quartiles. That explains the small movement. Robustness does not promise zero movement.

The mean follows the total. The high prices enter with their full values. Using the rounded old mean gives this consistency check:

$$ \frac{42(1924)+7393+8979}{44}\approx2208.64, $$

which rounds to the displayed $2,209. This is an approximate check, since the old mean itself was rounded.

The median follows a middle position. It moves only from $1,882 to $1,899.

Standard deviation uses squared distances. The large departures make it more than double, from $703 to $1,497. We will soon derive that calculation and its units.

The important contrast is not simply “some statistics change.” It is what each statistic pays attention to: endpoints, totals, squared distances, or middle ranks.

An outlier is not automatically an error

These prices are unusual relative to the low-quality group, but the source supplies a meaningful explanation: they come from higher-quality diamonds. A flag should prompt investigation. It does not by itself justify deleting an observation.

We are using the supplied summary values, not reconstructing a synthetic set of 44 prices from the plot.

Check your understanding

Why is the IQR increase $27 even though $Q_3$ increased by $40?

Reveal the calculation

The lower quartile increased too, by $13. The interval's width therefore changes by $40-13=27$. Moving both endpoints to the right is partly a location change; only their difference changes the width.
