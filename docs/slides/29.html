Normal models & practice · Slide 29

Calculate spread from eight heart rates

The calculation follows a simple path: find the center, measure each deviation, square, add, divide, and take a square root. Keeping the units visible tells us what each stage means.

~3 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

Find the center

The eight resting heart rates are

$$ 50,\ 51,\ 57,\ 61,\ 70,\ 71,\ 80,\ 84. $$

Their total is $524$, so

$$ \bar x=\frac{524}{8}=65.5\text{ beats per minute}. $$

Let each observation contribute its squared distance

Resting heart rate Deviation from 65.5 Squared deviation
50 $50-65.5=-15.5$ 240.25
51 $51-65.5=-14.5$ 210.25
57 $57-65.5=-8.5$ 72.25
61 $61-65.5=-4.5$ 20.25
70 $70-65.5=4.5$ 20.25
71 $71-65.5=5.5$ 30.25
80 $80-65.5=14.5$ 210.25
84 $84-65.5=18.5$ 342.25
Total 0 1146

The signed deviations sum to zero. That cancellation makes their simple average useless as a spread measure. Squaring prevents cancellation and gives extra weight to larger departures.

Divide, then recover the original units

There are $n=8$ observations. The usual sample variance divides the squared-deviation total by $n-1=7$:

$$ s^2=\frac{1146}{7} \approx163.7142857\ (\text{beats per minute})^2. $$

Take the square root:

$$ s=\sqrt{\frac{1146}{7}} \approx12.7951\text{ beats per minute} \approx12.8\text{ beats per minute}. $$

Use the unrounded variance in the calculation when possible. The slide's rounded route, $\sqrt{163.71}$, still gives the same one-decimal result.

Interpret the result without changing its definition

A precise statement is:

The sample is centered at 65.5 beats per minute, with a sample standard deviation of approximately 12.8 beats per minute.

SD is a scale for spread around the mean. The slide's phrase “roughly 12.8 away from the mean, on average” is informal: it must not be confused with the arithmetic mean of the absolute distances.

Calculate the actual average absolute distance

The absolute deviations sum to

$$ 15.5+14.5+8.5+4.5+4.5+5.5+14.5+18.5=86. $$

Their arithmetic mean is

$$ \frac18\sum_{i=1}^{8}|x_i-65.5| =\frac{86}{8}=10.75\text{ beats per minute}. $$

That is 10.75, not 12.8. The measures differ because SD squares deviations, uses the sample divisor $n-1$, and then takes a square root.

Neither the mean nor SD says that every observation is one SD from the center. These two summaries also do not prove normality. We cannot automatically apply the 68% rule to these eight observations without assessing the distributional shape.

Four useful checks

  • The mean lies between the minimum and maximum.
  • Signed deviations sum to zero.
  • Squared deviations are nonnegative.
  • The final SD has the same units as the heart rates.

Check your understanding

If every heart rate increased by 10 beats per minute, would you need to redo the squared-deviation table? What would the new mean and SD be?

Show the answer

The new mean would be $75.5$. Each observation and the mean both increase by 10, so every deviation and squared deviation stays the same. SD remains approximately $12.8$ beats per minute.
