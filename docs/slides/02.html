Two meanings of the center

There is more than one sensible meaning of “the center.” The mean preserves the total by sharing it equally; the median identifies the middle position after sorting. They can give different answers because they preserve different information.

~2 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

“Central tendency” is a name for the attempt to describe the location of a collection of observations with one representative value.

Suppose the waiting times are:

$$ 3,\quad 6,\quad 9. $$

Six looks like a natural center. But there are at least two distinct reasons:

  • The total waiting time is $18$ minutes, and an equal share of that total is $18/3=6$.
  • After sorting the observations, the middle waiting time is $6$.

These two reasons lead to two different definitions:

Summary The question it answers
Mean If the total were shared equally, what would each observation receive?
Median After ordering the observations, what value occupies the middle?

The answers happen to agree for $3,6,9$. They need not agree in general. For $3,6,12$, the equal-share value is $7$, while the middle observation remains $6$.

Neither answer is automatically wrong. Each preserves a different feature of the data.

Where this fits in exploratory data analysis

Exploratory data analysis means inspecting the data before relying on a compact numerical story: look at the distribution, unusual values, measurement quality, and relationships. A measure of central tendency describes location. It does not imply that every distribution has one obvious “typical” value, or that a distribution literally converges toward its center.

Important distinctions

  • EDA includes inspecting distributions, data quality, relationships, and unusual values. It is broader than calculating a center, and it need not begin with an expected-value calculation.
  • A distribution does not literally have to “converge” toward a center. Here the useful idea is location.
  • A finite list of finite numerical observations has an arithmetic mean. A population distribution need not have a finite expected value. Those are different settings.

Check your understanding

Three jobs cost $50$, $50$, and $200$ units to complete. Which center describes the middle job, and which center preserves the total when multiplied by three?

Work through the answer

The median is $50$: it is the middle value of the sorted list. The mean is $(50+50+200)/3=100$. Multiplying the mean by three returns the total cost of $300$; multiplying the median by three does not. This does not make either summary wrong. They answer different questions about the same jobs.
