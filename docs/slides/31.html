Normal models & practice · Slide 31

See what changed before calculating SD

Ask three questions: did the center move, did distances stretch, and did the sample size change? These structural questions explain the four comparisons in the slide more clearly than memorizing separate answers.

~4 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

The four choices

The slide asks for comparisons without calculations using these statements:

Statement Meaning
I Column A is greater.
II Column B is greater.
III The two quantities are equal.
IV The relationship cannot be determined from the information given.

Each entry below asks for the dataset's standard deviation:

Row Column A Column B
1 $\{0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8\}$ $\{2,4,6,8\}$
2 $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ $\{3,5,7,9,1\}$
3 $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ $\{1,3,5,7,9,9\}$
4 $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ $\{1,3,5,5,7,9\}$

Try a prediction first. The answers are II, III, II, I. The calculations below use sample variance; using population variance consistently would give the same four comparisons here.

1. Multiply every distance by ten

Column B is column A multiplied by 10. Its SD is therefore ten times as large:

$$ s_B=10s_A. $$

Answer: II. Variance is multiplied by $10^2=100$, while SD is multiplied by 10.

Check the exact numbers

Column A has mean $0.5$ and squared-deviation total

$$ SS_A=(-0.3)^2+(-0.1)^2+(0.1)^2+(0.3)^2=0.2=\frac15. $$

Therefore

$$ s_A^2=\frac1{15}, \qquad s_A=\frac1{\sqrt{15}}\approx0.2582. $$

Column B has mean 5 and squared-deviation total $9+1+1+9=20$, giving

$$ s_B^2=\frac{20}{3}=100s_A^2, \qquad s_B=\sqrt{\frac{20}{3}}\approx2.5820=10s_A. $$

2. Change the order, keep the observations

The columns contain the same five values in different orders. Their means and squared-deviation totals are unchanged.

Answer: III. Order is irrelevant to this collection's SD. Order could matter for a time-series question, but that is a different question.

For either column,

$$ \bar x=5,\qquad SS=(-4)^2+(-2)^2+0^2+2^2+4^2=40, $$

so

$$ s^2=\frac{40}{4}=10, \qquad s=\sqrt{10}\approx3.1623. $$

3. Add another upper-end observation

Column B adds another 9. Its mean moves upward, and more weight sits at the upper end. For these specific values, the added spread outweighs the larger variance divisor.

Answer: II. The new sample variance is $32/3\approx10.6667$, compared with the original 10.

Do not turn this into “adding a maximum always increases SD.” The center, squared-deviation total, and divisor all change. The exact comparison verifies the answer for this dataset.

Calculate the shifted mean and new variance

The new mean is

$$ \bar x_B=\frac{1+3+5+7+9+9}{6}=\frac{17}{3}. $$

The squared-deviation total is

$$ \begin{aligned} SS_B &=\left(-\frac{14}{3}\right)^2 +\left(-\frac8{3}\right)^2 +\left(-\frac2{3}\right)^2 +\left(\frac4{3}\right)^2 +\left(\frac{10}{3}\right)^2 +\left(\frac{10}{3}\right)^2\\ &=\frac{196+64+4+16+100+100}{9} =\frac{160}{3}. \end{aligned} $$

Thus

$$ s_B^2=\frac{160/3}{5}=\frac{32}{3}>10, \qquad s_B=\sqrt{\frac{32}{3}}\approx3.2660>\sqrt{10}\approx3.1623. $$

Square root preserves the order of nonnegative numbers, so comparing variances is enough to compare SDs.

A shortcut for appending one observation

Appending a value $y$ to a sample of size $n$ gives

$$ SS_{\mathrm{new}} =SS_{\mathrm{old}}+\frac{n}{n+1}(y-\bar x_{\mathrm{old}})^2. $$

To understand the correction, the new mean shifts by $(y-\bar x_{\mathrm{old}})/(n+1)$. Re-centering the old observations adds $n$ times this squared shift; the new observation's squared residual adds the remaining contribution. Combining them gives the displayed formula.

Here,

$$ SS_{\mathrm{new}}=40+\frac56(9-5)^2=\frac{160}{3}. $$

Then divide by the new sample divisor 5. The squared-deviation total grows enough to outweigh that divisor's increase from 4 to 5.

4. Add an observation exactly at the mean

Column B adds another 5. The mean stays at 5, the new squared deviation is zero, and the existing squared deviations do not change. The total stays 40 while the divisor rises from 4 to 5.

Answer: I.

$$ s_A^2=\frac{40}{4}=10, \qquad s_B^2=\frac{40}{5}=8. $$

Thus $s_A=\sqrt{10}\approx3.1623$ exceeds $s_B=\sqrt8\approx2.8284$. The same total squared spread has a larger divisor.

Keep the patterns together

Row Change Sample variance in A Sample variance in B Answer
1 Scale every value by 10 $1/15$ $20/3$ II
2 Reorder the same values 10 10 III
3 Add another 9 10 $32/3$ II
4 Add another 5 10 8 I

Check your understanding

You append the old mean to any sample with $n\ge2$ and positive sample variance. Does the new sample mean change? Does the sample variance rise or fall?

Show the answer

The mean stays unchanged. The appended observation has zero deviation, so the squared-deviation total also stays unchanged. The sample-variance divisor grows from $n-1$ to $n$, giving

$$ s_{\mathrm{new}}^2=\frac{n-1}{n}s_{\mathrm{old}}^2. $$

It decreases when the original variance is positive. If the original variance is zero, it remains zero.

Exercise 01 / Slide 12

Compare the mean and median from a histogram

The slide shows three histograms, described here so that the exercise can be attempted without opening the slide:

Histogram Shape shown
Shoe sizes One central peak, with roughly balanced tails on the two sides.
Years of car ownership Most observations are toward the smaller values, with a long tail toward larger values.
Winter daily low temperatures Most observations are toward the warmer values, with a long tail toward colder, more negative temperatures.

For each histogram, would you expect the mean and median to be approximately equal, the mean to be greater than the median, or the mean to be less than the median? Explain your choices.

Hint from the slide: the mean tends to follow the skew of the histogram.

Show solutionHide solution: Compare the mean and median from a histogram

The median locates the middle observation after sorting. The mean adds every value and divides by the count, so values far from the center can pull it toward themselves.

Histogram Expected comparison Why
Shoe sizes Mean approximately equals median. The two sides roughly balance, so neither tail pulls the mean much more than the other.
Car ownership Mean greater than median. The long right tail contains unusually large values that pull the mean upward.
Winter daily lows Mean less than median. The long left tail contains unusually low, negative temperatures that pull the mean downward.

“Warmer” is to the right even when all the temperatures are negative: for example, $-3>-12$. A tail toward $-12$ pulls the mean left.

These are qualitative expectations for the displayed shapes. A rough histogram does not give the exact mean or median, and the tail heuristic is not a theorem for every possible irregular distribution.

Exercise 02 / Slide 13

Interpret a score in two classes

A midterm is scored out of 100. The class mean is 76, and your score is 88. Compare your performance in the two scenarios depicted on the slide:

  • Scenario 1: scores are tightly concentrated around 76; only a small right-hand tail extends to 88 and beyond.
  • Scenario 2: scores are spread much more widely around the same mean; a visibly larger fraction extends to 88 and beyond.

How would you interpret your score in each scenario? What does the comparison show about reporting an average without reporting spread?

Show solutionHide solution: Interpret a score in two classes

In both classes, your score is $88-76=12$ points above the mean. That fact alone is identical in the two scenarios.

In Scenario 1, most classmates are close to 76. A score of 88 is unusually high compared with this concentrated group; few classmates scored as high.

In Scenario 2, scores vary widely. A 12-point difference from the mean is less unusual, and more classmates scored around or above 88.

Thus the same raw score and same mean can imply different relative standing. We need information about spread as well as the center. If the standard deviations were supplied, the standardized distance would be

$$ z=\frac{88-76}{s}=\frac{12}{s}. $$

A smaller $s$ makes the same 12-point difference a larger number of standard deviations. The picture supports a qualitative comparison, but it does not supply enough exact data to calculate a percentile or a numerical $z$-score.

Exercise 03 / Slides 20, 21

Diamond prices before and after two outliers

The original dataset contains sale prices of 42 low-quality diamonds, each smaller than one carat. Two high-quality diamonds priced at 7,393 and 8,979 US dollars are accidentally added. The slide supplies these summaries; monetary entries are in US dollars.

Statistic Original 42 diamonds After adding the two diamonds
Sample size 42 44
Minimum 416 416
First quartile $Q_1$ 1,416 1,429
Median 1,882 1,899
Mean 1,924 2,209
Third quartile $Q_3$ 2,497 2,537
Maximum 3,154 8,979
Sample standard deviation $s$ 703 1,497

(a) Original dataset

Compute its interquartile range (IQR) and range.

(b) After adding the outliers

Compute the new IQR and range. How do these summaries change, and what does this show about their sensitivity to outliers?

Show solutionHide solution: Diamond prices before and after two outliers

The two measures use different parts of the data:

$$ \operatorname{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1, \qquad \operatorname{Range}=\operatorname{Maximum}-\operatorname{Minimum}. $$

The IQR measures the width of the middle half of the observations. The range measures the distance between the two most extreme observations.

(a) Original dataset

$$ \operatorname{IQR}=2497-1416=\boxed{1081\text{ dollars}}, $$

$$ \operatorname{Range}=3154-416=\boxed{2738\text{ dollars}}. $$

(b) After adding the outliers

$$ \operatorname{IQR}=2537-1429=\boxed{1108\text{ dollars}}, $$

$$ \operatorname{Range}=8979-416=\boxed{8563\text{ dollars}}. $$

The IQR increases by only $1108-1081=27$ dollars, about $2.5\%$. The range increases by $8563-2738=5825$ dollars and becomes more than three times its old size.

The new maximum enters the range calculation directly. The two outliers have much less effect on the middle half of the data, so the IQR changes only slightly. Robust means resistant to extreme observations, not completely unchanged by them.

The other supplied summaries tell the same story: the mean rises by 285 dollars and the standard deviation rises by 794 dollars, while the median rises by only 17 dollars.

Exercise 04 / Slide 30

Rank the variability of professor ratings

Students rate four hypothetical professors on a scale from 1 to 5. The following table transcribes the number of dots at each rating in the slide's dot plots.

Professor Rating 1 Rating 2 Rating 3 Rating 4 Rating 5
A 1 2 4 2 1
B 0 0 6 0 0
C 5 0 0 0 5
D 2 2 2 2 2

Arrange the professors from smallest to largest variability in ratings. Explain your reasoning from the distributions before calculating.

Show solutionHide solution: Rank the variability of professor ratings

All four distributions have mean 3, but their distances from 3 differ.

  • B: all six ratings equal 3. Every distance from the mean is zero, so there is no variability.
  • A: most ratings are at 3 or close to it. Only two are at the extreme values 1 and 5.
  • D: ratings are evenly spread across the five values. Compared with A, more ratings lie at the extremes and fewer lie at the center.
  • C: every rating is at 1 or 5, two units from the mean. This has the greatest spread.

Therefore,

$$ \boxed{B<A<D<C.} $$

To verify, weight each squared distance by its count. For A,

$$ SS_A=1(1-3)^2+2(2-3)^2+4(3-3)^2+2(4-3)^2+1(5-3)^2=12. $$

Similarly, $SS_B=0$, $SS_D=2(4+1+0+1+4)=20$, and $SS_C=5(4)+5(4)=40$.

Professor Number of ratings Sum of squared distances Sample variance
B 6 0 $0/5=0$
A 10 12 $12/9=4/3$
D 10 20 $20/9$
C 10 40 $40/9$

Taking square roots preserves the order, so the standard deviations have the same ranking. The unequal sample size for B causes no difficulty: its squared-distance total is zero.

Exercise 05 / Slide 31

Compare standard deviations after changing the data

For each row, compare the standard deviations of the two datasets. First try to reason without calculating the standard deviations. Choose:

  • I: the standard deviation in column A is greater.
  • II: the standard deviation in column B is greater.
  • III: the standard deviations are equal.
  • IV: the relationship cannot be determined from the information given.
Row Column A Column B
1 $\{0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8\}$ $\{2,4,6,8\}$
2 $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ $\{3,5,7,9,1\}$
3 $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ $\{1,3,5,7,9,9\}$
4 $\{1,3,5,7,9\}$ $\{1,3,5,5,7,9\}$
Show solutionHide solution: Compare standard deviations after changing the data

The answers, in order, are II, III, II, I.

Row 1: all values are multiplied by 10

Column B stretches every distance from the mean by a factor of 10. Therefore its standard deviation is ten times as large: $s_B=10s_A$. Answer: II.

Row 2: only the order changes

These are exactly the same observations. Reordering does not change the mean or the distances from it. Answer: III.

Row 3: another 9 is added

Column B gives the upper extreme additional weight. For these particular values its standard deviation is slightly larger. Answer: II. The important qualification is that adding an extreme value does not always increase standard deviation: the center and the divisor change as well.

A short verification resolves this less obvious comparison. For A, the mean is 5 and

$$ SS_A=16+4+0+4+16=40,\qquad s_A^2=40/4=10. $$

For B, the mean is $34/6=17/3$. Its deviations are $-14/3,-8/3,-2/3,4/3,10/3,10/3$, so

$$ SS_B=\frac{196+64+4+16+100+100}{9}=\frac{160}{3}, $$

$$ s_B^2=\frac{160/3}{5}=\frac{32}{3}>10. $$

Comparing variances is sufficient: square root preserves their order.

Row 4: another observation at the mean is added

The added value is 5, exactly the original mean. The mean stays 5. The new squared distance is zero, so the squared-distance total remains 40. But the sample-variance divisor grows from 4 to 5:

$$ s_A^2=40/4=10,\qquad s_B^2=40/5=8. $$

Therefore A has the larger standard deviation. Answer: I.

These calculations use sample standard deviations. If population standard deviations are used consistently instead, the same four comparisons hold for these datasets.
