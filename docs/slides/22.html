Understanding spread · Slide 22

Boxplots: endpoints are not always whiskers

A boxplot compresses a distribution into a box, a median line, and two whiskers. To read it correctly, separate actual sample extrema, calculated fences, and whisker endpoints. They can be three different things.

~4 min reading/Source + intuition + practice/1 interactive experiment
Let’s understand it

Read the box first

The box runs from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$, so its length is IQR. The line inside marks the median. The middle 50% is the guiding interpretation, with the usual finite-sample and quantile-convention qualifications.

A boxplot can help us compare centers, dispersion, asymmetry, and potential anomalies without assuming normality. It is a compact picture, however: it does not reveal every cluster, gap, or peak that a histogram might show.

The ordinary five-number summary is

$$ \text{actual minimum},\quad Q_1,\quad\text{median},\quad Q_3,\quad\text{actual maximum}. $$

The source's labels “minimum” and “maximum” exclude outliers. Those describe possible whisker endpoints, not the actual extrema of a five-number summary.

Construct a common Tukey boxplot

First calculate two fences:

$$ \text{lower fence}=Q_1-1.5\operatorname{IQR}, $$

$$ \text{upper fence}=Q_3+1.5\operatorname{IQR}. $$

Then draw each whisker to the most extreme observed value inside its fence. Plot observations beyond the fences separately.

The fence is a threshold calculated from the quartiles. The whisker endpoint is an observation. A whisker does not usually end exactly at its fence.

Work the 44-diamond example

For the mixed sample,

$$ Q_1=1429,\qquad Q_3=2537,\qquad\operatorname{IQR}=1108. $$

Therefore

$$ 1.5\operatorname{IQR}=1662, $$

$$ \text{lower fence}=1429-1662=-233, $$

$$ \text{upper fence}=2537+1662=4199. $$

The original 42 observations all lie from 416 through 3154, so all are still within these new fences. The added observations, 7393 and 8979, lie beyond the upper fence.

Item Value for the 44 diamonds
Actual five-number summary 416, 1429, 1899, 2537, 8979
Lower and upper fences −233 and 4199
Tukey whisker endpoints 416 and 3154
Separately plotted high observations 7393 and 8979
Actual maximum 8979

The actual maximum remains 8979. Flagging it does not remove it from the sample or change its value.

Try it: predict where the upper whisker stops

Before comparing the diagrams, predict whether the mixed sample's upper whisker should end at 3154, 4199, or 8979. Then compare its actual extrema with its Tukey whiskers and locate the two separately displayed expensive diamonds.

Explore the ideaOpen larger ↗

Look for three roles: 3154 is an observed value inside the fence; 4199 is a calculated boundary; 8979 is an observed value beyond it. Only the first is the Tukey upper-whisker endpoint.

For the original 42-diamond sample, the fences are

$$ 1416-1.5(1081)=-205.5, $$

$$ 2497+1.5(1081)=4118.5. $$

Both original extrema, 416 and 3154, are inside, so that sample's whiskers coincide with its actual minimum and maximum.

Avoid three misleading readings

“Flagged means wrong.” A point outside a fence is worth investigating. The rule does not prove a recording error; here the higher quality of the new diamonds explains their higher prices. A negative lower fence also does not mean any negative prices were observed.

“Exactly 25% are below $Q_1$.” That statement can fail for a finite or tied sample. Quartile conventions estimate the quarter landmarks; they need not divide every small dataset into exact counts of quarters.

“The median is halfway between the minimum and maximum.” It is a middle position in sorted data, not generally the numerical midpoint of the two extremes.

Boxplot conventions vary: some whiskers reach actual extrema, others use Tukey's rule or specified percentiles. Read the convention before interpreting the endpoints. Two different distributions can also share every displayed summary, so use a histogram or individual observations when shape matters.

Check your understanding

A boxplot's upper fence is 100. Its largest observations are 90, 95, and 140. Where does a Tukey upper whisker end, and what is the actual maximum?

Reveal the reasoning

The upper whisker ends at 95, the largest observation within the fence. The actual maximum is 140, shown separately as a potential outlier. The fence at 100 need not be an observed value.
