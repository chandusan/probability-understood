Turn outcomes into numbers · Slide 03

Three ads: add path probabilities to find the click count

A user sees three ads. The first is clicked with probability $0.05$. After any click, the next ad is clicked with probability $0.15$; after an ignored ad, the next click probability returns to $0.05$. Let $X$ be the total number clicked. We want the probability of each possible value $X=0,1,2,3$.

~3 min reading/Source + intuition + practice/1 interactive experiment
Let’s understand it

Define the outcomes and the dependence

Write $C$ for “click” and $N$ for “no click.” An outcome is an ordered three-letter sequence such as $CNC$. The random variable counts its $C$ letters, so $X(CNC)=2$.

We use the slide's rule as a model in which the next response depends on the immediately previous response:

Previous response Probability of next $C$ Probability of next $N$
$C$ $0.15$ $0.85$
$N$ $0.05$ $0.95$

For the first ad, use $P(C)=0.05$ and $P(N)=0.95$. Every probability of ignoring an ad is one minus the corresponding click probability.

Multiply conditional probabilities along one path

For $CNN$, the factors mean:

  1. Click the first ad: probability $0.05$.
  2. Ignore the second given a first click: probability $0.85$.
  3. Ignore the third given a second nonclick: probability $0.95$.

Thus

$$P(CNN)=0.05\cdot0.85\cdot0.95=0.040375.$$

This is the chain rule for dependent responses. The final factor returns to $0.95$ because the immediately preceding response was $N$; the first click does not keep the higher click probability active forever.

Enumerate all eight paths

The table includes every possible sequence exactly once. Its probabilities are products of the successive conditional factors, not $1/8$ each.

Path $X$ Probability product Exact probability
$NNN$ 0 $0.95\cdot0.95\cdot0.95$ $0.857375$
$CNN$ 1 $0.05\cdot0.85\cdot0.95$ $0.040375$
$NCN$ 1 $0.95\cdot0.05\cdot0.85$ $0.040375$
$NNC$ 1 $0.95\cdot0.95\cdot0.05$ $0.045125$
$NCC$ 2 $0.95\cdot0.05\cdot0.15$ $0.007125$
$CCN$ 2 $0.05\cdot0.15\cdot0.85$ $0.006375$
$CNC$ 2 $0.05\cdot0.85\cdot0.05$ $0.002125$
$CCC$ 3 $0.05\cdot0.15\cdot0.15$ $0.001125$

For example, the three two-click paths have different probabilities even though $X$ assigns all three the same value. The original outcome contains more information than the final count.

Group paths with the same numerical value

Different paths are mutually exclusive outcomes, so we add their probabilities within each group:

$$ \begin{aligned} P(X=0)&=P(NNN)=0.857375,\\ P(X=1)&=P(CNN)+P(NCN)+P(NNC)\\ &=0.040375+0.040375+0.045125=0.125875,\\ P(X=2)&=P(NCC)+P(CCN)+P(CNC)\\ &=0.007125+0.006375+0.002125=0.015625,\\ P(X=3)&=P(CCC)=0.001125. \end{aligned} $$

The distribution is therefore:

Clicks $x$ $P(X=x)$ Percentage
0 $0.857375$ $85.7375\%$
1 $0.125875$ $12.5875\%$
2 $0.015625$ $1.5625\%$
3 $0.001125$ $0.1125\%$

The probabilities sum to one. There are no other possible click counts, so $P(X=x)=0$ outside $\{0,1,2,3\}$.

Why this count is not binomial

A binomial count requires independent trials with the same success probability. Here the chance of the next click changes with the preceding response, so the assumptions fail. For example, an independent $\operatorname{Binomial}(3,0.05)$ model would give $P(X=3)=0.05^3=0.000125$, whereas the stated model gives $0.001125$.

Having three trials and two outcomes per trial is not enough to establish a binomial distribution. The conditional mechanism determines the correct path weights.

Check your understanding

What is the probability of at least one click? Calculate it from both the positive-count rows and the no-click complement.

Reveal the reasoning

Adding gives $0.125875+0.015625+0.001125=0.142625$. The complement gives $1-P(NNN)=1-0.95^3=0.142625$. All three factors in $P(NNN)$ are $0.95$ because the path never contains a click.
