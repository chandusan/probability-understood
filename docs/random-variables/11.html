Probability mass & accumulation · Slide 11

Build and read the dice sum's cumulative staircase

For the sum $X$ of two independent fair six-sided dice, the CDF is $F(x)=P(X\le x)$. It rises whenever the threshold reaches a possible sum and stays flat between consecutive sums. The jump at each integer equals the PMF value there.

~3 min reading/Source + intuition + practice/1 interactive experiment
Let’s understand it

Accumulate the probabilities one value at a time

The PMF numerator counts are

$$1,2,3,4,5,6,5,4,3,2,1$$

for sums 2 through 12, with denominator 36. Their running totals are

$$1,3,6,10,15,21,26,30,33,35,36.$$

For example,

$$F(6)=\frac{1+2+3+4+5}{36}=\frac{15}{36},$$

and reaching 7 adds six more outcomes:

$$F(7)=F(6)+p(7)=\frac{15+6}{36}=\frac{21}{36}.$$

The complete CDF can be read from this table:

Threshold $x$ Values of $X$ included $F(x)$
$x<2$ None $0$
$2\le x<3$ 2 $1/36$
$3\le x<4$ 2 through 3 $3/36$
$4\le x<5$ 2 through 4 $6/36$
$5\le x<6$ 2 through 5 $10/36$
$6\le x<7$ 2 through 6 $15/36$
$7\le x<8$ 2 through 7 $21/36$
$8\le x<9$ 2 through 8 $26/36$
$9\le x<10$ 2 through 9 $30/36$
$10\le x<11$ 2 through 10 $33/36$
$11\le x<12$ 2 through 11 $35/36$
$x\ge12$ All possible sums $1$

Each row includes its left endpoint and excludes its right endpoint. The new mass is already included when the threshold equals an integer.

Interpret the filled and open circles

At $x=7$, the source graph has an open circle at height $15/36$ and a filled circle at $21/36$. The filled circle is the actual value $F(7)$. The open circle indicates the value approached from below, before the probability of a sum of 7 has been included.

The vertical line drawn between them is a visual connector showing a jump. It does not give the function several values at $x=7$; a function has one value at each input.

For any threshold from 7 up to but not including 8, the included outcomes are unchanged. Thus $F(7)=F(7.5)=F(7.99)=21/36$. This is right-continuity in a concrete example.

Recover an individual mass from a jump

The new probability added at 8 is

$$p(8)=F(8)-F(8^-)=\frac{26}{36}-\frac{21}{36}=\frac5{36}.$$

$F(8^-)$ denotes the value approached from below 8. For this integer-valued variable, it equals $F(7)$. In general, the jump at any $x$ is $P(X=x)$.

The CDF therefore contains the same distributional information as the PMF, presented cumulatively instead of value by value.

Calculate interval and tail probabilities carefully

For a strict lower endpoint,

$$P(6<X\le9)=F(9)-F(6)=\frac{30-15}{36}=\frac5{12}.$$

This retains sums 7, 8, and 9. If 6 must also be included, subtract only the mass accumulated through 5:

$$P(6\le X\le9)=F(9)-F(5)=\frac{30-10}{36}=\frac59.$$

For an upper-tail question, complement the appropriate lower tail:

$$P(X>9)=1-F(9)=\frac6{36}=\frac16.$$

These calculations are all selections of PMF masses. The CDF simply provides convenient running totals.

Explore the PMF and CDF together

Begin with the standard fair-dice setting. Move the threshold across an integer and compare the newly included outcomes, the PMF mass at that sum, and the size of the CDF jump.

Check your understanding

Find $P(X<7)$ and $P(X\le7)$. Explain why they differ.

Reveal the reasoning

$P(X<7)=F(6)=15/36$, whereas $P(X\le7)=F(7)=21/36$. Their difference is $6/36=P(X=7)$. For a discrete variable with positive mass at the endpoint, replacing a strict inequality by an inclusive one changes the probability.
