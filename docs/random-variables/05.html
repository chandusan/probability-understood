Turn outcomes into numbers · Slide 05

Stop at the first II or UUU, not at any later occurrence

After each training epoch, a model's validation loss is classified as decreasing ($D$), increasing ($I$), or unchanged ($U$) relative to the previous value. Training stops immediately at two consecutive increases, $II$, or three consecutive unchanged results, $UUU$. Let $N$ count the evaluated epochs until stopping. Find its three smallest possible values and every exact outcome sequence for each.

~4 min reading/Source + intuition + practice/1 interactive experiment
Let’s understand it

Specify what the first label means

We assume a baseline loss is available before the first counted epoch, so epoch 1 can already be labeled $D$, $I$, or $U$. Under this convention, the number of letters in a stopping sequence equals $N$. If an initial baseline evaluation were itself counted as an additional epoch, the total count would shift by one.

An exact stopping sequence must satisfy two conditions:

  1. It triggers $II$ or $UUU$ at its final letter.
  2. No earlier prefix has already triggered either rule.

For example, $DII$ stops at epoch 3. The string $IIU$ is not a possible three-epoch stopping sequence: training would already have ended after its first two letters.

The smallest value is N = 2

Neither rule can trigger after one letter. After two letters, only the two-increase rule can trigger:

$$\boxed{\{N=2\}=\{II\}.}$$

Here the event notation lists the completed stopping strings associated with that value. A three-letter rule cannot be satisfied in two epochs.

List every sequence with N = 3

There are two ways to stop at the third epoch.

End with $II$. The sequence has the form $\_II$. Its first letter cannot be $I$, since $III$ would have stopped at epoch 2. It may be $D$ or $U$, producing $DII$ and $UII$.

End with $UUU$. The only three-letter possibility is $UUU$, and neither of its shorter prefixes triggers a rule.

Therefore

$$\boxed{\{N=3\}=\{DII,UII,UUU\}.}$$

List every sequence with N = 4

Again divide by the rule that triggers at the last epoch. These cases are disjoint because a sequence cannot end simultaneously in $II$ and $UUU$.

Case 1: the final two letters are $II$. Write the sequence as $abII$. The second letter $b$ cannot be $I$, since positions 2 and 3 would already form $II$. Thus $b$ is $D$ or $U$.

If $b=D$, the first letter may be any of $D,I,U$, giving

$$DDII,\quad IDII,\quad UDII.$$

If $b=U$, the first letter may again be any of $D,I,U$, giving

$$DUII,\quad IUII,\quad UUII.$$

None stops earlier: the first two letters are not $II$, and the first three end in $I$, so they cannot be $UUU$. This yields six valid sequences.

Case 2: the final three letters are $UUU$. Write the sequence as $aUUU$. The first letter cannot be $U$, because $UUUU$ would already stop at epoch 3. The choices $a=D$ and $a=I$ give

$$DUUU,\quad IUUU.$$

Neither contains an earlier $II$. These give two more valid sequences, so

$$ \boxed{\{N=4\}=\{DDII,IDII,UDII,DUII,IUII,UUII,DUUU,IUUU\}.} $$

Collect the complete answer

Value of $N$ Exact stopping sequences Number of sequences
2 $II$ 1
3 $DII,UII,UUU$ 3
4 $DDII,IDII,UDII,DUII,IUII,UUII,DUUU,IUUU$ 8

Thus the three smallest possible values are 2, 3, and 4. Checking every earlier prefix is essential; merely checking that the last letters form a trigger would overcount.

A list of possible values is not yet a distribution

The problem supplies no probabilities for $D,I,U$ and no assumptions about how outcomes depend on earlier epochs. We can enumerate possible stopping strings, but we cannot infer $P(N=2)$, $P(N=3)$, or $P(N=4)$ from their counts alone.

The rules also do not guarantee termination for every imaginable sequence: $DDDD\cdots$ never triggers either one. A full stochastic model would have to address whether nontermination has positive probability. That does not affect the finite stopping sequences requested here.

Check your understanding

Why is $DIII$ excluded from the list for $N=4$, while $IDII$ is included?

Reveal the reasoning

$DIII$ first triggers $II$ at positions 2–3, so it would stop at epoch 3 and never produce a fourth evaluated label. In $IDII$, the $D$ breaks the initial increase run; the first trigger is the $II$ at positions 3–4, so $N=4$.
