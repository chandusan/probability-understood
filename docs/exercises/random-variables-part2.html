Exercise 01 / Slide 7

Probabilities, density, and median from a CDF

A continuous random variable $X$ has CDF

$$F(x)=P(X\le x)=\begin{cases} 0,&x<-2,\\[2pt] \displaystyle\frac12+\frac3{32}\left(4x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right),&-2\le x<2,\\[6pt] 1,&x\ge2. \end{cases}$$

(a)

Compute $P(X<0)$.

(b)

Compute $P(-1<X<1)$.

(c)

Compute $P(0.5<X)$.

(d)

Find the probability density function $f(x)$.

(e)

Find the median of $X$.

Show solutionHide solution: Probabilities, density, and median from a CDF

Because $X$ is continuous, a single point has probability zero. Thus including or excluding any endpoint does not change the interval probabilities below. The CDF gives all probability accumulated up to its input.

(a) the probability to the left of zero

The CDF already measures probability to the left. Substitute zero into its middle branch:

$$P(X<0)=F(0) =\frac12+\frac3{32}\left(0-0\right) =\boxed{\frac12}.$$

We may use $F(0)=P(X\le0)$ for $P(X<0)$ because $P(X=0)=0$.

(b) the probability between minus one and one

Subtract the probability accumulated through $-1$ from that accumulated through $1$:

$$P(-1<X<1)=F(1)-F(-1).$$

Evaluate the two pieces explicitly:

$$F(1)=\frac12+\frac3{32}\left(4-\frac13\right) =\frac12+\frac{11}{32}=\frac{27}{32},$$

$$F(-1)=\frac12+\frac3{32}\left(-4+\frac13\right) =\frac12-\frac{11}{32}=\frac5{32}.$$

Therefore,

$$\boxed{P(-1<X<1)=\frac{27-5}{32}=\frac{11}{16}=0.6875.}$$

The negative sign inside $F(-1)$ matters: $(-1)^3=-1$, so subtracting $(-1)^3/3$ adds $1/3$.

(c) the probability above one half

The complement of $X>1/2$ is $X\le1/2$. Thus

$$P(X>1/2)=1-F(1/2).$$

First evaluate the CDF:

$$\begin{aligned} F(1/2) &=\frac12+\frac3{32}\left(2-\frac{1/8}{3}\right)\\ &=\frac12+\frac3{32}\left(\frac{47}{24}\right)\\ &=\frac{128}{256}+\frac{47}{256}=\frac{175}{256}. \end{aligned}$$

Hence,

$$\boxed{P(X>0.5)=\frac{81}{256}=0.31640625.}$$

This is smaller than $1/2$, as it should be: the event $X>0.5$ is a subset of $X>0$.

(d) introduce the whole expression, then differentiate its pieces

On $-2<x<2$, rewrite the complete middle branch as

$$F(x)=\frac12+\frac{3x}{8}-\frac{x^3}{32}.$$

Differentiate each term. The constant contributes zero; the linear term contributes $3/8$; the cubic term contributes $-3x^2/32$:

$$F'(x)=0+\frac38-\frac{3x^2}{32} =\frac3{32}(4-x^2).$$

Outside the support, the CDF is constant, so its derivative is zero. One convenient complete density is

$$\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac3{32}(4-x^2),&-2<x<2,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}}$$

The density approaches zero at both endpoints, matching the outside branches. It is nonnegative because $x^2\le4$ within this range. Its total area provides a check:

$$\int_{-2}^{2}\frac3{32}(4-x^2)\,dx =\frac3{32}\left[4x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-2}^{2} =\frac3{32}\left(\frac{16}{3}-\left(-\frac{16}{3}\right)\right)=1.$$

(e) find the point that splits probability in half

A median $m$ for this continuous distribution satisfies $F(m)=1/2$. Inside the support,

$$\frac12+\frac3{32}\left(4m-\frac{m^3}{3}\right)=\frac12.$$

Subtract $1/2$, divide by $3/32$, and factor:

$$4m-\frac{m^3}{3}=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\left(4-\frac{m^2}{3}\right)=0.$$

The algebraic candidates are $m=0$ and $m=\pm\sqrt{12}$. The latter two are outside $[-2,2]$, where this polynomial branch does not apply. They cannot be medians. Thus

$$\boxed{m=0.}$$

There is also a geometric reason: $f(-x)=f(x)$, so the density is symmetric around zero. Each side has half the total area. The density is positive throughout $(-2,2)$, so the CDF is strictly increasing there and this median is unique.
