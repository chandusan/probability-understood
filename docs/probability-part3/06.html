Probability after new information · Slide 06

Neither subscription, given at most one subscription

A marketing firm studies two streaming platforms. A randomly selected household subscribes to StreamFlix with probability $0.45$, to BingeMax with probability $0.60$, and to both with probability $0.25$. Given that the household subscribes to at most one platform, what is the probability it subscribes to neither? The answer is $4/15$, about $26.67\%$.

~3 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

Define the subscription events and the information

Let

$$F=\{\text{subscribes to StreamFlix}\},\qquad G=\{\text{subscribes to BingeMax}\}.$$

The given probabilities are

$$P(F)=0.45,\quad P(G)=0.60,\quad P(F\cap G)=0.25.$$

Define two further events:

$$N=F^c\cap G^c=\{\text{subscribes to neither}\},$$

$$T=(F\cap G)^c=\{\text{subscribes to at most one}\}.$$

“At most one” permits zero or one subscription. It excludes only the households with both. The question is $P(N\mid T)$, not the unconditional probability $P(N)$.

Fill the four disjoint regions

The $45\%$ StreamFlix total includes the $25\%$ who have both. Subtract that overlap to get StreamFlix only:

$$P(F\cap G^c)=0.45-0.25=0.20.$$

Similarly,

$$P(F^c\cap G)=0.60-0.25=0.35.$$

The union probability is

$$P(F\cup G)=0.45+0.60-0.25=0.80.$$

Consequently,

$$P(N)=1-0.80=0.20.$$

These calculations produce the complete table shown geometrically on the source slide:

Household's subscriptions Event Probability Included in condition $T$?
StreamFlix only $F\cap G^c$ $0.20$ Yes
BingeMax only $F^c\cap G$ $0.35$ Yes
Both $F\cap G$ $0.25$ No
Neither $F^c\cap G^c$ $0.20$ Yes

The four rows are disjoint and sum to one. This is a useful check that no household has been omitted or counted twice.

Identify the denominator before dividing

The condition retains three rows: StreamFlix only, BingeMax only, and neither. Their total probability is

$$P(T)=0.20+0.35+0.20=0.75.$$

Equivalently, $P(T)=1-P(F\cap G)=1-0.25$. Every household in $N$ is also in $T$, so $N\cap T=N$. Thus

$$ \begin{aligned} P(N\mid T) &=\frac{P(N\cap T)}{P(T)}\\ &=\frac{P(N)}{P(T)}\\ &=\frac{0.20}{0.75} =\boxed{\frac4{15}\approx0.266667}. \end{aligned} $$

Think of a hypothetical group of 100 households matching these proportions: 25 have both subscriptions and are ruled out by the information. Of the 75 remaining households, 20 have neither. Hence the relevant fraction is $20/75$.

Why the tempting alternative denominators fail

Dividing by $0.55=0.20+0.35$ would condition on exactly one subscription. That is a different event, and it contains no households with neither subscription. The correct probability of neither given exactly one is zero; the ratio $0.20/0.55$ is not that conditional probability because its numerator does not lie inside that conditioning event.

Dividing by $0.80$ would condition on at least one subscription, which also excludes neither. Leaving the denominator as 1 would ignore the information altogether and return $0.20$.

No independence assumption is needed. In fact, the supplied joint probability $0.25$ differs from $0.45\cdot0.60=0.27$, so the two subscription events are not independent under these numbers.

Check your understanding

Given at most one subscription, what is the probability of exactly one subscription? Explain why it complements the requested answer.

Reveal the reasoning

Exactly one has probability $0.20+0.35=0.55$. Therefore its conditional probability is $0.55/0.75=11/15$. Within the condition “at most one,” the only possibilities are zero and one subscriptions, so $4/15+11/15=1$.
