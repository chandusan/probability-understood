Total probability & Bayes’ rule · Slide 22

Win probability and who scored first

Use the slide's football percentages as the assumptions of a hypothetical probability model: the team scores first in 86% of games, wins 90% of games when scoring first, and wins 20% when the opponent scores first. We will calculate the overall win probability and then reverse the question: among wins, how often did the team score first?

~3 min reading/Source + intuition + practice
Let’s understand it

Translate the story into three supplied probabilities

Let

$$F=\{\text{the team scores first}\},\qquad W=\{\text{the team wins}\}.$$

Assume each game has a first scorer and no ties, as in the exercise's simplified setup. Thus $F^c$ means the opponent scores first, and $W^c$ means the team loses. The supplied numbers are

$$P(F)=0.86,\qquad P(W\mid F)=0.90, \qquad P(W\mid F^c)=0.20.$$

It follows that $P(F^c)=0.14$, $P(W^c\mid F)=0.10$, and $P(W^c\mid F^c)=0.80$.

The source refers to Spain and recent performance, but it supplies no dataset or observation period. Here the percentages are exercise inputs, not verified estimates of the team's current performance. The calculations below are exact for those stipulated inputs.

Part (a): a win can arrive through either first-scorer branch

The event W splits into two disjoint pieces:

$$W=(F\cap W)\cup(F^c\cap W).$$

The first piece has probability

$$P(F\cap W)=P(F)P(W\mid F)=(0.86)(0.90)=0.774.$$

The second has probability

$$P(F^c\cap W)=P(F^c)P(W\mid F^c)=(0.14)(0.20)=0.028.$$

Add them using total probability:

$$\boxed{P(W)=0.774+0.028=0.802=80.2\%.}$$

The overall rate is closer to 90% than to 20% because the 90% branch accounts for 86% of the games. Taking the simple average $(90\%+20\%)/2=55\%$ would assign the wrong branch weights.

Build the complete table

Scale the model to 1,000 games to make the reference groups visible:

First scorer Team wins Team loses Total
Team first $860(0.90)=774$ $860(0.10)=86$ 860
Opponent first $140(0.20)=28$ $140(0.80)=112$ 140
Total 802 198 1,000

This is a proportional illustration, not a claim that a real 1,000-game record has these exact counts. Dividing every entry by 1,000 gives the four joint probabilities and their totals.

Part (b): restrict the reference group to wins

We now know W occurred. The relevant group contains the 802 wins, not all 1,000 games and not the 860 games in which the team scored first.

Of those wins, 774 came from the team-first branch. Therefore

$$\begin{aligned} P(F\mid W) &=\frac{P(F\cap W)}{P(W)}\\ &=\frac{0.774}{0.802} =\frac{774}{802} =\boxed{\frac{387}{401}\approx0.9650873}. \end{aligned}$$

The answer is approximately 96.51%. The remaining $14/401\approx3.49\%$ of wins came after the opponent scored first.

Why the reversed conditional is different

The given $P(W\mid F)=90\%$ uses team-first games as its denominator: $774/860$. The requested $P(F\mid W)\approx96.51\%$ uses wins as its denominator: $774/802$.

The overlap is the same 774 games; the reference populations differ. Bayes' rule formalizes this change of denominator. Reversing the conditioning is not accomplished by copying the given 90% or taking its complement.

Check your understanding

If the team lost, what is the probability that the opponent scored first?

Condition on the other result column

There are 198 losses in the scaled table, and 112 followed an opponent-first goal. Thus $P(F^c\mid W^c)=112/198=56/99\approx56.57\%$. The denominator is the loss total, since losing is the information supplied.
