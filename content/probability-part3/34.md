# For the third draw, condition on what was removed

Knowing that the first two balls were red changes the composition of whichever urn was chosen. The next task is to calculate a third-red probability under each possible urn while retaining that evidence. These conditional probabilities will then be combined using the updated urn weights.

## Keep three different probabilities distinct

Let $E=R_1\cap R_2$. We already found

$$
P(A\mid E)=\frac{11}{12},\qquad P(B\mid E)=\frac1{12}.
$$

These tell us which urn is likely to be in use. They do not tell us the color of the next ball. For that, distinguish

- $P(R_3\mid A)$: the third-red probability if A is known, without information about the first two colors.
- $P(R_3\mid A,E)$: the third-red probability if A is known and the first two balls were red.
- $P(R_3\mid E)$: the third-red probability given two reds, while the urn remains unknown.

Each conditioning statement defines a different collection of possible experiments.

## Update the physical contents under each explanation

Under A, two of the 12 red balls have been removed, leaving 10 red and 8 black. There are 18 balls, so

$$
P(R_3\mid A,E)=\frac{10}{18}=\frac59.
$$

Under B, two of the four red balls have been removed, leaving 2 red and 16 black. Therefore

$$
P(R_3\mid B,E)=\frac2{18}=\frac19.
$$

The denominator 18 counts the balls physically available for the third draw. It is neither 20, the initial count, nor 19, the count before the second draw.

The urn was selected once, so we do not take balls from both urns. The two rows describe alternative explanations of the same observed experiment:

| Explanation | Original contents | Contents after E | Weight after E |
|---|---|---|---:|
| A was selected | 12 red, 8 black | 10 red, 8 black | $11/12$ |
| B was selected | 4 red, 16 black | 2 red, 16 black | $1/12$ |

## Why the unconditional third-draw fraction differs

Without observing the first two colors, $P(R_3\mid A)=12/20$. To justify that by counting, consider the $20\cdot19\cdot18$ ordered triples of distinct labeled balls. There are $12\cdot19\cdot18$ triples with a red third ball: choose that red ball first, then choose the first two labels from the other 19 balls. The ratio is $12/20$.

Once E is specified, the earlier two draws are constrained to be red. Only 10 red balls remain for the third position. There is no contradiction: conditioning has changed the allowed set of triples.

## Learning and depletion pull in different directions

Before any colors are observed, the next-red probability is

$$
\frac12\frac{12}{20}+\frac12\frac4{20}=\frac25.
$$

Observing two reds makes A much more plausible, pushing the prediction upward. Removing those red balls reduces the red fraction within either possible urn, pushing downward relative to keeping the balls available. Combining the two effects gives

$$
P(R_3\mid E)
=\frac{11}{12}\frac{10}{18}+\frac1{12}\frac2{18}
=\frac{14}{27}>\frac25.
$$

Thus red draws are not automatically “less likely to be followed by red” when the urn itself is unknown. Depletion reduces each known-urn red fraction, while learning shifts the probability toward the red-rich urn.
