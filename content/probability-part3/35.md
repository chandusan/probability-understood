# Predict the next red by averaging within the updated evidence

The third-draw prediction is a conditional total-probability calculation. Inside the experiments where the first two balls were red, urn A has weight 11/12 and urn B has weight 1/12. Combine those weights with the red fractions in the depleted urns.

## Partition the evidence into the two possible urns

Write $E=R_1\cap R_2$. Given E, the cases “A was chosen” and “B was chosen” remain disjoint and exhaustive. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R_3\mid E)
={}&P(R_3\mid A,E)P(A\mid E)\\
&+P(R_3\mid B,E)P(B\mid E).
\end{aligned}
$$

The conditioning on E appears in **both factors of each product**. The first factor describes a next draw from the remaining balls. The second factor describes which urn is likely after observing the earlier draws.

Substituting the values gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R_3\mid E)
&=\frac{10}{18}\frac{11}{12}+\frac2{18}\frac1{12}\\
&=\frac{110}{216}+\frac2{216}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{14}{27}\approx0.518519}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This lies between $1/9$ and $5/9$, the two known-urn probabilities. It is much closer to $5/9$ because A receives eleven times the weight of B.

## Verify with one joint-probability ratio

Conditional probability offers a second route:

$$
P(R_3\mid R_1,R_2)
=\frac{P(R_1\cap R_2\cap R_3)}{P(R_1\cap R_2)}.
$$

Calculate the numerator by splitting over the original urn choice:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R_1\cap R_2\cap R_3)
&=\frac12\frac{12}{20}\frac{11}{19}\frac{10}{18}
+\frac12\frac4{20}\frac3{19}\frac2{18}\\
&=\frac{1320+24}{13680}=\frac{28}{285}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $P(E)=18/95$, the ratio is

$$
\frac{28/285}{18/95}=\frac{28}{54}=\frac{14}{27}.
$$

The two methods organize the same joint probabilities differently. The posterior-weight method separates learning from prediction; the joint-ratio method provides a direct arithmetic check.

## Make the second calculation a literal count

With labeled balls there are $2\cdot20\cdot19\cdot18=13680$ equally likely outcomes consisting of an urn and three ordered draws. Among these, the evidence E permits

$$
(12\cdot11+4\cdot3)\cdot18=2592
$$

outcomes: choose the urn and its first two red balls, then allow any remaining third ball. The favorable subset also requires the third ball to be red, giving

$$
12\cdot11\cdot10+4\cdot3\cdot2=1344.
$$

Thus $1344/2592=14/27$. The denominator is the evidence-compatible sample space, not all 13680 original outcomes.

## Diagnose two tempting wrong answers

| Calculation | Result | Missing update |
|---|---:|---|
| $(1/2)(10/18)+(1/2)(2/18)$ | $1/3$ | Urn probabilities were not updated |
| $(11/12)(12/20)+(1/12)(4/20)$ | $17/30$ | Removed balls were still treated as available |
| $(11/12)(10/18)+(1/12)(2/18)$ | $14/27$ | Both updates are included |

## Check your understanding

Suppose the third ball also turns out red. What is the new probability that the urn is A?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Use the third red as another piece of evidence</summary>

Within the two-red evidence, update the urn weights again:

$$
P(A\mid E,R_3)
=\frac{(11/12)(10/18)}{(11/12)(10/18)+(1/12)(2/18)}
=\frac{110}{112}=\frac{55}{56}.
$$

Equivalently, among the 1344 red-red-red outcomes, 1320 come from A. Both routes give $1320/1344=55/56$.
