# Finish Bayes' rule by counting every route to the evidence

The denominator of Bayes' rule is the total probability of the observed event. When the sample space is partitioned into two groups, evidence can come from either group. Adding those two joint contributions supplies the normalization that turns each contribution into a posterior probability.

## Partition the observed event

Suppose $A_1$ and $A_2$ are disjoint and exhaustive. Then

$$B=(B\cap A_1)\cup(B\cap A_2),$$

with disjoint pieces. Consequently,

$$\begin{aligned}
P(B)
&=P(B\cap A_1)+P(B\cap A_2)\\
&=P(B\mid A_1)P(A_1)+P(B\mid A_2)P(A_2).
\end{aligned}$$

Each conditional probability has to be multiplied by its group's share. Conditional probabilities alone describe fractions of different groups and cannot be added as if they already described fractions of the whole population.

## Substitute into the compact Bayes formula

The preceding slide established

$$P(A_1\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A_1)P(A_1)}{P(B)}.$$

Replacing the denominator gives

$$\boxed{
P(A_1\mid B)=
\frac{P(B\mid A_1)P(A_1)}
{P(B\mid A_1)P(A_1)+P(B\mid A_2)P(A_2)}.}$$

The numerator is one contribution to the denominator. This immediately explains why the result lies between zero and one, provided the denominator is positive.

## Use a table to make normalization visible

Return to the two boxes: Box 1 is selected 30% of the time and has red probability 80%; Box 2 is selected 70% of the time and has red probability 20%. Let B mean red.

| Possible source | Prior | Red likelihood | Joint red contribution | Posterior after red |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Box 1 | $0.30$ | $0.80$ | $0.24$ | $0.24/0.38=12/19$ |
| Box 2 | $0.70$ | $0.20$ | $0.14$ | $0.14/0.38=7/19$ |
| Total | $1$ | — | $0.38$ | $1$ |

The joint contributions sum to 0.38 because red occurs on only 38% of all trials. Dividing each contribution by 0.38 makes the posterior column sum to one because it now describes **only red trials**.

In the scaled 1,000-trial picture, the same normalization is $240/(240+140)$ and $140/(240+140)$.

## Diagnose three possible denominator mistakes

Using 0.30 as the denominator would calculate $P(B\mid A_1)=0.24/0.30$, which reverses the requested direction. Using only 0.24 would ignore red results from Box 2 and incorrectly produce certainty about Box 1.

Using $0.80+0.20$ would add two conditional rates without accounting for the unequal box-selection probabilities. Those rates happen to sum to one here, but their sum is not the probability of red. In another example their sum could even exceed one.

The dependable question is: **Which complete paths produce the evidence?** Their joint probabilities, and only those probabilities, belong in the denominator.

## Check the assumptions at the boundary

Bayes' ratio requires $P(B)>0$. If neither group can produce B, the denominator is zero and the proposed observation is impossible under the model. If one group has prior probability zero, omit that group when using conditional rates defined by elementary ratios.

The events $A_1,A_2$ need not mean physical causes. They can be categories, possible box choices, or competing hypotheses. The theorem concerns event probabilities; a causal claim would need additional assumptions.
