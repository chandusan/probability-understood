# Four coin tosses: every pair passes, but the triple fails

A fair coin is tossed four times independently. Let $A$ mean the first two tosses agree, $B$ mean there is an odd number of heads, and $C$ mean the last two tosses agree. Are these three events mutually independent? **No.** Every pair is independent, but all three events cannot occur together.

## State the complete model and the events

Each toss is heads or tails with probability $1/2$, independently of the other tosses. An outcome records the four results in order, so there are $2^4=16$ equally likely outcomes, each of probability $(1/2)^4=1/16$.

The events are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\{\text{tosses 1 and 2 are HH or TT}\},\\
B&=\{\text{the total number of heads is 1 or 3}\},\\
C&=\{\text{tosses 3 and 4 are HH or TT}\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For example, $HHTT$ belongs to $A$ and $C$, but not $B$, since it contains two heads. $HHHT$ belongs to $A$ and $B$, but not $C$.

## List all 16 outcomes explicitly

“Yes” means the outcome belongs to that event. Every row carries the same weight, $1/16$.

| Outcome | Number of heads | $A$ | $B$ | $C$ |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| HHHH | 4 | Yes | No | Yes |
| HHHT | 3 | Yes | Yes | No |
| HHTH | 3 | Yes | Yes | No |
| HHTT | 2 | Yes | No | Yes |
| HTHH | 3 | No | Yes | Yes |
| HTHT | 2 | No | No | No |
| HTTH | 2 | No | No | No |
| HTTT | 1 | No | Yes | Yes |
| THHH | 3 | No | Yes | Yes |
| THHT | 2 | No | No | No |
| THTH | 2 | No | No | No |
| THTT | 1 | No | Yes | Yes |
| TTHH | 2 | Yes | No | Yes |
| TTHT | 1 | Yes | Yes | No |
| TTTH | 1 | Yes | Yes | No |
| TTTT | 0 | Yes | No | Yes |

Fairness of each toss and independence together justify the equal weights. Four individually fair but linked tosses would not necessarily produce this table's uniform probabilities.

## Find the three individual probabilities

For $A$, choose the first pair as $HH$ or $TT$ in two ways; the last two tosses have four possibilities. Thus $|A|=2\cdot4=8$. The same reasoning gives $|C|=8$.

For $B$, exactly one head has $\binom41=4$ possible positions, and exactly three heads has $\binom43=4$. These cases are disjoint, giving $|B|=8$.

Therefore

$$P(A)=P(B)=P(C)=\frac8{16}=\frac12.$$

## Check every pair, not just one

The intersections can be read directly from the table:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A\cap B&=\{HHHT,HHTH,TTHT,TTTH\},\\
A\cap C&=\{HHHH,HHTT,TTHH,TTTT\},\\
B\cap C&=\{HTHH,HTTT,THHH,THTT\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Each contains four outcomes, so every pair has probability $4/16=1/4$. Compare each with its product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A\cap B)&=\frac14=P(A)P(B),\\
P(A\cap C)&=\frac14=P(A)P(C),\\
P(B\cap C)&=\frac14=P(B)P(C).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the events are **pairwise independent**. In particular, knowing $A$ alone leaves $P(B\mid A)=(1/4)/(1/2)=1/2$ unchanged.

## Now check the triple intersection

If $A$ occurs, the first two tosses contribute either zero or two heads. If $C$ also occurs, the last two contribute either zero or two heads. The total must then be 0, 2, or 4—always even.

Consequently, $B$ cannot occur together with both $A$ and $C$:

$$A\cap B\cap C=\varnothing,\qquad P(A\cap B\cap C)=0.$$

Mutual independence would require

$$P(A\cap B\cap C)=P(A)P(B)P(C)=\left(\frac12\right)^3=\frac18.$$

Since $0\ne1/8$, the three events are **not mutually independent**. The missing condition is visible both in the table—no row has three “Yes” entries—and in the parity argument.

## Why two pieces of information can reveal more than either alone

Knowing $A$ alone leaves the last pair free, so an odd total is still possible with probability $1/2$. Knowing $C$ alone similarly leaves the first pair free. But knowing **both** fixes the parity of the total:

$$P(B\mid A\cap C)=\frac{0}{1/4}=0.$$

Pairwise independence promises that one event alone does not change another's probability. It does not promise that a combination of two events carries no additional information.
