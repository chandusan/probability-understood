# An even result changes the chance of rolling a 2

A fair six-sided die is rolled and covered. You are truthfully told that the result is even. What is the probability that the hidden result is 2? Before receiving the information, the answer was $1/6$. After restricting attention to the possible even results, the answer becomes $1/3$.

## Define the original experiment and the two events

There is one roll, with sample space

$$S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}.$$

Fairness means each outcome has probability $1/6$. Define

$$
A=\{2\},\qquad B=\{2,4,6\}.
$$

Here $A$ is the event we want to assess, and $B$ is the information we receive. We want $P(A\mid B)$, read “the probability of $A$, given $B$.”

Receiving the information “even” eliminates outcomes 1, 3, and 5. It does not distinguish 2 from 4 or 6, so the three surviving outcomes retain equal weights.

## Look at the probabilities before and after the information

| Die result | Original probability | Compatible with “even”? | Probability given “even” |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | $1/6$ | No | $0$ |
| 2 | $1/6$ | Yes | $1/3$ |
| 3 | $1/6$ | No | $0$ |
| 4 | $1/6$ | Yes | $1/3$ |
| 5 | $1/6$ | No | $0$ |
| 6 | $1/6$ | Yes | $1/3$ |

The final column must add to one, since some result compatible with the information occurred. Keeping probability $1/6$ on each even number would make the column total only $1/2$. We must rescale the remaining probabilities.

There is one favorable outcome among three equally likely remaining outcomes:

$$\boxed{P(A\mid B)=\frac13.}$$

## Obtain the same answer from the conditional formula

The information event has probability

$$P(B)=\frac36=\frac12.$$

The event “2 and even” is just $A\cap B=\{2\}$, with probability $1/6$. Therefore

$$
P(A\mid B)
=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
=\frac{1/6}{3/6}
=\frac13.
$$

The common denominator 6 cancels. This explains why favorable-count divided by remaining-count works here. It works because the original outcomes are equally likely; a loaded die would require their actual probability weights.

## The information changes our assessment of the same roll

No physical transformation has made the die more likely to produce 2. The roll is already complete. We have changed the collection of outcomes relevant to our uncertainty.

The condition can also make an event impossible. For example,

$$P(\{1\}\mid B)=0.$$

Or it can make an event certain:

$$P(B\mid B)=1.$$

Conditional probability need not concern a future event. It describes what we can say about an outcome when some relevant information is known.

The calculation conditions directly on the fact that the result is even. If a puzzle instead makes the speaker's choice of message depend on the hidden result, that reporting mechanism would be additional information to model.

## Check your understanding

With the same fair die, let $C$ mean “the result is greater than 3.” What is $P(C\mid B)$? What is $P(A\mid C)$?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the reasoning</summary>

Among the even outcomes $\{2,4,6\}$, two are greater than 3. Thus $P(C\mid B)=2/3$. Among the outcomes $\{4,5,6\}$ compatible with $C$, none equals 2, so $P(A\mid C)=0$. In each calculation, the event after the bar determines the reference group.

</details>
