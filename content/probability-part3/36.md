# Update a fraud flag with the transaction's base rate and SMS result

An alert is evidence about fraud, not proof of fraud. Most transactions in this exercise are legitimate, so false alarms can outnumber true alerts. A second piece of evidence, an unconfirmed SMS, changes the calculation again when its accuracy is interpreted conditional on the transaction's true status.

## Translate the complete problem into events

Let L mean legitimate, F fraudulent, S flagged suspicious, and U that the requested SMS confirmation is unsuccessful. The source specifies

$$
P(L)=0.95,\qquad P(F)=0.05,
$$

and

$$
P(S\mid L)=0.08,\qquad P(S\mid F)=0.85.
$$

The 8% false-positive rate is the complement of the 92% legitimate-safe rate. The 85% detection rate is conditional on fraud. Neither rate directly answers “given an alert, what is the transaction's status?”

For the verification step, legitimate transactions go unconfirmed 10% of the time, while fraudulent transactions go unconfirmed 95% of the time. The stated conditional-independence assumption lets those status-specific response rates apply within the flagged group:

$$
P(U\mid S,L)=0.10,\qquad P(U\mid S,F)=0.95.
$$

We need (a) $P(L\mid S)$ and (b) $P(F\mid S,U)$.

## Part (a): retain every route to a suspicious flag

There are two disjoint routes: a legitimate transaction is falsely flagged, or a fraudulent transaction is correctly flagged. Their joint probabilities are

$$
P(L\cap S)=0.95\cdot0.08=0.076,
$$

$$
P(F\cap S)=0.05\cdot0.85=0.0425.
$$

Consequently,

$$
P(S)=0.076+0.0425=0.1185.
$$

Restrict to flagged transactions and keep the legitimate ones in the numerator:

$$
\boxed{P(L\mid S)=\frac{0.076}{0.1185}
=\frac{152}{237}\approx0.641350.}
$$

About $64.14\%$ of flagged transactions are legitimate under this model. The corresponding fraud probability is $85/237\approx35.86\%$. The substantial legitimate base rate explains why an 85% detection rate does not imply an 85% chance of fraud after a flag.

## Part (b): require both the flag and the unconfirmed SMS

Use the multiplication rule along each complete branch:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(F\cap S\cap U)
&=P(F)P(S\mid F)P(U\mid S,F)\\
&=0.05\cdot0.85\cdot0.95=0.040375,
\end{aligned}
$$

while

$$
P(L\cap S\cap U)=0.95\cdot0.08\cdot0.10=0.0076.
$$

The denominator must include both explanations for the combined evidence:

$$
P(S\cap U)=0.040375+0.0076=0.047975.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(F\mid S,U)
=\frac{0.040375}{0.047975}
=\frac{1615}{1919}\approx0.841584.}
$$

The unconfirmed response raises the fraud probability from about $35.86\%$ to $84.16\%$. It remains below one because a legitimate cardholder can also fail to confirm.

## Check the fractions with a concrete population table

Imagine a model population of 40,000 transactions whose category proportions match the stated probabilities exactly. The successive filters produce:

| True status | All transactions | Flagged suspicious | Flagged and unconfirmed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Legitimate | 38,000 | 3,040 | 304 |
| Fraudulent | 2,000 | 1,700 | 1,615 |
| Total | 40,000 | 4,740 | 1,919 |

Part (a) uses $3040/4740=152/237$. Part (b) uses $1615/1919$. Each denominator counts the group matching the question's observed evidence, and each numerator counts the requested status within that group. These are illustrative model counts, not a claim about measured bank data.

## Update sequentially to verify part (b)

After the alert, the fraud and legitimate weights are $85/237$ and $152/237$. Apply the SMS evidence to those updated weights:

$$
P(F\mid S,U)
=\frac{(85/237)(0.95)}{(85/237)(0.95)+(152/237)(0.10)}
=\frac{1615}{1919}.
$$

Unconfirmed messages are $0.95/0.10=9.5$ times as likely for fraudulent transactions as for legitimate ones, explaining the direction of this second update.

## What the independence assumption actually says

The model assumes the SMS response behavior is independent of the flagging mechanism **given the true status**. It does not say S and U are independent across all transactions. Fraud status affects both, creating a shared source of association. Because verification is requested after a flag, the calculation above consistently uses the response probability conditional on being flagged and on status.
