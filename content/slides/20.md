# Diamonds: measure the middle and the endpoints

The same collection of prices has an overall span and a central price band. Range measures the first; IQR measures the width of the second. The supplied summaries let us calculate both without reconstructing every price.

## Read the information we actually have

The sample consists of **42 low-quality diamonds, each less than one carat**. Its complete summary table is:

| Statistic | Supplied value |
|---|---:|
| Number of diamonds $n$ | 42 |
| Minimum | \$416 |
| First quartile $Q_1$ | \$1,416 |
| Median | \$1,882 |
| Mean | \$1,924 |
| Third quartile $Q_3$ | \$2,497 |
| Maximum | \$3,154 |
| Sample standard deviation $s$ | \$703 |

The histogram above places the prices in the lower few thousand dollars. Its largest bar is around \$1,500–\$2,000, with another relatively tall bar around \$2,500–\$3,000. Its horizontal scale runs from 0 to about \$3,500.

## Solve before opening the explanation

Which two entries should you subtract for IQR? Which two should you subtract for range?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Work through both calculations</summary>

For IQR, use the two quartiles:

$$
\operatorname{IQR}=2497-1416=1081\text{ dollars}.
$$

For range, use the actual endpoints:

$$
R=3154-416=2738\text{ dollars}.
$$

The middle interval runs from \$1,416 to \$2,497 and is \$1,081 wide. The full observed sample runs from \$416 to \$3,154 and is \$2,738 wide.

</details>

## Give the answers their meaning

The difference between 1,081 and 2,738 is not evidence that one calculation is more accurate. They summarize different parts of the distribution.

IQR describes the width between the lower- and upper-quarter landmarks. Range reaches all the way to the cheapest and most expensive observed diamonds. The standard deviation, \$703, will answer yet another question by using every observation's squared departure from the mean.

The mean, \$1,924, and median, \$1,882, are fairly close. That is useful context, but closeness alone does not prove symmetry or normality. The histogram provides additional information about shape that a short summary table cannot retain.

## What we cannot reconstruct

The source gives these summaries, not the full list of 42 individual prices. We cannot recover the raw data, the exact quartile algorithm, or every exact histogram-bin count from the table alone. Its rounded centers and standard deviation should be used as supplied, not treated as enough information to infer exact underlying totals.

## Check your understanding

Suppose the highest observed price rose by \$1,000 while the minimum and both quartiles stayed fixed. By how much would range and IQR change?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the reasoning</summary>

Range would increase by \$1,000 because it subtracts the unchanged minimum from the larger maximum. IQR would not change because $Q_3-Q_1$ stayed fixed. A tail change need not be a middle-rank change.

</details>
