# Choose the kind of spread you need

“Spread” sounds like one question, but it hides several. Do we care about the two extremes, the ordinary middle experience, or every observation's distance from the center? Different dispersion measures preserve different information.

## A center is only half a description

A mean or median compresses a group to one location. It does not tell us whether observations agree closely with that location or vary dramatically around it.

This matters whenever we make decisions under uncertainty. A delivery service averaging two days might reliably deliver in two days, or alternate between same-day delivery and long delays. Those services have different practical value even if their averages match.

Understanding variability also matters when evaluating a model and when using sample observations to infer something about a population. A numerical center without context about variation can create a misleading impression of certainty.

## Four measures, four questions

| Measure | The question it answers | Information it emphasizes |
|---|---|---|
| **Range** | How far apart are the two observed endpoints? | The smallest and largest observations |
| **IQR** | How wide is the central half? | The 25th- and 75th-percentile landmarks |
| **Variance** | How large are the squared departures from the mean? | Every observation, with large departures strongly weighted |
| **Standard deviation** | What is that squared-deviation scale in the original units? | The square root of variance |

These measures can disagree without any calculation being wrong. Move a single observation far into the upper tail: the range will grow immediately, variance will respond strongly, and IQR may barely change.

## Match the summary to the decision

If you need the full **observed** span, report the range. If you want the middle experience while limiting the influence of a few extremes, the median and IQR are useful companions. If your analysis is built around squared errors, the mean and variance fit that purpose naturally.

The choice depends on mathematical properties, sensitivity to outliers, and what information you can afford to lose. Reporting several complementary summaries is often better than declaring one universally best.

## Keep the vocabulary precise

**Variability** and **dispersion** name the general idea. **Variance** is also the name of a specific squared-deviation measure. Range and IQR describe variability, but they are not alternative formulas for variance.

No independence assumption is needed merely to summarize the numbers you have. Assumptions about sampling become important when you ask how well those summaries estimate a population or how uncertain the estimates are.

## Check your understanding

You care about the ordinary price band in a market, but a handful of luxury products have exceptionally high prices. Would the range or IQR better describe that ordinary middle band?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the reasoning</summary>

IQR directly describes the width of the central half and is less sensitive to a few extreme prices. Range still answers a useful, different question: the distance between the cheapest and most expensive observed products. Neither measure alone describes the entire distribution.
