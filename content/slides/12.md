# Follow the tail of the histogram

Name the direction of skew by the tail that stretches away from the main group. In these three histograms, that tail helps predict where the mean sits relative to the median: roughly together, pulled right, or pulled left.

## First learn to identify the tail

A tail is the portion that stretches away from the main concentration of observations. The direction of skew is named after the **tail**, not after the side containing the highest bars.

A right-skewed histogram can have most observations toward the left. It is called right-skewed because the thinner, extended tail runs to the right.

## 1. Shoe sizes

The pictured plot is roughly symmetric around its central peak. Observations on the low and high sides broadly balance.

Expected result:

$$
\text{mean}\approx\text{median}.
$$

In the ruler picture, neither side has a much longer tail pulling the balance point away from the middle.

## 2. Years of car ownership

Most observations occupy the smaller values, and the histogram extends in a long tail toward larger numbers of years.

Expected result:

$$
\text{mean}>\text{median}.
$$

A few large values add substantially to the total and pull the equal-share value to the right. The ordered midpoint remains closer to the main body of observations.

## 3. Winter daily low temperatures

Most observations are near the comparatively warmer values, around $-5^\circ\mathrm{C}$, while a tail stretches left toward colder, more negative temperatures.

Expected result:

$$
\text{mean}<\text{median}.
$$

Very cold days pull the mean leftward. The negative signs can be distracting: “left” still means a numerically smaller value. For instance, $-12$ is smaller than $-5$.

## Exercise answers together

| Source histogram | Shape | Expected relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Shoe sizes | Roughly symmetric | Mean $\approx$ median |
| Years of car ownership | Right-skewed | Mean $>$ median |
| Winter daily low temperatures | Left-skewed | Mean $<$ median |

The reasoning is the same in all three: the mean responds to distances and totals, while the median responds to ordered position.

## Important distinctions

- “The mean follows the skew” is a useful heuristic for these familiar shapes, not a universal theorem that orders mean and median for every conceivable distribution.
- A histogram generally supports qualitative judgments here. Exact means and medians require the observations, or additional information about bin contents and an explicitly stated approximation.
- Even a symmetric-looking finite histogram need not have exactly equal sample mean and median. Approximate symmetry supports an approximate comparison.
- The next slides show why knowing the center is not enough: we also need to know how far observations spread around it.
