# A fractional rank is a position, not a value

A percentile asks for a location in ordered data. When that location falls between two observations, interpolation estimates a value between them. But first we must choose the rule that turns a percentile into a rank.

## Separate the two steps

The slide sorts the observations and proposes this rank for the $p$-th percentile:

$$
h=\frac{p}{100}n.
$$

If the rank is an integer, it uses the observation there. For a noninteger interior rank, it interpolates between the neighboring observations.

Those are two distinct choices:

1. **Rank rule:** where in the sorted list should we look?
2. **Interpolation rule:** what value should we assign between two ranks?

Confusing these steps is the main source of apparently contradictory percentile answers.

## Our example: why rank 2.25 gives 30

Sort the five values

$$
10,20,60,80,100.
$$

Rank 2 contains 20; rank 3 contains 60. Rank 2.25 means one quarter of the way from the second position to the third. Therefore

$$
20+0.25(60-20)=30.
$$

The fractional part, 0.25, tells us how much of the **gap** to travel. It does not mean 25% of the value 60, nor that the percentile itself is 2.25.

| Fractional rank | Position between ranks 2 and 3 | Value |
|---:|---|---:|
| 2.00 | At the second observation | 20 |
| 2.25 | One quarter across the gap | 30 |
| 2.50 | Halfway | 40 |
| 2.75 | Three quarters across | 50 |
| 3.00 | At the third observation | 60 |

In general, if $h=k+g$ with $0<g<1$, linear interpolation gives

$$
x_{(k)}+g\bigl(x_{(k+1)}-x_{(k)}\bigr).
$$

For an integer rank, use that observation directly.

## The important correction: NumPy uses another default rank

Let $u=p/100$. The slide's stated one-based rank is $h=nu$. NumPy's default linear method uses zero-based position $(n-1)u$, or equivalently the one-based rank

$$
h=1+(n-1)u=1+(n-1)\frac{p}{100}.
$$

For the **45th percentile** of our five values:

| Convention | Rank | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Slide's $h=nu$ | $5(0.45)=2.25$ | $20+0.25(40)=30$ |
| Default linear $h=1+(n-1)u$ | $1+4(0.45)=2.8$ | $20+0.8(40)=52$ |

The interpolation arithmetic is the same; the starting rank differs. NumPy explicitly documents `method="linear"` as its default. pandas also offers interpolation choices. See the [NumPy quantile documentation](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.quantile.html) and [pandas quantile documentation](https://pandas.pydata.org/docs/reference/api/pandas.Series.quantile.html).

Notice that interpolation follows the spacing of the values. Moving a quarter of one rank can change the answer a lot if the neighboring observations are far apart.

## Why “exactly p percent below” needs care

With five distinct observations, the empirical cumulative proportions jump in steps of 20%. There need not be any threshold with exactly 45% of observations at or below it. An interpolated percentile estimates a location; it does not create an observed value there.

The slide's $h=nu$ is not inherently an invalid convention, but it is not NumPy's default and needs endpoint rules. At $p=0$ it produces rank zero even though one-based observations begin at one. Different methods can also disagree with the usual sample median on small datasets.

For an exercise, state the chosen convention. For software, state the selected method. Keep its rank, interpolation, and endpoint rules together.

## Check your understanding

Using default linear interpolation, what is the 25th percentile of $10,20,60,80,100$? Why is it not obtained by interpolating at rank 1.25?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the reasoning</summary>

The one-based default rank is $1+(5-1)(0.25)=2$, so the answer is 20. Rank $5(0.25)=1.25$ belongs to the slide's different rank rule; it would produce $10+0.25(20-10)=12.5$. The difference is the rank convention, not the arithmetic.
