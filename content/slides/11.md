# Choose the center your question needs

Choose a center by deciding what you want it to represent. A middle person, an equal share of a total, and the best constant prediction are related ideas, but they need not lead to the same numerical answer.

## Read the two markers in the picture

The pictured right-skewed distribution labels the median as **1.05** and the mean as **1.53**. Most observations cluster toward the lower values, but a long tail extends to the right. The solid green median marker stays closer to the main body; the dashed red mean marker sits farther into the tail.

The diagram makes the balancing-ruler picture visible. A small number of large observations put weight far out on the right. Those observations pull the mean farther right than the median.

The median asks where the middle observation lies. It does not need to move far merely because a few already-large observations become much larger.

## Turn “typical” into a specific question

“Typical” is not a complete decision rule until we name what the number should tell us.

| Purpose | Useful choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Describe the middle person's experience | Median | Defined by ordered position |
| Report an equal share of a total | Mean | $n\bar{x}$ recovers the total |
| Choose a constant minimizing squared prediction error | Mean | Least-squares property |
| Choose a constant minimizing absolute prediction error | Median(s) | Absolute-loss property |
| Reduce the influence of a small fraction of extreme replacements | Median | High replacement-breakdown resistance |

For example, a median wait describes the middle customer. The mean wait is directly connected to the total waiting time accumulated across customers. Both can be useful; they answer different operational questions.

Reporting both is often informative because their separation can reveal an asymmetric distribution. It is also important to report spread: two datasets can share a center while giving people very different experiences.

**Memory image:** the mean is the balance point; the median is the middle person.

## Important distinctions

- Neither summary is universally best. The loss, scientific question, and intended interpretation determine the choice.
- “Sensitive” is not automatically a defect. If an unusually large value represents a genuine large cost that must be included in a total, sensitivity to that value may be desirable.
- The pictured histogram supplies the values $1.05$ and $1.53$ as illustrative summaries. Its individual observations are not available here, so we cannot claim to reconstruct the histogram's raw data or recompute those figures.

## Check your understanding

Three annual salaries are 30,000, 30,000, and 300,000. Which summary describes the middle employee, and which can recover the total payroll?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Work through the answer</summary>

The median is $30{,}000$, matching the middle employee. The mean is $120{,}000$ because the total is $360{,}000$. Multiplying that mean by three recovers the payroll. A large difference between mean and median reveals something about the salary distribution; it is not a reason to declare one calculation invalid.
