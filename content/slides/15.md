# Range: observed endpoints and possible values

A sample range tells you how far your observations reached. A population's support tells you where observations could occur. The farthest places you visited are not necessarily the edges of the whole map.

## Sort first, then subtract the ends

For observations $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, write their sorted values as

$$
x_{(1)}\le x_{(2)}\le\cdots\le x_{(n)}.
$$

The parentheses denote **order statistics**: $x_{(1)}$ is the smallest observation, not necessarily the first one collected. The sample range is

$$
R(X)=x_{(n)}-x_{(1)}.
$$

It uses only two values. That makes it quick to understand, but also means it cannot tell us how the observations are arranged between the endpoints.

## Our example: a sample does not fill its support

Suppose a population has support $[2,10]$, while the sample we happened to observe is

$$
3,5,7.
$$

Then

$$
R_{\text{sample}}=7-3=4,
$$

while the population's endpoint span is

$$
R_{\text{population}}=10-2=8.
$$

Both answers are correct. The sample minimum is 3 because that is the smallest value we saw. It does not establish that values below 3 are impossible. Similarly, observing a maximum of 7 does not shrink the population's support to 7.

Think of three people standing inside a room. The distance between the two outermost people need not equal the full width of the room.

## Three properties you can see without memorizing

**Nonnegativity:** the maximum cannot be smaller than the minimum, so

$$
R(X)\ge0.
$$

For a nonempty finite sample, the range is zero exactly when all observations coincide.

**Translation invariance:** adding a constant moves both endpoints equally:

$$
R(X+c)=R(X).
$$

For example, $3,5,7$ becomes $13,15,17$ after adding 10. The range stays 4.

**Scale equivariance:** multiplying by $c$ multiplies every distance by $|c|$:

$$
R(cX)=|c|R(X).
$$

Multiplying $3,5,7$ by $-2$ gives $-6,-10,-14$, which sorts as $-14,-10,-6$. The range is $-6-(-14)=8$, not $-8$. Reflection reverses the order; it does not make distances negative.

The combined rule is

$$
R(aX+b)=|a|R(X).
$$

Range has the same units as the observations: minutes, dollars, or a temperature difference in degrees Celsius.

## Support needs a little care

The slide describes a continuous distribution supported on an interval $[a,b]$, with density $f(x)>0$ in its interior. In that simple bounded setting, its endpoint span is $b-a$.

More generally, support need not be one interval. It can contain separated regions, discrete values, or extend without bound. A normal distribution has support on the whole real line, although every finite sample of finite observations has a finite sample range.

A general endpoint span uses

$$
\sup(\operatorname{support})-\inf(\operatorname{support}),
$$

when finite. An endpoint can belong to the support even if the density equals zero at that exact point. Support concerns values with probability in arbitrarily small surrounding neighborhoods, rather than only the density's height at one point.

The slide begins with independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations. That assumption is relevant to statistical inference; it is not required to calculate a descriptive maximum minus minimum.

## Check your understanding

A population has support $[0,20]$. Your observations are $4,6,9$. What are the sample range and population endpoint span? What happens to the sample range if every observation is replaced by $-3x+5$?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the calculation</summary>

The sample range is $9-4=5$. The population endpoint span is $20-0=20$. Under $-3x+5$, translation changes no distance and multiplication by $-3$ triples distances, so the sample range becomes $3(5)=15$.
