# Read the three-dimensional comparison

The two panels encode entire three-number datasets as points. Both datasets choose the same constant point under absolute error, but they choose different points under squared error. Read each target as three copies of one proposed center.

## One colored point represents a whole dataset

Each three-number dataset is packaged into **one** point:

$$
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{old}}=(6,3,9),
\qquad
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{new}}=(6,3,12).
$$

The candidate constant line is

$$
\mathbf{q}(c)=(c,c,c).
$$

Moving the last observation from $9$ to $12$ changes only the third coordinate of the data point. The comparison asks where each point is best represented on the same constant line under each loss.

## Median / absolute-error panel

Both datasets have median $6$, so both choose

$$
\mathbf{q}(6)=(6,6,6).
$$

Their minimum absolute errors differ:

$$
\|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{old}}-(6,6,6)\|_1
=0+3+3=6,
$$

$$
\|\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{new}}-(6,6,6)\|_1
=0+3+6=9.
$$

The optimal center remains the same, but the total cost increases. **An unchanged median does not mean unchanged variability or unchanged prediction error.**

The bent paths depict axis-aligned movement: their lengths correspond to adding the absolute coordinate errors.

## Mean / squared-error panel

The old dataset chooses $(6,6,6)$:

$$
(6-6)^2+(3-6)^2+(9-6)^2=18.
$$

The new dataset chooses $(7,7,7)$:

$$
(6-7)^2+(3-7)^2+(12-7)^2
=1+16+25=42.
$$

For the new dataset, keeping the old constant $6$ would give

$$
0+9+36=45,
$$

so moving to $7$ reduces squared error by $3$.

The straight residual segments at the mean targets are perpendicular to the constant line. For example, the new residual is $(-1,-4,5)$, whose entries sum to zero.

## Why our sorted example has different coordinates but identical conclusions

Elsewhere we used $(3,6,12)$ to make the median easy to see. The panel uses $(6,3,12)$, preserving the original observation order.

Reordering the coordinates changes where a point appears relative to the labeled axes, but it changes neither its mean nor the sum of absolute or squared coordinate errors. The constant targets remain $(6,6,6)$ for the median and $(7,7,7)$ for the mean.

## Important distinctions

- The colored dots are entire datasets, not individual observations in a conventional three-point scatterplot.
- The mean is a scalar; the graphic's target is the constant vector formed by repeating that scalar in every coordinate.
- The source calls both targets “projection targets.” For the mean, this is an **orthogonal Euclidean projection**. For the median, it is a closest point under the $L_1$ metric; its residual generally is not perpendicular to the line.
- A screenshot of a 3D scene can obscure depth. The equations, rather than apparent screen overlap, establish the targets.
