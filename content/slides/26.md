# Standard deviation is a ruler for spread

Variance measures squared distance from the mean. Taking its square root brings the answer back to the data's original units, giving us a ruler we can compare with individual observations.

## Square the units, then return to them

If the observations are in minutes, deviations are in minutes and squared deviations are in minutes squared:

$$
\underbrace{s^2}_{\text{minutes}^2}
=\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2}{n-1},
\qquad
\underbrace{s}_{\text{minutes}}=\sqrt{s^2}.
$$

SD describes the scale of variation around the mean. It is not the arithmetic average of absolute distances. We will calculate both measures for heart rates shortly.

## Zero SD means every observed value is identical

For $n\ge2$, every term $(x_i-\bar x)^2$ is nonnegative. Their sum can equal zero only if every term is zero:

$$
s=0
\iff x_i=\bar x\text{ for every }i
\iff x_1=x_2=\cdots=x_n.
$$

A constant column cannot distinguish the observed rows. That is the useful meaning behind the slide's “no informational entropy” wording. A constant sample does not prove that the feature is constant in the population or scientifically irrelevant elsewhere.

## An extreme observation can dominate

Move one observation farther and farther from the rest. Its squared distance grows, and sample SD can grow without bound. Like the mean, SD is **non-robust**: one sufficiently extreme replacement can drive it arbitrarily far. Middle-rank summaries such as the IQR respond less strongly to a few extremes.

This does not mean every outlier should be deleted. It may be a measurement error, a rare real event, or the observation that matters most. Investigate its meaning.

## Shift location; stretch spread

Reordering values changes none of these summaries. Adding the same constant to all values moves the mean but leaves distances unchanged. Multiplication stretches or reflects those distances.

Under $y_i=ax_i+b$,

$$
\bar y=a\bar x+b,
\qquad
s_y^2=a^2s_x^2,
\qquad
s_y=|a|s_x.
$$

| Transformation | Mean | Variance | SD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add 10 | Increases by 10 | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| Multiply by 10 | Multiplied by 10 | Multiplied by 100 | Multiplied by 10 |
| Multiply by $-2$ | Multiplied by $-2$ | Multiplied by 4 | Multiplied by 2 |
| Replace $x$ with $3x+10$ | $3\bar x+10$ | $9s^2$ | $3s$ |

The absolute value matters: multiplying by $-2$ reverses the number line and doubles distances. Spread cannot become negative. Range and IQR have the same absolute scale factor $|a|$ under consistent, transformation-respecting definitions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Derive the transformation rules</summary>

Averaging $y_i=ax_i+b$ gives $\bar y=a\bar x+b$. Therefore

$$
y_i-\bar y=(ax_i+b)-(a\bar x+b)=a(x_i-\bar x).
$$

Squaring multiplies each contribution by $a^2$. The common translation $b$ disappears. The divisor is unchanged because the number of observations is unchanged, so $s_y^2=a^2s_x^2$. Taking square roots gives $s_y=|a|s_x$.

</details>

## What can you do with the answer?

Use SD to describe spread in the original units, compare variability in comparable datasets, calculate standardized distances, and interpret approximately normal data with the empirical rule. SD alone does not reveal the full shape, prove normality, or establish a percentile.

## Check your understanding

A dataset has mean 20 and SD 4. You replace each value by $-2x+10$. What are the new mean, SD, and variance?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

The mean is $-2(20)+10=-30$. SD is $|-2|(4)=8$. The original variance is $4^2=16$, so the new variance is $(-2)^2(16)=64$. The shift changes the center; the scale factor changes spread.

</details>
