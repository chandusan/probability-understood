# An 88 needs a comparison scale

You scored **88**, and the class average was **76**. You know you are above average. What you do not yet know is whether you are a little above the crowd or far beyond almost everyone in it. The missing information is **spread**.

## Same advantage, different achievement

Look at the two actual exam histograms above. Both mark the average at 76 with a solid red line and your 88 with a dashed blue line.

In **Scenario 1**, most scores crowd into a narrow interval near the mid-70s. Your score lies in the thin right tail, beyond almost the whole cluster. In **Scenario 2**, scores are spread much more widely across the displayed 50–100 interval. An 88 remains a good score, but it is less unusual relative to that wider distribution.

Your numerical advantage never changed:

$$
88-76=12\text{ points}.
$$

Its meaning changed because the class's ordinary differences changed. A twelve-point departure is large beside a tightly packed group and less remarkable beside a widely dispersed one.

## Two cafés tell the same story

Imagine these waiting times, in minutes:

| Café | Three recorded waits | Mean |
|---|---|---:|
| Consistent | 5, 6, 7 | 6 |
| Unpredictable | 0, 6, 12 | 6 |

The average alone makes the cafés look identical. Their customers have very different experiences. One café keeps almost everyone close to six minutes; the other produces a mixture of immediate service and a long wait.

A center answers **“Where is the group located?”** A dispersion measure answers **“How different are the individual observations from that shared summary?”** We need both to understand what an observation means.

## What the picture cannot tell us

We can make a qualitative comparison from the two histograms, but neither the mean nor the picture supplies an exact percentile for 88. The slides do not give the two standard deviations or the underlying exam scores. We should therefore say “more unusual in Scenario 1,” rather than invent a precise class rank or z-score.

**Memory image:** a crowd can stand at the same average location while forming either a tight huddle or a long procession.

## Check your understanding

Two classes both average 70. In one, almost everyone scores between 68 and 72; in the other, scores stretch from 40 to 100. In which class would an 80 look more exceptional, and why?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the reasoning</summary>

An 80 looks more exceptional in the tightly clustered class. It is ten points above the mean in both classes, but ten points is much larger relative to the ordinary variation in the first. An exact percentile still requires more information than those descriptions provide.

</details>
