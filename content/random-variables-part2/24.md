# Read the customer table as a distribution over everyone

A customer model records churn status and engagement tier together. Before computing anything, make the unit of observation and the meaning of each cell explicit: every entry is a fraction of the entire modeled customer population.

## State the model and the question

Choose one customer at random from the population represented by the model. Define

$$
X=\begin{cases}0,&\text{retained},\\1,&\text{churned},\end{cases}
\qquad
Y=\begin{cases}1,&\text{low engagement},\\2,&\text{medium engagement},\\3,&\text{high engagement}.\end{cases}
$$

The joint PMF is

| Churn status $X$ | Low: $Y=1$ | Medium: $Y=2$ | High: $Y=3$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Retained: $X=0$ | $0.05$ | $0.25$ | $0.40$ |
| Churned: $X=1$ | $0.15$ | $0.10$ | $0.05$ |

The task is to find the **marginal distribution of $X$** and the **marginal distribution of $Y$**.

## Translate a cell into ordinary language

The bottom-left cell means

$$
P(X=1,Y=1)=0.15.
$$

Thus 15% of all customers both churned and had low engagement. It does not yet tell us the churn percentage *among low-engagement customers*. That question has a different denominator.

To make the joint probabilities tangible, imagine a population of 100 customers distributed exactly according to this table: 5 retained low-engagement customers, 25 retained medium-engagement customers, 40 retained high-engagement customers, and respectively 15, 10, and 5 churned customers in those tiers. This is an illustrative population consistent with the model, not a claim about the source dataset's actual size.

## Check that this is a valid joint PMF

Each entry is nonnegative, and

$$
0.05+0.25+0.40+0.15+0.10+0.05=1.
$$

The six groups are disjoint: one customer has one status and one engagement tier. They cover the modeled population, so their probabilities must sum to one.

## Identify the cells each question requires

For $P(X=1)$, keep the entire churned row. A churned customer could have any of the three engagement levels.

For $P(Y=1)$, keep the entire low-engagement column. A low-engagement customer could be retained or churned.

These are additions over disjoint groups. The denominator remains the whole population in both cases. The next page completes and interprets both marginal distributions.
