# Reverse the conditioning and normalize rows instead

Knowing the number of sixes leaves a binomial number of fives among the non-six rolls. The fair die treats faces five and six symmetrically, but reversing a conditional question still changes which group supplies the denominator.

## State the reversed question

Roll a fair die independently five times. Let $X$ count sixes and $Y$ count fives. After observing $X=x$, we want

$$
P(Y=y\mid X=x)=\frac{p(x,y)}{p_X(x)}.
$$

There are $5-x$ non-six positions. Each permits the five faces $1,2,3,4,5$, of which exactly one is five. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(Y=y\mid X=x)
=\binom{5-x}{y}\left(\frac15\right)^y
\left(\frac45\right)^{5-x-y}},
$$

for $y=0,1,\ldots,5-x$. Equivalently, $Y\mid(X=x)\sim\operatorname{Bin}(5-x,1/5)$.

As before, the observation gives only a count, not the locations of the sixes. Every possible choice of those locations gives the same distribution for the remaining five count, so averaging over the unknown locations preserves this result.

## Read each row as a complete PMF

Rows now specify the observed six count $x$, and columns list possible five counts $y$. These decimals are exact.

| $x\backslash y$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.32768 | 0.40960 | 0.20480 | 0.05120 | 0.00640 | 0.00032 |
| 1 | 0.40960 | 0.40960 | 0.15360 | 0.02560 | 0.00160 | 0 |
| 2 | 0.51200 | 0.38400 | 0.09600 | 0.00800 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 0.64000 | 0.32000 | 0.04000 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

| 5 | 1.00000 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Every row sums to one. This table is the transpose of the preceding conditional table because exchanging the labels five and six preserves the model.

## Symmetry does not allow arbitrary reversal of a conditional

Consider the single joint event $X=1,Y=2$. Its probability is $480/7776$. The two conditional questions give

$$
P(X=1\mid Y=2)=\frac{480/7776}{1250/7776}
=0.384,
$$

$$
P(Y=2\mid X=1)=\frac{480/7776}{3125/7776}
=0.1536.
$$

The numerator is the same, but $Y=2$ and $X=1$ describe groups of different sizes. The answers therefore differ.

The symmetry instead guarantees

$$
P(X=1\mid Y=2)=P(Y=1\mid X=2).
$$

Both sides ask for one occurrence of a chosen face given two occurrences of the other face. Swapping the **variable labels while keeping their respective numbers** maps one question to the other.

## Check your understanding

Given that four sixes appeared, what is the probability of at least one five?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Only one non-six position remains</summary>

The remaining position is equally likely to show $1,2,3,4,$ or $5$. Thus $P(Y\ge1\mid X=4)=P(Y=1\mid X=4)=1/5$. Two fives are impossible, and $P(Y=0\mid X=4)=4/5$.
