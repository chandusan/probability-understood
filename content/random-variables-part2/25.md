# Customer marginals are row totals and column totals

For the customer model, marginalizing churn status means pooling all engagement tiers, and marginalizing engagement means pooling both churn statuses. These totals answer population-wide questions while preserving the joint table's original probability weights.

## Add the rows to get the churn distribution

Let $X=0$ mean retained and $X=1$ mean churned; let $Y=1,2,3$ mean low, medium, and high engagement. The completed table is

| Churn status $X$ | Low: $Y=1$ | Medium: $Y=2$ | High: $Y=3$ | $p_X(x)$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Retained: $X=0$ | $0.05$ | $0.25$ | $0.40$ | $0.70$ |
| Churned: $X=1$ | $0.15$ | $0.10$ | $0.05$ | $0.30$ |
| $p_Y(y)$ | $0.20$ | $0.35$ | $0.45$ | $1$ |

For retention, add the first row:

$$
p_X(0)=0.05+0.25+0.40=0.70.
$$

For churn, add the second row:

$$
p_X(1)=0.15+0.10+0.05=0.30.
$$

The marginal distribution of $X$ is therefore 70% retained and 30% churned. These probabilities add to one because the two statuses cover everyone and cannot overlap.

## Add the columns to get the engagement distribution

Pooling retained and churned customers within each tier gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_Y(1)&=0.05+0.15=0.20,\\
p_Y(2)&=0.25+0.10=0.35,\\
p_Y(3)&=0.40+0.05=0.45.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus 20% of all customers have low engagement, 35% medium engagement, and 45% high engagement. Again, $0.20+0.35+0.45=1$.

In the illustrative population of 100 customers, these are literally 20, 35, and 45 customers. The row totals are 70 retained and 30 churned.

## Keep joint, marginal, and conditional questions distinct

The table allows three questions that sound similar but count different groups:

| Question | Calculation | Meaning in 100 illustrative customers |
|---|---|---|
| Low engagement **and** churned? | $P(X=1,Y=1)=0.15$ | 15 of all 100 |
| Churned, whatever the engagement? | $P(X=1)=0.30$ | 30 of all 100 |
| Churned **among** low-engagement customers? | $P(X=1\mid Y=1)=0.15/0.20=0.75$ | 15 of the 20 low-engagement customers |

The third question changes the denominator. It introduces a conditional distribution, which the next section develops carefully.
