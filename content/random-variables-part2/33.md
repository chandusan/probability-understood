# Low-engagement churn: condition first, then compare with the baseline

The model assigns 15% of all customers to the combination “low engagement and churned.” Because only 20% of customers have low engagement, the churn probability within that group is 75%. Comparing it with the overall 30% churn rate gives the requested ratio of 2.5.

## State both questions and the full table

Select a customer at random from the modeled population. Let $X=0$ mean retained and $X=1$ mean churned. Let $Y=1,2,3$ mean low, medium, and high engagement. The joint probabilities and their totals are

| Status $X$ | Low: $Y=1$ | Medium: $Y=2$ | High: $Y=3$ | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Retained: 0 | $0.05$ | $0.25$ | $0.40$ | $0.70$ |
| Churned: 1 | $0.15$ | $0.10$ | $0.05$ | $0.30$ |
| Total | $0.20$ | $0.35$ | $0.45$ | $1$ |

Part (a) asks for the complete conditional distribution of churn status $X$ given low engagement, $Y=1$. Part (b) asks how its churn probability compares with the **overall baseline churn rate**.

## Part (a): restrict attention to the low-engagement column

The probability of belonging to that column is

$$
P(Y=1)=P(X=0,Y=1)+P(X=1,Y=1)
=0.05+0.15=0.20.
$$

Divide each selected cell by this column total:

$$
P(X=0\mid Y=1)=\frac{0.05}{0.20}=\frac14=0.25,
$$

$$
P(X=1\mid Y=1)=\frac{0.15}{0.20}=\frac34=0.75.
$$

The requested conditional distribution is therefore

| Status $x$ | $P(X=x\mid Y=1)$ |
|---|---:|
| Retained: 0 | $0.25$ |
| Churned: 1 | $0.75$ |

Its entries sum to one. In an illustrative population of 100 customers matching the table, 20 have low engagement: 5 retained and 15 churned. The conditional churn fraction is $15/20$, not $15/100$.

## Part (b): compare with the overall baseline

Before learning a customer's engagement tier, the baseline churn probability is the total churned row:

$$
P(X=1)=0.15+0.10+0.05=0.30.
$$

The ratio requested by the exercise is

$$
\boxed{\frac{P(X=1\mid Y=1)}{P(X=1)}
=\frac{0.75}{0.30}=2.5.}
$$

According to this model, the churn probability for a low-engagement customer is **2.5 times the population's baseline churn probability**. Equivalently, it is 150% higher relative to that baseline. The absolute increase is $0.75-0.30=0.45$, or 45 percentage points.

## Why a comparison with non-low engagement gives a different number

The preceding definition of relative risk compared a selected group with its complement. This exercise instead explicitly asks for the overall baseline, which includes low-engagement customers. The value 2.5 answers the exercise as written.

If we wanted the usual two-group comparison “low versus non-low,” the denominator would be the churn probability among medium- and high-engagement customers:

$$
P(X=1\mid Y\ne1)
=\frac{0.10+0.05}{0.35+0.45}
=\frac{0.15}{0.80}=0.1875.
$$

That different question has ratio

$$
\frac{P(X=1\mid Y=1)}{P(X=1\mid Y\ne1)}
=\frac{0.75}{0.1875}=4.
$$

Both calculations are valid; their comparison groups differ. Naming the denominator prevents “relative risk” from hiding this distinction. Neither comparison alone tells us whether changing engagement would cause a change in churn.
