# Count the arrangements by choosing positions

In five die rolls, fix a desired count of $x$ sixes and $y$ fives. The other $5-x-y$ rolls have faces in $\{1,2,3,4\}$, denoted $F$. Each fixed arrangement of these categories has the same probability. We now count how many arrangements there are.

## Choose the six positions first

There are five labeled positions: rolls 1 through 5. Choose which $x$ positions will contain sixes:

$$\binom5x\text{ choices}.$$

The sixes are indistinguishable as category labels. Choosing their positions already determines where they go; there is no extra $x!$ factor.

## Choose the five positions from those left

After placing the sixes, exactly $5-x$ positions remain. Choose $y$ of these for the fives:

$$\binom{5-x}y\text{ choices}.$$

We do not use $\binom5y$, because the positions already occupied by sixes are unavailable. A roll cannot be both a five and a six.

The remaining positions must all be $F$, giving exactly one further category arrangement. This is the factor $\binom{5-x-y}{5-x-y}=1$ shown in the source.

Consequently,

$$\boxed{K=\binom5x\binom{5-x}y.}$$

Every valid pattern is produced once: it tells us exactly which positions were selected in both steps.

## See the cancellation in factorial form

Expanding the binomial coefficients gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
K
&=\frac{5!}{x!(5-x)!}\cdot
\frac{(5-x)!}{y!(5-x-y)!}\\[3pt]
&=\boxed{\frac{5!}{x!y!(5-x-y)!}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The $(5-x)!$ factor cancels between the two fractions. The result is called a **multinomial coefficient**: it counts arrangements containing specified numbers of several categories.

For example, with $x=2$ and $y=1$,

$$K=\binom52\binom31=10\cdot3=30.$$

Each such arrangement has two sixes, one five, and two $F$ positions. There are $4^2=16$ choices of actual faces for those two $F$ positions. Therefore this event contains $30\cdot16=480$ of the 7776 elementary die sequences.

## Why the factorial denominator removes duplicates

<details markdown="1"><summary>Derive the same count by starting from $5!$ permutations</summary>

Temporarily give every category symbol its own label, such as $6_1,6_2,5_1,F_1,F_2$ when $(x,y)=(2,1)$. The five labeled items have $5!$ arrangements.

Now erase those labels. Swapping the two six labels does not change the category pattern, nor does swapping the two $F$ labels. Each category pattern was counted $2!\,1!\,2!=4$ times. Hence the correct count is $5!/4=30$.

For general counts, each pattern is repeated $x!$ times through permutations of its six labels, $y!$ times through permutations of its five labels, and $(5-x-y)!$ times through permutations of its $F$ labels. Dividing by their product gives $5!/[x!y!(5-x-y)!]$.

</details>

## Assemble the two factors

Different category patterns cannot occur together, so their probabilities add. Since all patterns with these fixed counts have the same probability,

$$
p(x,y)=
\frac{5!}{x!y!(5-x-y)!}
\left(\frac16\right)^x
\left(\frac16\right)^y
\left(\frac46\right)^{5-x-y}.
$$

The factorial factor counts positions. The powers give the probability of one such arrangement. They perform different jobs.
