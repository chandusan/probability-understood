# Assemble and interpret the multinomial joint PMF

For five independent fair-die rolls, let $X$ count sixes and $Y$ count fives. A pair $(x,y)$ requires $x$ sixes, $y$ fives, and $5-x-y$ faces from $\{1,2,3,4\}$. The probability is the number of ways to place these categories multiplied by the probability of one placement.

## Write the complete distribution, including its support

$$
\boxed{
p(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{5!}{x!y!(5-x-y)!}
\left(\dfrac16\right)^x
\left(\dfrac16\right)^y
\left(\dfrac46\right)^{5-x-y},
&x,y\text{ nonnegative integers},\ x+y\le5,\\[6pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

The source displays the condition $x+y\le5$. We also need to state that both counts are nonnegative integers. For example, a pair such as $(1.5,1)$ is not a possible pair of counts, even though its sum is less than 5.

The factors have separate meanings:

| Factor | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| $5!/[x!y!(5-x-y)!]$ | Number of category arrangements |
| $(1/6)^x$ | The required sixes in one arrangement |
| $(1/6)^y$ | The required fives in that arrangement |
| $(4/6)^{5-x-y}$ | The remaining rolls are neither six nor five |

## A version using only favorable and total counts

All powers in the denominator combine into $6^5$. Thus the same formula is

$$
\boxed{
p(x,y)=\frac{\dfrac{5!}{x!y!(5-x-y)!}\,4^{5-x-y}}{7776}.
}
$$

The numerator counts actual face sequences: choose the category arrangement, then choose one of four faces independently for every remaining position.

For example,

$$
p(2,1)=\frac{\dfrac{5!}{2!1!2!}\,4^2}{7776}
=\frac{30\cdot16}{7776}
=\frac{480}{7776}\approx0.06172840.
$$

## What makes this a multinomial distribution?

A binomial count comes from repeated independent trials with two categories, such as “six” and “not six.” Here we retain three categories: “six,” “five,” and “neither.” Each trial still has fixed category probabilities and is independent of the other trials.

The three category counts follow a **multinomial distribution**. Because the third count equals $5-X-Y$, the pair $(X,Y)$ contains all the information about those three totals.

Compare this with the image audit: there, an image is removed after selection, so the remaining category proportions change. That leads to the hypergeometric counting formula. Here, rolling a six does not remove a six from the die; the next roll still has six probability $1/6$.

## Independent rolls do not make the counts independent

The independence assumption applies to different rolls. Both counts use the same five rolls and compete for those positions. For example, learning $X=5$ forces $Y=0$.

Thus $P(X=5,Y=1)=0$, even though $P(X=5)>0$ and $P(Y=1)>0$. We cannot calculate this joint PMF by multiplying the two separate binomial PMFs.

## Check the total probability by partitioning sequences

Each of the 7776 actual die sequences belongs to exactly one pair $(x,y)$. Summing the numerator over the 21 possible pairs therefore counts all 7776 sequences once. Dividing by 7776 gives

$$\sum_{x=0}^{5}\sum_{y=0}^{5-x}p(x,y)=1.$$

This checks the distribution directly from the sample space, without needing a new algebraic identity.
