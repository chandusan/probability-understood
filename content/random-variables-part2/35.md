# Normalize the nine weights by their total

For the model $p(x,y)=k(x^y/y!)2^{-x}$ on $x=1,2,3$ and $y=0,1,2$, the unnormalized weights sum to $57/16$. A common multiplier of $16/57$ therefore makes the joint probability total exactly one.

## Evaluate the three weights for each tier

Write $w(x,y)=(x^y/y!)2^{-x}$. For $x=1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
w(1,0)&=\frac{1^0}{0!}\frac12=\frac12=\frac8{16},\\
w(1,1)&=\frac{1^1}{1!}\frac12=\frac12=\frac8{16},\\
w(1,2)&=\frac{1^2}{2!}\frac12=\frac14=\frac4{16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first row totals $(8+8+4)/16=20/16$.

For $x=2$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
w(2,0)&=\frac{2^0}{0!}\frac14=\frac14=\frac4{16},\\
w(2,1)&=\frac{2^1}{1!}\frac14=\frac12=\frac8{16},\\
w(2,2)&=\frac{2^2}{2!}\frac14=\frac12=\frac8{16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This row also totals $20/16$.

For $x=3$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
w(3,0)&=\frac{3^0}{0!}\frac18=\frac18=\frac2{16},\\
w(3,1)&=\frac{3^1}{1!}\frac18=\frac38=\frac6{16},\\
w(3,2)&=\frac{3^2}{2!}\frac18=\frac9{16}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last row totals $(2+6+9)/16=17/16$. These row totals can exceed one because we are still working with weights, not probabilities.

## Put every weight in one table

| $x\backslash y$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | Weight total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $8/16$ | $8/16$ | $4/16$ | $20/16$ |
| 2 | $4/16$ | $8/16$ | $8/16$ | $20/16$ |
| 3 | $2/16$ | $6/16$ | $9/16$ | $17/16$ |

The total over all nine allowed pairs is

$$
S=\frac{20+20+17}{16}=\frac{57}{16}.
$$

The equation $kS=1$ now gives

$$
\boxed{k=\frac1S=\frac{16}{57}.}
$$

## See how the normalization acts on one cell

For an Enterprise observation with two breaches,

$$
p(3,2)=k\,w(3,2)
=\frac{16}{57}\cdot\frac9{16}
=\frac9{57}.
$$

Every table entry has the same cancellation: an unnormalized weight $r/16$ becomes probability $r/57$. Their numerators $8,8,4,4,8,8,2,6,9$ add to 57, so the normalized probabilities add to one.

Dividing every weight by their total is the central idea. The common denominator 16 is only a convenient way to perform the arithmetic.

## Check the model conditions

All nine weights are positive, and $k=16/57>0$, so all joint probabilities are nonnegative. Their sum is $kS=1$. Outside the stated support, probability is zero. These checks establish a valid discrete joint PMF, ready for marginal and conditional calculations.
