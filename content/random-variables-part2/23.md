# Recover the dice marginals by counting or by summing

Roll a fair die independently five times. Let $X$ count sixes and $Y$ count fives. Summing the joint table recovers the familiar binomial distribution for each count, because the other distinguished face becomes part of the ordinary “not a success” category.

## Use exact counts before rounding

There are $6^5=7776$ equally likely ordered roll sequences. The following table lists their **counts**, so divide every interior entry and every marginal total by 7776 to obtain its probability.

| $X\backslash Y$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Row total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1024 | 1280 | 640 | 160 | 20 | 1 | 3125 |
| 1 | 1280 | 1280 | 480 | 80 | 5 | 0 | 3125 |
| 2 | 640 | 480 | 120 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 1250 |
| 3 | 160 | 80 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 250 |
| 4 | 20 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 25 |
| 5 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| Column total | 3125 | 3125 | 1250 | 250 | 25 | 1 | 7776 |

For instance, exactly two sixes and one five has

$$
\binom52\binom31 4^2=10\cdot3\cdot16=480
$$

sequences. Choose two six positions, choose one five position among the remaining three, and fill the two other positions with any of $1,2,3,4$.

The slide displays probabilities rounded to four decimal places. Exact counts avoid small discrepancies when adding those rounded entries.

## Obtain the marginal by summing a row

To find $P(X=2)$, retain every allowed number of fives:

$$
P(X=2)=\frac{640+480+120+10}{7776}
=\frac{1250}{7776}\approx0.16075.
$$

The complete marginal distributions are

| Count $r$ | $P(X=r)$ | $P(Y=r)$ |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | $3125/7776$ | $3125/7776$ |
| 1 | $3125/7776$ | $3125/7776$ |
| 2 | $1250/7776$ | $1250/7776$ |
| 3 | $250/7776$ | $250/7776$ |
| 4 | $25/7776$ | $25/7776$ |
| 5 | $1/7776$ | $1/7776$ |

They agree because exchanging the face labels five and six preserves every sequence's probability.

## Derive the same answer directly

For exactly $x$ sixes, choose their $x$ positions in $\binom5x$ ways. Those positions must show six. Each of the other $5-x$ positions can show any of the five non-six faces. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=x)
&=\frac{\binom5x5^{5-x}}{6^5}\\
&=\boxed{\binom5x\left(\frac16\right)^x
\left(\frac56\right)^{5-x}},
\qquad x=0,1,\ldots,5.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is $X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(5,1/6)$: five independent trials, with success meaning a six. The same argument gives $Y\sim\operatorname{Bin}(5,1/6)$.

Summing over $Y$ has combined “five” and “one of $1,2,3,4$” into the single category “not six,” whose probability is $1/6+4/6=5/6$.

## Equal marginals do not make the two counts independent

If $Y=5$, all rolls were fives, so $X$ must be zero. In contrast, before observing $Y$, $P(X=0)=3125/7776<1$. Information about one count can change the distribution of the other even though their separate distributions have identical shapes.
