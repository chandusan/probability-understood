# Marginalizing pools groups; conditioning selects one group

A marginal distribution describes a variable across the whole population. A conditional distribution describes it inside a group specified by an observation. The distinction is best understood by watching which outcomes remain in the denominator.

## Compare three questions on the same customer population

Let $X$ indicate churn, with $X=1$ for churned, and let $Y=1,2,3$ label low, medium, and high engagement. The relevant probabilities are

| Engagement tier | All customers in tier | Churned and in tier |
|---|---:|---:|
| Low | $0.20$ | $0.15$ |
| Medium | $0.35$ | $0.10$ |
| High | $0.45$ | $0.05$ |

**Joint:** $P(X=1,Y=1)=0.15$. Out of all customers, how many satisfy both conditions?

**Marginal:** $P(X=1)=0.15+0.10+0.05=0.30$. Out of all customers, how many churned, with any engagement tier allowed?

**Conditional:** $P(X=1\mid Y=1)=0.15/0.20=0.75$. Out of only the low-engagement customers, how many churned?

Marginalizing adds across the tier groups. Conditioning chooses one tier and makes that group the reference population.

## “Held fixed” means fixed in the question

The slide describes conditioning as holding the other variable fixed. For $P(X=x\mid Y=1)$, this means considering only outcomes whose recorded $Y$ value is one.

It does not mean physically forcing customers to have low engagement. The distribution describes an observed subgroup. A claim about what would happen if we changed a customer's engagement would require additional assumptions beyond this table.

## The marginal is a weighted average of the conditionals

The conditional churn probabilities are

$$
P(X=1\mid Y=1)=\frac34,
\quad P(X=1\mid Y=2)=\frac27,
\quad P(X=1\mid Y=3)=\frac19.
$$

Recover the overall churn probability by weighting each conditional probability by its group's size:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=1)
&=\frac34(0.20)+\frac27(0.35)+\frac19(0.45)\\
&=0.15+0.10+0.05=0.30.
\end{aligned}
$$

Each product recovers one joint cell. Adding the products pools the three disjoint groups again. An unweighted average of the three conditional rates would give each tier equal importance even though the tiers contain different fractions of customers.

## A useful reading habit

Before calculating, finish this sentence: **“Among which outcomes am I measuring a fraction?”**

If the answer is “all outcomes,” you are looking for a joint or marginal probability. If it is “only outcomes satisfying the information given,” you need a conditional probability with that group's probability in the denominator.
