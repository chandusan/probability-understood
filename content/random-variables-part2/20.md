# Use a reversible swap to count the two sides equally

For five independent fair-die rolls, let $X$ count sixes and $Y$ count fives. Summing the nine cells with $X>Y$ gives $2676/7776\approx0.3441$. We can derive the same result by explaining exactly why the outcomes with more sixes and those with more fives come in equally likely pairs.

## Read the shaded cells in the source

Rows represent $X$ and columns represent $Y$. The cells below the main diagonal have row label greater than column label, so they satisfy $x>y$. Only cells with $x+y\le5$ have positive probability.

The diagonal itself represents $X=Y$. Those outcomes are excluded because “more” is a strict inequality.

## Give each qualifying sequence one partner

Take any actual five-roll sequence and exchange every 5 with 6 and every 6 with 5. Leave faces 1 through 4 unchanged. For example,

$$66512\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad55612.$$

The first sequence has two sixes and one five. Its partner has one six and two fives. More generally, the swap sends counts $(x,y)$ to $(y,x)$, so every outcome with $X>Y$ becomes one with $Y>X$.

The correspondence is exact:

- Applying the swap twice recovers the original sequence, so two different sequences cannot acquire the same partner.
- Every sequence with more fives has a partner with more sixes, obtained by that same swap.
- Both sequences have probability $1/6^5$ because the rolls are independent and the die is fair.

Therefore the two events contain the same number of equally likely sequences, giving

$$\boxed{P(X>Y)=P(Y>X).}$$

This is the reason symmetry works here: the operation changes which side wins while preserving probability.

## First remove the tied outcomes

Every sequence belongs to exactly one of three disjoint events:

$$X>Y,\qquad X=Y,\qquad Y>X.$$

If $X=Y=k$, the special faces occupy $2k$ of the five rolls. Hence $k$ can only be 0, 1, or 2. Count each case directly:

| Tie case | Why the count has these factors | Number of sequences |
|---|---|---:|
| $X=Y=0$ | Every position has one of four other faces | $4^5=1024$ |
| $X=Y=1$ | Choose the six position, then the five position; fill the other three | $5\cdot4\cdot4^3=1280$ |
| $X=Y=2$ | Choose two six positions, then two five positions from the remaining three; fill the last position | $\binom52\binom32\cdot4=120$ |

The tied cases total

$$1024+1280+120=2424,$$

so

$$P(X=Y)=\frac{2424}{7776}=\frac{101}{324}.$$

Even though five is odd, ties are possible: rolls showing 1 through 4 contribute to neither count.

## Split the remaining outcomes into equal halves

After removing ties, $7776-2424=5352$ sequences remain. The reversible swap puts exactly half on each side, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X>Y)
&=\frac{1-P(X=Y)}2\\
&=\frac12\left(1-\frac{2424}{7776}\right)\\
&=\frac{2676}{7776}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{223}{648}\approx0.34413580.}
\end{aligned}
$$

We divide by two only after removing ties. The two strict-inequality events share the remaining probability equally; they do not each receive half of the whole sample space.
