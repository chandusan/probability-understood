# Read the area under a density graph

The shaded region on the source slide represents $P(a\le X\le b)$. Its boundaries tell us which values of $X$ count; its area tells us how much probability those values carry.

## Read the axes before reading the shaded region

The horizontal axis contains possible values of $X$. The vertical axis contains density values $f(x)$. The entire area under the curve equals one. Shading only from $a$ to $b$ selects a portion of that total:

$$P(a\le X\le b)=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.$$

A taller part of the curve puts more probability into a small interval of a given width. A wider interval can accumulate more probability even when its density is lower. Comparing heights alone is therefore insufficient when interval widths differ.

## Build a probability from thin strips

Partition $[a,b]$ into many narrow pieces of width $\Delta x$. Choose a point $x_i$ in the $i$th piece. Its approximate probability is

$$\text{height}\times\text{width}=f(x_i)\Delta x.$$

Add the strip probabilities:

$$P(a\le X\le b)\approx\sum_i f(x_i)\Delta x.$$

The integral is the limit of these sums as the pieces become arbitrarily narrow. This gives a direct reason for integrating: we are adding the probability contributions over all parts of the interval.

## Work through a complete area calculation

For a concrete example, suppose

$$f(x)=\begin{cases}2x,&0<x<1,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$

The total region is a triangle of base $1$ and height $2$, so its area is $1$. Find $P(1/4<X<3/4)$.

Using an antiderivative,

$$P(1/4<X<3/4)=\int_{1/4}^{3/4}2x\,dx
=\left[x^2\right]_{1/4}^{3/4}
=\frac9{16}-\frac1{16}=\boxed{\frac12}.$$

Geometrically, the selected region is a trapezoid with width $1/2$ and parallel heights $1/2$ and $3/2$. Its area is

$$\frac{\frac12+\frac32}{2}\times\frac12=\frac12.$$

Both methods measure exactly the same region. The density at the midpoint happens to be $1$, but $1$ is not the interval probability; we still need to account for width.

## Connect this picture to the CDF

For this example, $F(x)=x^2$ on $0\le x\le1$. The large region from zero to $3/4$ contains probability $9/16$. The smaller region from zero to $1/4$ contains $1/16$. Removing the smaller from the larger leaves the shaded interval:

$$P(1/4<X<3/4)=F(3/4)-F(1/4).$$

The integral and the CDF difference are two descriptions of the same accumulation. When a CDF is already given, subtraction often saves us an unnecessary integration.
