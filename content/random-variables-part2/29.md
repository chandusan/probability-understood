# Condition the dice table by keeping and normalizing one column

Roll a fair die independently five times; let $X$ be the number of sixes and $Y$ the number of fives. If we learn that exactly two fives appeared, the remaining possibilities for the number of sixes must be measured among sequences with two fives.

## Specify the new information precisely

We are told $Y=2$. We know the **count** of fives, not their positions. We want the complete conditional distribution

$$
P(X=x\mid Y=2).
$$

The original sample space has $6^5=7776$ equally likely ordered sequences. To count sequences consistent with the observation, first choose the two five positions. Each remaining position can be any of the five faces other than five:

$$
\#\{Y=2\}=\binom52 5^3=10\cdot125=1250.
$$

Hence $P(Y=2)=1250/7776$. After conditioning, these 1250 equally likely sequences form the relevant group.

## Count each possible number of sixes inside that group

Having chosen the two five positions, choose $x$ six positions from the remaining three. Every other position must be one of $1,2,3,4$. This gives

$$
\#\{X=x,Y=2\}=\binom52\binom3x4^{3-x}.
$$

For example, $x=1$ gives $10\cdot3\cdot16=480$ sequences. Dividing by the 1250 sequences with two fives gives

$$
P(X=1\mid Y=2)=\frac{480}{1250}=0.384.
$$

Equivalently, use joint and marginal probabilities:

$$
\frac{P(X=1,Y=2)}{P(Y=2)}
=\frac{480/7776}{1250/7776}=\frac{480}{1250}.
$$

The original denominator cancels because both probabilities refer to the same initial sample space.

## Write the complete conditional distribution

| Sixes $x$ | Sequences with $X=x,Y=2$ | $P(X=x\mid Y=2)$ |
|---|---:|---|
| 0 | 640 | $640/1250=0.512$ |
| 1 | 480 | $480/1250=0.384$ |
| 2 | 120 | $120/1250=0.096$ |
| 3 | 10 | $10/1250=0.008$ |
| 4 or 5 | 0 | $0$ |

The four positive probabilities add to one. Four or five sixes are impossible because two of the five rolls were already fives.

## Recognize the changed success probability

In a position known not to be a five, the possible faces are $1,2,3,4,6$, each equally likely. One of those five faces is a six, so the six probability is $1/5$ within those positions.

This produces $X\mid(Y=2)\sim\operatorname{Bin}(3,1/5)$. Both parts have changed from the original marginal $\operatorname{Bin}(5,1/6)$: three non-five positions remain, and six occupies one of five remaining face choices.
