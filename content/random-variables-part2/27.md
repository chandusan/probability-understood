# A conditional PMF is a joint probability divided by the given group's total

To find the distribution of $X$ after observing $Y=y$, keep the $Y=y$ part of the joint table and divide each retained entry by that part's total. The denominator is determined by the information after the conditioning bar.

## Derive the formula from conditional probability

For events $A$ and $B$ with $P(B)>0$,

$$
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}.
$$

Set $A=\{X=x\}$ and $B=\{Y=y\}$. Their intersection is the event that both values occur, so

$$
\boxed{
p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=P(X=x\mid Y=y)
=\frac{p(x,y)}{p_Y(y)}
},\qquad p_Y(y)>0.
$$

The numerator is one joint cell. The denominator is the sum of **all** cells compatible with the observed $Y=y$:

$$
p_Y(y)=\sum_u p(u,y).
$$

Here $u$ runs through all possible values of $X$; using a different letter helps distinguish this sum from the particular value $x$ in the numerator.

## Verify that it really is a distribution

Once $y$ is fixed, the denominator stays the same while $x$ varies. Thus

$$
\sum_x p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)
=\frac{\sum_xp(x,y)}{p_Y(y)}
=\frac{p_Y(y)}{p_Y(y)}=1.
$$

All entries are nonnegative. We therefore obtain a valid PMF for $X$ within the observed group.

If $p_Y(y)=0$, the elementary ratio is undefined. There is no positive-probability group to normalize. We do not assign a conditional PMF by dividing zero by zero.

## Reverse the question carefully

If the observation is $X=x$ and we want the distribution of $Y$, then

$$
\boxed{
p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)=P(Y=y\mid X=x)
=\frac{p(x,y)}{p_X(x)}
},\qquad p_X(x)>0.
$$

The joint cell is the same, but the denominator changes. With rows labeled by $X$ and columns by $Y$, conditioning on $Y$ normalizes a column; conditioning on $X$ normalizes a row.

## One cell can answer two different conditional questions

In the customer model, $P(X=1,Y=1)=0.15$ means churned and low engagement. The low-engagement total is $P(Y=1)=0.20$, while the churn total is $P(X=1)=0.30$.

$$
P(X=1\mid Y=1)=\frac{0.15}{0.20}=0.75,
$$

$$
P(Y=1\mid X=1)=\frac{0.15}{0.30}=0.50.
$$

Among low-engagement customers, 75% churned. Among churned customers, 50% had low engagement. The two statements restrict attention to different groups, so there is no reason for their answers to agree.

## Recover a joint probability when needed

Rearranging the definition gives

$$
p(x,y)=p_Y(y)p_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y).
$$

For example, $0.20\cdot0.75=0.15$: 20% belong to the low-engagement group, and 75% of that group churned. Multiplication converts a within-group fraction back into a fraction of everyone.
