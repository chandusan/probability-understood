# Unpack the normalization and construct the whole CDF

The handwritten source solution obtains $c=n+1$ and $P(X>0.05)=1-(0.05)^{n+1}$. Let us unpack the normalization, assemble the full CDF, and check why the restriction on $n$ matters.

## Unpack the source normalization

For $n>-1$,

$$1=c\left[\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}\right]_0^1=\frac{c}{n+1}.$$

Hence the normalized density is

$$f(x)=\begin{cases}(n+1)x^n,&0<x<1,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$

Its nonnegative height and unit total area make it a valid density. The values assigned to the density exactly at zero and one do not affect any interval probabilities.

## Derive the CDF on each part of the number line

If $x\le0$, no probability has been accumulated. If $0<x<1$, accumulate the density from zero to $x$. Use $t$ as the integration variable so that $x$ remains the boundary:

$$F(x)=\int_0^x(n+1)t^n\,dt
=\left[t^{n+1}\right]_0^x=x^{n+1}.$$

If $x\ge1$, all probability has been included. Therefore,

$$\boxed{F(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\le0,\\x^{n+1},&0<x<1,\\1,&x\ge1.\end{cases}}$$

Now the requested tail is immediate:

$$P(X>0.05)=1-F(0.05)=1-(0.05)^{n+1}.$$

For any interval $0\le a<b\le1$, the same CDF gives $P(a<X\le b)=b^{n+1}-a^{n+1}$.

## Why an infinite height can still have finite area

When $-1<n<0$, $x^n$ grows without bound as $x$ approaches zero from the right. This does not automatically invalidate a density: we need finite area, not a finite maximum height.

For example, $n=-1/2$ gives $f(x)=1/(2\sqrt{x})$. Its area is

$$\int_0^1\frac1{2\sqrt{x}}\,dx
=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\left[\sqrt{x}\right]_{\varepsilon}^1
=1.$$

Here the integral is improper because the function becomes unbounded at the boundary; the limit gives it a precise meaning.

At $n=-1$, the integral of $1/x$ diverges logarithmically. For $n<-1$, the power integral also diverges at zero. No positive finite constant $c$ can make these divergent areas equal one. Choosing $c=0$ would instead give total area zero.

## A check that connects density, CDF, and a quantile

The median $m$ is a point with half the probability on each side. This CDF is strictly increasing within its support, so its median is the unique solution of

$$m^{n+1}=\frac12,\qquad \boxed{m=2^{-1/(n+1)}}.$$

For $n=0$, $m=1/2$. For $n=1$, $m=1/\sqrt2\approx0.7071$. The latter is larger because the density $2x$ places more probability toward the right end. A median divides **area** in half, not necessarily the horizontal interval.
