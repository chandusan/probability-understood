# Condition on a mixed CDF without losing its jumps

Let $X$ be pipeline execution time in minutes, with the CDF shown in the source graph. Find $P(X>2\mid X\le4)$: among executions completed within four minutes, what fraction took more than two minutes?

## Read the exact boundary values from the graph

The plotted curve has jumps. The filled point at each jump gives the CDF value at that threshold, because $F(x)$ includes executions with $X=x$.

| Boundary | Just before it | At the boundary | Point probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $F(0^-)=0$ | $F(0)=0.25$ | $P(X=0)=0.25$ |
| $2$ | $F(2^-)=0.45$ | $F(2)=0.65$ | $P(X=2)=0.20$ |
| $4$ | $F(4^-)=0.85$ | $F(4)=0.95$ | $P(X=4)=0.10$ |

The graph reaches $F(6)=1$. We do not need a formula for the curved portions: the two relevant CDF values already give the required probability.

## Name the condition and the event

Let $A=\{X>2\}$ be the event we want, and let $B=\{X\le4\}$ be the information we are given. Both happen exactly when $2<X\le4$.

Start with the conditional probability rule:

$$P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}.$$

The denominator is the probability of the new population, all executions taking at most four minutes:

$$P(B)=P(X\le4)=F(4)=0.95.$$

The numerator keeps the portion of that population taking more than two minutes:

$$P(A\cap B)=P(2<X\le4)=F(4)-F(2)=0.95-0.65=0.30.$$

Therefore,

$$\boxed{P(X>2\mid X\le4)=\frac{0.30}{0.95}=\frac6{19}\approx0.3158.}$$

The conditional probability is about **31.58%**.

## See the division using 100 representative executions

Imagine a population of 100 executions whose proportions exactly match this model. There are 95 at or below four minutes and 65 at or below two minutes. Remove the latter group from the former: $95-65=30$ executions satisfy $2<X\le4$.

Once told $X\le4$, we restrict attention to the 95 eligible executions. The desired fraction is $30/95$, not $30/100$. Conditioning changes the denominator because it changes which group we are comparing within.

## Check the endpoints explicitly

The event excludes $X=2$, so we subtract $F(2)=0.65$, including the whole jump at two. It includes $X=4$, so we use $F(4)=0.95$, including the jump at four.

We can also break the numerator into pieces:

$$P(2<X<4)=0.85-0.65=0.20,$$

$$P(X=4)=0.95-0.85=0.10.$$

Together these give $0.20+0.10=0.30$. The jump at two is absent; the jump at four is present.

<details markdown="1"><summary>What if the question included two minutes?</summary>

For $P(X\ge2\mid X\le4)$, the numerator is $P(2\le X\le4)$. Now we must keep the probability at two, so remove only what lies strictly below it:

$$P(X\ge2\mid X\le4)=\frac{F(4)-F(2^-)}{F(4)}
=\frac{0.95-0.45}{0.95}=\frac{10}{19}.$$

This differs by $0.20/0.95$, exactly the conditional share of the atom at two. For mixed distributions, an endpoint change can have a substantial effect.

</details>
