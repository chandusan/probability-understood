# Count sixes and fives in five independent die rolls

Roll a fair six-sided die five times, independently. Let $X$ be the number of sixes and $Y$ the number of fives. We want the joint PMF $p(x,y)=P(X=x,Y=y)$: the probability of exactly $x$ sixes and exactly $y$ fives among those five rolls.

## Identify the equally likely outcomes

An elementary outcome is an ordered sequence of five actual faces, such as $(6,2,5,6,1)$. Each position has six possible faces, so there are

$$6^5=7776$$

sequences. Fairness and independence make each sequence have probability $(1/6)^5$.

The example sequence gives $(X,Y)=(2,1)$. Many other sequences give the same pair. Our task is to count all of them, rather than treating the possible pairs as equally likely.

## Group the faces into three categories

For this question, faces 1, 2, 3, and 4 have the same role: they contribute to neither count. Call that category $F$. On each roll,

| Category | Meaning | Probability |
|---|---|---:|
| $6$ | A six | $1/6$ |
| $5$ | A five | $1/6$ |
| $F$ | Any of 1, 2, 3, or 4 | $4/6$ |

If the counts are $x$ sixes and $y$ fives, the remaining

$$5-x-y$$

rolls must belong to $F$. This third count is determined by the first two.

## State the support before writing a formula

The counts must be nonnegative integers, and a roll cannot be both a six and a five. Therefore

$$\boxed{x\ge0,\quad y\ge0,\quad x+y\le5,\quad x,y\text{ integers}.}$$

For example, $(2,1)$ and $(0,5)$ are possible, while $(4,2)$ is impossible. Individually, both $X$ and $Y$ range from 0 through 5, but not all $6\times6$ pairs can occur.

There are $6+5+4+3+2+1=21$ possible pairs: for each $x$, there are $6-x$ choices for $y$.

## Separate two questions in the derivation

First, what is the probability of one fixed arrangement of categories, such as $F5FF6$? Second, how many arrangements contain the same counts $x$ and $y$?

Once those are known, add the equally probable, disjoint arrangements:

$$
p(x,y)=
\underbrace{\text{number of category arrangements}}_{\text{count positions}}
\times
\underbrace{\text{probability of one arrangement}}_{\text{multiply across rolls}}.
$$

The next pages derive these two factors explicitly and then assemble the complete distribution.
