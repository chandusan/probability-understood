# Derive the joint PMF by choosing images from each category

Select 7 images uniformly without replacement from 8 with minor errors, 5 completely mislabeled, and 12 perfect images. For $X=x$ minor-error images and $Y=y$ completely mislabeled images, we must select exactly $7-x-y$ perfect images. This turns the event into three explicit choices.

## Count every qualifying sample once

There are

$$\binom8x$$

ways to choose the $x$ minor-error images. For each such choice, there are

$$\binom5y$$

ways to choose the $y$ completely mislabeled images. For each pair of choices, there are

$$\binom{12}{7-x-y}$$

ways to choose the perfect images.

The categories are disjoint. Their three selected subsets combine into one seven-image sample, and any qualifying sample splits back into exactly these three subsets. The multiplication rule for counting therefore gives

$$
\#\{X=x,Y=y\}=\binom8x\binom5y\binom{12}{7-x-y}.
$$

**Multiplying these counts does not assume the random variables are independent.** It counts the choices that construct one sample with a fixed composition.

## Divide by the equally likely sample count

All $\binom{25}{7}=480700$ seven-image subsets are equally likely. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
p(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\binom8x\binom5y\binom{12}{7-x-y}}{\binom{25}{7}},
&x,y\text{ integers},\ 0\le x\le7,\ 0\le y\le5,\ x+y\le7,\\[6pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
}
$$

This is an example of a **multivariate hypergeometric distribution**: sample without replacement from a finite population split into categories, and record the category counts. The name summarizes the sampling setup we have just counted.

## Work one cell all the way through

For $p(2,1)$, choose 2 of the 8 minor-error images, 1 of the 5 completely mislabeled images, and 4 of the 12 perfect images:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(2,1)
&=\frac{\binom82\binom51\binom{12}4}{\binom{25}7}\\
&=\frac{28\cdot5\cdot495}{480700}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{69300}{480700}\approx0.14416476.}
\end{aligned}
$$

So roughly 14.42% of all equally likely samples have exactly that composition. The factor $\binom{12}4$ matters: specifying the error counts still leaves many choices for the four perfect images.

## Check the edge cases

For no errors of either kind, all seven images are perfect:

$$p(0,0)=\frac{\binom{12}7}{\binom{25}7}=\frac{792}{480700}.$$

For seven minor-error images and no completely mislabeled images,

$$p(7,0)=\frac{\binom87}{\binom{25}7}=\frac8{480700}.$$

This second event is possible but extremely rare. A small rounded value must not be confused with an impossible event such as $p(7,1)=0$.

## Why the PMF adds to one

Every seven-image subset has exactly one pair of error counts. Grouping all 480700 subsets by that pair neither loses nor repeats any subset. Thus the favorable counts over the entire support add back to the denominator:

$$
\sum_{y=0}^{5}\sum_{x=0}^{7-y}
\binom8x\binom5y\binom{12}{7-x-y}
=\binom{25}7.
$$

Dividing by $\binom{25}7$ proves that the joint probabilities total 1.
