# Reverse the order to normalize an exponential density

Let $f(x,y)=ce^{x^2}$ on the triangle $0\le y\le x\le1$, and zero elsewhere. Find $c$ so that this is a probability density. The key is choosing an order that avoids integrating $e^{x^2}$ by itself.

## Write the normalization equation

The requirement is

$$
\iint_{0\le y\le x\le1}ce^{x^2}\,dx\,dy=1.
$$

Horizontal slices give

$$
c\int_0^1\int_y^1e^{x^2}\,dx\,dy=1.
$$

This is a valid setup, but the inner function $e^{x^2}$ has no elementary antiderivative. That does not make the probability undefined; it suggests finding a more convenient order.

## Keep the triangle, change the slices

The same support can be written as

$$
0\le x\le1,\qquad 0\le y\le x.
$$

Thus

$$
c\int_0^1\int_0^x e^{x^2}\,dy\,dx=1.
$$

With $x$ fixed, $e^{x^2}$ is constant with respect to $y$. The inner integral is simply height times interval length:

$$
\int_0^x e^{x^2}\,dy=xe^{x^2}.
$$

## The missing factor appears

We now have

$$
c\int_0^1xe^{x^2}\,dx=1.
$$

Set $u=x^2$. Then $du=2x\,dx$, so $x\,dx=du/2$. The endpoints remain 0 and 1:

$$
\int_0^1xe^{x^2}\,dx
=\frac12\int_0^1e^u\,du
=\frac{e-1}{2}.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{c=\frac{2}{e-1}.}
$$

## Why the reversal helped

Integrating over the available $y$ range produced its length $x$. That factor is exactly what the substitution $u=x^2$ needs. The easier algebra emerged from the geometry of the support.

Since $c>0$ and $e^{x^2}>0$, nonnegativity also holds. We have now checked both requirements for a density: nonnegative values and total integral one.
