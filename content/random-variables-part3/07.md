# A square example: integrate x first

Compute the integral of $f(x,y)=x^2y+xy^3$ over the unit square $0\le x\le1$, $0\le y\le1$. We will first integrate with respect to $x$, then verify the result using the other order.

## Start with the full integral

$$
I=\int_0^1\int_0^1(x^2y+xy^3)\,dx\,dy.
$$

There are two terms. The addition rule for integration lets us integrate them separately. In the inner integral, both $y$ and $y^3$ are constant multipliers.

## Integrate the inner expression term by term

$$
\int(x^2y+xy^3)\,dx
=\frac{x^3y}{3}+\frac{x^2y^3}{2}.
$$

At $x=1$ this is $y/3+y^3/2$; at $x=0$ it is zero. Hence

$$
I=\int_0^1\left(\frac y3+\frac{y^3}{2}\right)dy.
$$

Notice that $x$ has disappeared: all the horizontal contributions at each $y$ have already been added.

## Finish with the outer integral

$$
\begin{aligned}
I
&=\left[\frac{y^2}{6}+\frac{y^4}{8}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac16+\frac18
=\boxed{\frac7{24}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The value is positive, as expected because the surface is nonnegative on the square.

## A separate check using the product structure

Each term factors into a function of $x$ times a function of $y$. On a rectangle,

$$
\iint x^2y\,dx\,dy
=\left(\int_0^1x^2dx\right)\left(\int_0^1y\,dy\right)
=\frac13\frac12=\frac16.
$$

Similarly, the $xy^3$ term contributes $(1/2)(1/4)=1/8$. Their sum is again $7/24$. This separation works here because the support is a rectangle; it cannot be carried over unchanged to a triangle.
