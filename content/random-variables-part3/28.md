# Check normalization and conditional probabilities

For $f(x,y)=cx+1$ on $x>0$, $y>0$, $x+y<1$, normalization gave $c=3$. We now verify the result using horizontal slices and use the conditional densities to answer concrete probability questions.

## Normalize in the other order

With $y$ fixed, $0<x<1-y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\int_0^{1-y}(3x+1)\,dx\,dy
&=\int_0^1\left[\frac{3x^2}{2}+x\right]_0^{1-y}dy\\
&=\int_0^1\left[\frac32(1-y)^2+(1-y)\right]dy\\
&=\frac32\frac13+\frac12=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

This confirms the normalizing constant independently of the first integration order.

## Given X = 1/4, how likely is Y < 1/2?

The conditional support is $0<y<3/4$, and

$$
f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid1/4)=\frac1{3/4}=\frac43.
$$

Therefore

$$
P(Y<1/2\mid X=1/4)
=\int_0^{1/2}\frac43\,dy
=\boxed{\frac23}.
$$

The requested interval occupies two-thirds of the allowed interval, and the conditional density is uniform there.

## Reverse the condition

Given $Y=1/4$, the marginal denominator is

$$
f_Y(1/4)=\frac{(3/4)(17/4)}2=\frac{51}{32}.
$$

Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X<1/2\mid Y=1/4)
&=\int_0^{1/2}\frac{3x+1}{51/32}\,dx\\
&=\frac{32}{51}\left[\frac32x^2+x\right]_0^{1/2}\\
&=\frac{32}{51}\frac78
=\boxed{\frac{28}{51}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The two conditional questions have different answers. Conditioning on one coordinate fixes a different slice, and the density behaves differently along the two directions.

## A boundary caution

The conditional formulas require a positive marginal denominator. At the extreme endpoint $x=1$, the allowed $y$ interval collapses and $f_X(1)=0$; the displayed ratio cannot be evaluated there. This does not affect probabilities because that exact endpoint has probability zero.
