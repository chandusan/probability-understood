# Condition on a group of observed variables

A conditional joint distribution describes the variables still uncertain after another group has been observed. Restrict the original joint model to the observed values, then divide by the marginal probability or density of those observations.

## Write the rule for two groups

Let $\mathbf U=(X_1,\ldots,X_q)$ contain the target variables and $\mathbf V=(X_{q+1},\ldots,X_k)$ the observed variables. For $f_{\mathbf V}(\mathbf v)>0$,

$$
\boxed{
f_{\mathbf U\mid\mathbf V}(\mathbf u\mid\mathbf v)
=\frac{f_{\mathbf U,\mathbf V}(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)}{f_{\mathbf V}(\mathbf v)}.
}
$$

The denominator integrates the numerator over every possible target tuple:

$$
f_{\mathbf V}(\mathbf v)=\int\cdots\int f_{\mathbf U,\mathbf V}(\mathbf u,\mathbf v)\,d\mathbf u.
$$

Consequently, the conditional density integrates to one over $\mathbf u$. For continuous observed variables, this is the density-based extension of conditioning; it is not division by the zero point probability $P(\mathbf V=\mathbf v)$.

## A discrete example with explicit renormalization

Return to four patients with cure, reduction and no-change probabilities $0.3$, $0.5$, and $0.2$. Suppose we learn $Z=1$: exactly one patient had no change. Find the distribution of the remaining cure and reduced-pain counts.

The possible pairs satisfy $x+y=3$. By the conditional rule,

$$
P(X=x,Y=y\mid Z=1)=\frac{p(x,y,1)}{P(Z=1)}.
$$

Here

$$
P(Z=1)=\binom41(0.2)(0.8)^3.
$$

Substituting and cancelling common factors gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=x,Y=y\mid Z=1)
&=\frac{[4!/(x!y!)](0.3)^x(0.5)^y(0.2)}{4(0.2)(0.8)^3}\\
&=\frac{3!}{x!y!}\left(\frac{0.3}{0.8}\right)^x
\left(\frac{0.5}{0.8}\right)^y,
\qquad x+y=3.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, among the three improved patients, the normalized cure probability is $0.3/0.8=3/8$ and the reduction probability is $0.5/0.8=5/8$.

## Answer a concrete conditional question

The probability that exactly two of those three patients are cured is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X=2,Y=1\mid Z=1)
&=\binom32\left(\frac38\right)^2\left(\frac58\right)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{135}{512}}\approx0.263672.
\end{aligned}
$$

This also equals the original table probability $0.108$ divided by $P(Z=1)=0.4096$.

The general pattern is the same throughout the chapter: the joint distribution describes everything together, marginalization adds over omitted possibilities, and conditioning restricts and renormalizes the possibilities consistent with new information.
