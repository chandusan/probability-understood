# Horizontal slices: left to right, then bottom to top

A region is horizontally simple when each horizontal slice passes through one uninterrupted interval. Write it as $c\le y\le d$ and $a(y)\le x\le b(y)$, where $a(y)$ is the left boundary and $b(y)$ the right boundary.

## The corresponding integral

$$
\boxed{
\iint_A f(x,y)\,dx\,dy
=\int_c^d\int_{a(y)}^{b(y)}f(x,y)\,dx\,dy.
}
$$

At each fixed $y$, $dx$ takes us across the region from left to right. The outer $dy$ then includes every horizontal slice. This is a **Type II** description.

## Reverse the curved-region example

The region between $y=x^2$ and $y=x$ with $0\le x\le1$ can also be described using $y$. Rearrange the boundary equations:

$$
y=x\ \Rightarrow\ x=y,
\qquad
y=x^2\ \Rightarrow\ x=\sqrt y.
$$

Only the nonnegative square root is relevant because $x\ge0$. For $0\le y\le1$, we have $y\le\sqrt y$, giving

$$
A=\{(x,y):0\le y\le1,\ y\le x\le\sqrt y\}.
$$

Its area is therefore

$$
\int_0^1\int_y^{\sqrt y}1\,dx\,dy
=\int_0^1(\sqrt y-y)\,dy
=\frac23-\frac12=\frac16.
$$

The answer agrees with the vertical-slice calculation.

## Reversal is a geometric operation

We did more than exchange $dx$ and $dy$: we solved the boundary equations for the other coordinate and identified the correct left and right endpoints.

Some regions need several pieces in one order but only one in the other. Choose the order that makes the region and the integrand easiest to handle, while preserving every point of the event.
