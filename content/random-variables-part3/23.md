# A conditional density rescales a fixed slice

The conditional density of $Y$ given $X=x$ describes the distribution along the slice at that observed $x$. Divide the slice's joint density by its total weight so that the resulting one-variable density integrates to one.

## The formula and its denominator

For $f_X(x)>0$,

$$
\boxed{f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)=\frac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)}.}
$$

The denominator is precisely the integral of the numerator along the slice:

$$
\int f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,dy
=\frac{\int f(x,y)\,dy}{f_X(x)}=1.
$$

Reversing the roles gives $f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=f(x,y)/f_Y(y)$ wherever $f_Y(y)>0$.

## How can we condition on a zero-probability point?

For a continuous variable, $P(X=x)=0$, so we do not use the elementary ratio $P(Y\in B,X=x)/P(X=x)$ directly. Instead, picture conditioning on a very thin band $x<X<x+h$.

For a sufficiently regular density and small positive $h$,

$$
P(Y\in B\mid x<X<x+h)
\approx\frac{h\int_Bf(x,y)\,dy}{h f_X(x)}
=\int_B\frac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)}\,dy.
$$

The band width cancels. As the band narrows, this motivates the conditional-density formula. The formula defines a density along the slice, not a positive probability for the exact event $X=x$.

## A simple triangular example

Use the separate model $f(x,y)=2$ on $0<y<x<1$. Since $f_X(x)=2x$,

$$
f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)=\frac1x,\qquad 0<y<x.
$$

Given $X=x$, $Y$ is uniform on $(0,x)$. At $X=0.8$, the conditional density is $1.25$ on $(0,0.8)$, and

$$
P(Y<0.2\mid X=0.8)=\int_0^{0.2}1.25\,dy=0.25.
$$

A conditional density can exceed one; its area still equals one.

## Connection to prediction

In a regression problem, $X$ represents observed inputs and $Y$ the outcome to predict. The conditional distribution describes the remaining uncertainty about $Y$ at those inputs. Its mean, when it exists, is one possible point prediction; the full density also describes spread and tail probabilities.
