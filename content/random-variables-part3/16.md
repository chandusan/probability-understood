# Rebuild the triangle using vertical slices

To verify the volume under $x^2y+xy^3$ over $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$, $x+y\le1$, integrate $y$ first. At each fixed $x$, the vertical segment begins at $y=0$ and ends at $y=1-x$.

## Write the complete new setup

$$
I=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-x}(x^2y+xy^3)\,dy\,dx.
$$

The outer coordinate ranges from 0 to 1. The inner upper bound is the sloping line, solved for $y$.

## Apply the power rule with x fixed

$$
\int(x^2y+xy^3)\,dy
=\frac{x^2y^2}{2}+\frac{xy^4}{4}.
$$

At the upper endpoint $y=1-x$,

$$
\int_0^{1-x}(x^2y+xy^3)\,dy
=\frac{x^2(1-x)^2}{2}+\frac{x(1-x)^4}{4}.
$$

Both terms are nonnegative on $0\le x\le1$, so the cross-sectional area has the expected sign.

## Expand without skipping the combination

The first term is

$$
\frac{x^2}{2}-x^3+\frac{x^4}{2}.
$$

The second term is

$$
\frac x4-x^2+\frac{3x^3}{2}-x^4+\frac{x^5}{4}.
$$

Adding coefficients of the same powers gives

$$
\frac x4-\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^3}{2}-\frac{x^4}{2}+\frac{x^5}{4}
=\frac{x-2x^2+2x^3-2x^4+x^5}{4}.
$$

The next integration is therefore over this one-variable polynomial. The bounds came from geometry; the expansion only changes the form of the integrand, not the region being measured.
