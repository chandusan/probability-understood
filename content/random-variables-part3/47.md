# Solve the treatment event by two equivalent methods

For four patients, $X$ counts cures, $Y$ reduced pain, and $Z$ no change, with category probabilities $0.3$, $0.5$, and $0.2$. Find the probability that at least as many patients improve as remain unchanged: $P(X+Y\ge Z)$.

## Translate the event using the fixed total

Since $X+Y+Z=4$,

$$
X+Y\ge Z
\iff4-Z\ge Z
\iff Z\le2.
$$

Equality is included: two improved and two unchanged patients qualify.

## Method 1: combine the three categories into two

For each patient, “no change” has probability $0.2$, and “some improvement” has probability $0.3+0.5=0.8$. Therefore

$$
Z\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(4,0.2).
$$

The complement is especially short:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Z\le2)
&=1-P(Z=3)-P(Z=4)\\
&=1-\binom43(0.2)^3(0.8)-\binom44(0.2)^4\\
&=1-0.0256-0.0016\\
&=\boxed{0.9728}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Method 2: use the multinomial rows directly

The failing event $X+Y<Z$ contains exactly these triples:

$$
(1,0,3),\qquad(0,1,3),\qquad(0,0,4).
$$

Their probabilities are

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(1,0,3)&=4(0.3)(0.2)^3=0.0096,\\
p(0,1,3)&=4(0.5)(0.2)^3=0.0160,\\
p(0,0,4)&=(0.2)^4=0.0016.
\end{aligned}
$$

Add these disjoint cases and subtract from one:

$$
1-(0.0096+0.0160+0.0016)=\boxed{0.9728}.
$$

## Why the two methods agree

The binomial method groups complete cures and reduced pain into one category because the event only cares about their combined count. The two $Z=3$ multinomial cases combine to

$$
4(0.3+0.5)(0.2)^3=4(0.8)(0.2)^3.
$$

Combining categories saves arithmetic while preserving exactly the distinctions the question needs.
