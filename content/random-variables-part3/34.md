# Integrating a bivariate normal gives normal marginals

If $(X_1,X_2)$ is bivariate normal with parameters $\mu_1,\mu_2,\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\rho$, then each coordinate separately is normal: $X_i\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$. We can see why by completing a square before integrating out the other coordinate.

## Rewrite the exponent

With $z_i=(x_i-\mu_i)/\sigma_i$,

$$
z_1^2-2\rho z_1z_2+z_2^2
=(1-\rho^2)z_1^2+(z_2-\rho z_1)^2.
$$

Divide by $1-\rho^2$:

$$
Q=z_1^2+\frac{(z_2-\rho z_1)^2}{1-\rho^2}.
$$

The joint density becomes the product of two factors:

$$
\frac{e^{-z_1^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_1}
\cdot
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(z_2-\rho z_1)^2}{2(1-\rho^2)}\right].
$$

## Integrate over x₂ while holding x₁ fixed

The first factor is constant with respect to $x_2$. The second factor is a normal density in $x_2$: its centre is

$$
\mu_2+\rho\frac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}(x_1-\mu_1),
$$

and its standard deviation is $\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$. Therefore its integral over all $x_2$ equals one.

What remains is

$$
\boxed{f_{X_1}(x_1)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_1}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x_1-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2}\right].}
$$

Swapping the roles of the coordinates gives $X_2\sim N(\mu_2,\sigma_2^2)$.

## Interpret what disappeared

The correlation parameter affects which coordinate values occur together, but it does not alter the specified marginal means or standard deviations. Integrating out the other coordinate removes the information about pairing.

Be careful with notation: in $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, the second argument here is **variance**. The standard deviation is $\sigma$.
