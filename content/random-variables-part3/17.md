# Confirm the triangle's volume in the second order

After integrating vertically through the triangle, we obtained $I=\int_0^1(x-2x^2+2x^3-2x^4+x^5)/4\,dx$. We finish the calculation and compare it with the horizontal-slice result.

## Integrate each power

$$
\begin{aligned}
I
&=\left[\frac{x^2}{8}-\frac{x^3}{6}
+\frac{x^4}{8}-\frac{x^5}{10}+\frac{x^6}{24}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac18-\frac16+\frac18-\frac1{10}+\frac1{24}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The denominator 120 makes the arithmetic transparent:

$$
I=\frac{15-20+15-12+5}{120}
=\frac3{120}
=\boxed{\frac1{40}}.
$$

This matches the value from horizontal slicing.

## Why agreement is a useful check

The two calculations had different slice functions:

$$
S_y(y)=\frac{y(1-y)^3}{3}+\frac{y^3(1-y)^2}{2},
$$

and

$$
S_x(x)=\frac{x^2(1-x)^2}{2}+\frac{x(1-x)^4}{4}.
$$

They need not look alike. One measures horizontal cross-sections and the other vertical cross-sections. Integrating either across its full range measures the same volume.

## Turn the surface into a probability model

Because the unscaled volume is $1/40$, a valid density on this triangle would be

$$
f_{X,Y}(x,y)=40(x^2y+xy^3),\qquad x,y\ge0,\ x+y\le1,
$$

and zero elsewhere. Its integral is $40(1/40)=1$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Would the same multiplier 40 normalize this surface on the unit square?</summary>

No. The square integral is $7/24$, so its normalizing multiplier is $24/7$. Normalization depends on both the formula and the support.

</details>
