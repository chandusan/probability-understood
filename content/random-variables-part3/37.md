# Read the conditional mean and standard deviation from the square

The conditional normal density contains the term $(z_2-\rho z_1)^2/(1-\rho^2)$. Substitute the standardized coordinates back into this term to identify the ordinary normal form in $x_2$.

## Put the difference over a common scale

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_2-\rho z_1
&=\frac{x_2-\mu_2}{\sigma_2}
-\rho\frac{x_1-\mu_1}{\sigma_1}\\
&=\frac{x_2-\left[\mu_2+\rho(\sigma_2/\sigma_1)(x_1-\mu_1)\right]}{\sigma_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Define

$$
a=\mu_2+\rho\frac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}(x_1-\mu_1),
\qquad
b=\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}.
$$

Then

$$
\frac{(z_2-\rho z_1)^2}{1-\rho^2}
=\frac{(x_2-a)^2}{b^2}.
$$

## Recognize the normal density

The entire conditional density is now

$$
\boxed{
f_{X_2\mid X_1}(x_2\mid x_1)
=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\,b}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(x_2-a)^2}{2b^2}\right].
}
$$

This is precisely a normal density with mean $a$ and standard deviation $b$. Consequently,

$$
E[X_2\mid X_1=x_1]=\mu_2+\rho\frac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}(x_1-\mu_1),
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X_2\mid X_1=x_1)=\sigma_2^2(1-\rho^2).
$$

Here $E$ denotes the probability-weighted mean, and $\operatorname{Var}$ denotes variance.

## Reverse the roles carefully

If instead $X_2=x_2$ is given, swap indices throughout:

$$
X_1\mid X_2=x_2
\sim N\!\left(
\mu_1+\rho\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}(x_2-\mu_2),
\sigma_1^2(1-\rho^2)
\right).
$$

The given coordinate is centred using its own mean, and the target coordinate supplies the leading mean and variance. This avoids the index mismatches in the source slide.

## A final consistency check

At $\rho=0$, conditioning gives the original marginal normal distribution. As $|\rho|$ approaches one, the conditional spread approaches zero, reflecting an increasingly precise linear relationship.
