# Prove the normal conditional density with a consistent direction

We will derive the density of $X_2$ given $X_1=x_1$. The original proof slide mixes conditional subscripts and intermediate signs; keeping the target and the given variable fixed throughout makes the calculation precise.

## Begin with the general conditional rule

The target is $X_2$ and the given variable is $X_1$, so the denominator must be the marginal density of $X_1$:

$$
f_{X_2\mid X_1}(x_2\mid x_1)
=\frac{f(x_1,x_2)}{f_{X_1}(x_1)}.
$$

Write $z_i=(x_i-\mu_i)/\sigma_i$. Then

$$
f(x_1,x_2)=
\frac{e^{-Q/2}}{2\pi\sigma_1\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}},
\qquad
f_{X_1}(x_1)=\frac{e^{-z_1^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_1}.
$$

## Divide the constants and the exponentials separately

The constants simplify to

$$
\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_1}{2\pi\sigma_1\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}
=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}.
$$

For the exponentials, dividing subtracts the denominator's exponent:

$$
\frac{e^{-Q/2}}{e^{-z_1^2/2}}
=e^{-Q/2+z_1^2/2}.
$$

The plus sign before $z_1^2/2$ matters: subtracting a negative exponent gives a positive contribution.

## Complete the square

From the definition of $Q$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q-z_1^2
&=\frac{z_1^2-2\rho z_1z_2+z_2^2-(1-\rho^2)z_1^2}{1-\rho^2}\\
&=\frac{z_2^2-2\rho z_1z_2+\rho^2z_1^2}{1-\rho^2}\\
&=\frac{(z_2-\rho z_1)^2}{1-\rho^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
f_{X_2\mid X_1}(x_2\mid x_1)
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_2\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(z_2-\rho z_1)^2}{2(1-\rho^2)}\right].
}
$$

This is already a normalized density in $x_2$. The remaining task is to rewrite the square in original units so that its mean and standard deviation can be read directly.
