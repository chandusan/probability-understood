# State all four questions for a joint density

Let $f(x,y)=\frac65(x+y^2)$ for $0<x<1$, $0<y<1$, and zero elsewhere. We want to verify the density, find $P(X<1/4,Y<1/4)$, derive both marginals, and calculate $P(1/4<Y<3/4)$.

## Identify the support and the four operations

The support is the unit square. Because $x+y^2\ge0$ there, nonnegativity is immediate. The remaining validity check is total integral one.

Each question corresponds to a different region or operation:

| Question | Operation |
|---|---|
| Is this a density? | Integrate over the full square |
| Are both coordinates below $1/4$? | Integrate over the lower-left small square |
| What is the density of $X$? | Integrate $y$ from 0 to 1 |
| What is the density of $Y$? | Integrate $x$ from 0 to 1 |
| Is $Y$ between $1/4$ and $3/4$? | Integrate its marginal over that interval |

## Solve the joint probability explicitly

The event $X<1/4$, $Y<1/4$ means both inequalities hold. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X<1/4,Y<1/4)
&=\frac65\int_0^{1/4}\int_0^{1/4}(x+y^2)\,dx\,dy\\
&=\frac65\int_0^{1/4}\left(\frac1{32}+\frac{y^2}{4}\right)dy\\
&=\frac65\left[\frac y{32}+\frac{y^3}{12}\right]_0^{1/4}\\
&=\frac65\left(\frac1{128}+\frac1{768}\right)\\
&=\boxed{\frac7{640}}=0.0109375.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the inner step, $x^2/2$ becomes $1/32$, and $xy^2$ becomes $y^2/4$.

## Why area alone would be wrong

The small square has area $1/16$, but the probability is not $1/16$. The density is relatively low near the origin and rises as either $x$ or $y$ grows. Equal-area regions therefore need not have equal probability.
