# A triangular density: support controls every answer

Let $f(x,y)=cx+1$ for $x>0$, $y>0$, $x+y<1$, and zero elsewhere. Find the constant $c$, both marginal densities, and both conditional densities. The triangular support is as important as the formula.

## Normalize over the triangle

Vertical slices have $0<x<1$ and $0<y<1-x$. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
1
&=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-x}(cx+1)\,dy\,dx\\
&=\int_0^1(cx+1)(1-x)\,dx\\
&=c\left(\frac12-\frac13\right)+1-\frac12
=\frac c6+\frac12.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore $c=3$, and $3x+1$ is positive everywhere on the support.

## Marginal of X

At fixed $x$, the allowed $y$ interval has length $1-x$:

$$
\boxed{f_X(x)=(3x+1)(1-x),\quad 0<x<1.}
$$

## Marginal of Y

At fixed $y$, $x$ ranges from 0 to $1-y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_Y(y)
&=\int_0^{1-y}(3x+1)\,dx\\
&=\frac32(1-y)^2+(1-y)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{(1-y)(5-3y)}2,\quad 0<y<1.}
\end{aligned}
$$

Both marginals are zero outside their indicated intervals.

## Conditional density of Y given X

Divide by $f_X(x)$, with $0<x<1$:

$$
\boxed{
f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)=\frac1{1-x},\quad 0<y<1-x.
}
$$

Along each vertical slice, the original density is constant in $y$, so the conditional distribution is uniform. Its permitted interval shrinks as $x$ grows.

## Conditional density of X given Y

For $0<y<1$,

$$
\boxed{
f_{X\mid Y}(x\mid y)=\frac{2(3x+1)}{(1-y)(5-3y)},\quad 0<x<1-y.
}
$$

This distribution increases with $x$ within its permitted interval. Both conditional densities are zero outside their conditional support.

## Why these variables are dependent

Given a large $X$, $Y$ must be small because $X+Y<1$. The formula's lack of an explicit $y$ term does not mean independence: the permitted range of $y$ still depends on $x$.
