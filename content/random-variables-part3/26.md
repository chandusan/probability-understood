# Derive both marginals and the interval probability

For the joint density $f(x,y)=\frac65(x+y^2)$ on $0<x<1$, $0<y<1$, find the marginal distributions of $X$ and $Y$. Then use the marginal of $Y$ to calculate $P(1/4<Y<3/4)$.

## Marginal of X: add all possible y values

For a fixed $0<x<1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_X(x)
&=\int_0^1\frac65(x+y^2)\,dy\\
&=\frac65\left[xy+\frac{y^3}{3}\right]_0^1\\
&=\boxed{\frac65\left(x+\frac13\right)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

It is zero outside $(0,1)$. The result increases linearly because the joint surface increases linearly with $x$.

## Marginal of Y: add all possible x values

For a fixed $0<y<1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_Y(y)
&=\int_0^1\frac65(x+y^2)\,dx\\
&=\frac65\left[\frac{x^2}{2}+xy^2\right]_0^1\\
&=\boxed{\frac65\left(\frac12+y^2\right)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

It too is zero outside $(0,1)$.

## Solve the remaining interval question

The event $1/4<Y<3/4$ places no further restriction on $X$, so its probability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(1/4<Y<3/4)
&=\int_{1/4}^{3/4}f_Y(y)\,dy\\
&=\frac65\left[\frac y2+\frac{y^3}{3}\right]_{1/4}^{3/4}\\
&=\frac65\left(\frac14+\frac{26}{192}\right)\\
&=\frac65\frac{37}{96}
=\boxed{\frac{37}{80}}=0.4625.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Check the interpretation

Integrating a marginal over its entire support gives one. Integrating it over a shorter interval gives the probability of that interval. These are ordinary one-variable density calculations after the other coordinate has been integrated out.

The marginals do not automatically imply independence. For example, at $x=y=1/2$, the joint density is $0.9$, whereas the product $f_X(1/2)f_Y(1/2)$ is also $0.9$; agreement at one point proves nothing. At $x=y=1/4$, the joint value is $3/8$, but the product of marginal values is $189/400$, so factorization fails and these variables are dependent.
