# Model four treatment outcomes with three counts

A hypothetical treatment has three outcomes for each patient: complete cure with probability $0.30$, reduced pain with probability $0.50$, and no change with probability $0.20$. Apply it to four patients, assuming their outcomes are independent with these same probabilities. Let $X,Y,Z$ count those three outcomes respectively.

## State the full joint distribution

There are four trials and three categories, so

$$
X+Y+Z=4.
$$

For nonnegative integer counts satisfying that constraint,

$$
\boxed{
P(X=x,Y=y,Z=z)
=\frac{4!}{x!y!z!}(0.3)^x(0.5)^y(0.2)^z.
}
$$

The probability is zero for other triples.

The independence of different patients is a modeling assumption here. The probabilities on the slide are inputs to this mathematical example, not a claim about a particular real treatment.

## Work one exact outcome composition

Find the probability of one cure, two reductions and one unchanged patient:

$$
P(X=1,Y=2,Z=1)
=\frac{4!}{1!2!1!}(0.3)(0.5)^2(0.2).
$$

Choose the cured patient in 4 ways, then choose the two reduced-pain patients from the remaining three in $\binom32=3$ ways. The last patient is unchanged. There are therefore $12$ qualifying sequences.

Each sequence has probability

$$
0.3\cdot0.5^2\cdot0.2=0.015.
$$

Multiplying gives

$$
\boxed{P(X=1,Y=2,Z=1)=12(0.015)=0.18.}
$$

## Translate the requested event

The slide also asks for $P(X+Y\ge Z)$. The left side counts patients whose pain improved; the right side counts unchanged patients. Since $X+Y=4-Z$,

$$
X+Y\ge Z
\iff4-Z\ge Z
\iff Z\le2.
$$

We can solve this by adding qualifying multinomial table entries or by treating “no change” as a binomial category. Both methods should give the same answer.
