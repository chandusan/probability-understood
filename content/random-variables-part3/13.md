# Set up the polynomial surface over a triangle

Find the volume beneath $f(x,y)=x^2y+xy^3$ over $A=\{(x,y):x+y\le1,\ x\ge0,\ y\ge0\}$. This is the same surface used over the unit square, but now only the triangular portion below the line $x+y=1$ is included.

## State the geometry before the algebra

The region has vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,1)$. Its boundary equations are

$$
x=0,\qquad y=0,\qquad x+y=1.
$$

The function is nonnegative throughout this region, so the integral has the interpretation of ordinary volume.

## Describe horizontal slices

Choose $y$ between 0 and 1. The slice begins at the vertical axis $x=0$ and ends at the sloping line $x=1-y$:

$$
I=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-y}(x^2y+xy^3)\,dx\,dy.
$$

## Describe vertical slices

Choose $x$ between 0 and 1. The slice begins at the horizontal axis $y=0$ and ends at $y=1-x$:

$$
I=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-x}(x^2y+xy^3)\,dy\,dx.
$$

Both are valid descriptions of the same volume.

## Predict something about the answer

The triangular region is contained within the unit square. Because the integrand is nonnegative, removing the rest of the square can only reduce the integral. Thus

$$
0<I<\frac7{24}.
$$

The inequality is strict because the removed region has positive area and positive height except on boundaries.

This check will not find every arithmetic error, but it immediately rules out a negative answer or an answer larger than the full-square integral.
