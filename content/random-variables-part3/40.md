# The requirements for any multivariate density

A function $f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is a joint density for continuous random variables $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ when it is nonnegative, integrates to one over the full space, and produces event probabilities by integration over the corresponding regions.

## State the rules precisely

First,

$$
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\ge0.
$$

Second,

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,dx_1\cdots dx_n=1.
$$

Third, for any event region $A$,

$$
P((X_1,\ldots,X_n)\in A)
=\int\cdots\int_Af(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\,dx_1\cdots dx_n.
$$

As before, a formula must be accompanied by its support. Outside that support, the density is zero.

## A three-dimensional example

Suppose $f(x,y,z)=1$ on the unit cube $0<x,y,z<1$ and zero elsewhere. The total integral is the cube's volume, one.

The event $X<1/2$, $Y<1/3$, $Z<1/4$ is a smaller box with probability

$$
\int_0^{1/2}\int_0^{1/3}\int_0^{1/4}1\,dz\,dy\,dx
=\frac12\frac13\frac14=\frac1{24}.
$$

The factors here come from the constant density and rectangular support. They are not a license to multiply arbitrary marginal probabilities in dependent models.

## The discrete version uses sums

For discrete variables, replace density by a probability mass function $p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ and replace integrals by sums:

$$
p\ge0,\qquad \sum_{x_1}\cdots\sum_{x_n}p(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=1.
$$

To find an event probability, add the masses of all qualifying tuples.

The distinction between continuous and discrete models changes how we add contributions. It does not change the logic of support, total probability, marginalization, or conditioning.
