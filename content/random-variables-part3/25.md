# Verify the square density integrates to one

For $f(x,y)=\frac65(x+y^2)$ on the unit square, a legitimate density needs nonnegative values and total integral one. We check both conditions explicitly rather than assuming the factor $6/5$ is correct.

## Nonnegativity

On $0<x<1$, $0<y<1$, both $x$ and $y^2$ are nonnegative. Hence

$$
\frac65(x+y^2)\ge0.
$$

Outside the square the density is defined to be zero, which is also nonnegative.

## Total probability

Integrate first with respect to $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\int_0^1\frac65(x+y^2)\,dx\,dy
&=\frac65\int_0^1\left[\frac{x^2}{2}+xy^2\right]_0^1dy\\
&=\frac65\int_0^1\left(\frac12+y^2\right)dy\\
&=\frac65\left[\frac y2+\frac{y^3}{3}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac65\left(\frac12+\frac13\right)\\
&=\frac65\frac56=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both requirements hold, so this is a valid joint density.

## Where did the factor 6/5 come from?

If the model had been written as $c(x+y^2)$, the unscaled integral would be $5/6$. Normalization would require

$$
c\frac56=1,
\qquad c=\frac65.
$$

The constant compensates for the unscaled surface's volume. It does not change which areas are relatively more or less likely.

## A density height is not a probability

Near $(1,1)$, this density approaches $12/5=2.4$, exceeding one. That is allowed. A sufficiently small patch of area $\Delta A$ near that corner receives probability approximately $2.4\Delta A$.

The constraints apply to integrated probabilities. No single point receives probability equal to its surface height, and no positive-area region can have probability exceeding the total integral one.
