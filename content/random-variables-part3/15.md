# Finish the triangular integral explicitly

After integrating $x^2y+xy^3$ horizontally over $0\le x\le1-y$, the remaining integral is $I=\int_0^1\{y(1-y)^3/3+y^3(1-y)^2/2\}\,dy$. We now expand each piece and evaluate it carefully.

## First contribution

Use $(1-y)^3=1-3y+3y^2-y^3$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1
&=\frac13\int_0^1(y-3y^2+3y^3-y^4)\,dy\\
&=\frac13\left[\frac{y^2}{2}-y^3+\frac{3y^4}{4}-\frac{y^5}{5}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac13\left(\frac12-1+\frac34-\frac15\right)
=\frac1{60}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Second contribution

Use $(1-y)^2=1-2y+y^2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_2
&=\frac12\int_0^1(y^3-2y^4+y^5)\,dy\\
&=\frac12\left[\frac{y^4}{4}-\frac{2y^5}{5}+\frac{y^6}{6}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac12\left(\frac14-\frac25+\frac16\right)
=\frac1{120}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Add the volumes

$$
\boxed{I=I_1+I_2=\frac1{60}+\frac1{120}=\frac1{40}.}
$$

The answer is positive and below $7/24$, the volume under the same surface over the full unit square.

## Why not take half the square's answer?

The triangle occupies half the square's area, but the height is not constant. The omitted portion contains many points with larger coordinates, where $x^2y+xy^3$ is larger. Half the base area therefore need not contain half the volume.

An area ratio directly gives a probability only when the joint density is constant over the full support. With a varying density, location matters as well as area.
