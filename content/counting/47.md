# A triple becomes a full house only when the kickers pair

Start with three cards of one rank. The remaining two cards determine which category you have: two different ranks give exactly three of a kind; two cards of the same other rank give a full house. The source places these two constructions together, making their difference especially clear.

## Exactly three of a kind

For pattern $\{x,x,x,y,z\}$, the ranks x, y, and z are all different.

| Decision | Number of choices |
|---|---:|
| Choose the triple's rank x | 13 |
| Choose three suits at that rank | $\binom43=4$ |
| Choose the two distinct kicker ranks | $\binom{12}{2}=66$ |
| Choose one suit at each kicker rank | $4^2=16$ |

Thus

$$
N_{\text{three of a kind}}
=13\binom43\binom{12}{2}4^2
=13\cdot4\cdot66\cdot16
=54{,}912.
$$

The two kicker ranks are selected as an unordered pair because they share the same role: each appears once. Requiring them to differ keeps full houses out of this category.

The probability is $54{,}912/2{,}598{,}960\approx0.02112845$.

## A full house assigns two different rank roles

For pattern $\{x,x,x,y,y\}$, first choose the triple's rank x in 13 ways, then the pair's different rank y in 12 ways. These are different roles, so the rank count is

$$
13\cdot12=156,
$$

also written ${}_{13}P_2$. Do not divide by two: three aces plus two sevens differs from three sevens plus two aces.

Choose the suits of the triple in $\binom43=4$ ways and those of the pair in $\binom42=6$ ways. Therefore

$$
N_{\text{full house}}
=13\cdot12\binom43\binom42
=156\cdot4\cdot6
=3{,}744.
$$

The probability is

$$
P(\text{full house})=\frac{3{,}744}{2{,}598{,}960}
\approx0.00144058,
$$

or about $0.144058\%$.

## Correct the source's arithmetic

The slide correctly lists the factors 156, 4, and 6, but prints their product as 3,696. Their actual product is **3,744**. The summary later repeats the smaller count and its corresponding incorrect probability.

This is an arithmetic error, not a different category convention: the rank-and-suit construction specifies exactly the standard five-card full-house category. A separate source error adds 48 too many high-card hands; the two errors happen to cancel in the source table's grand total.

## Check your understanding

Count hands consisting of three aces and two kings. Then count full houses using only the ranks aces and kings, without specifying which is the triple.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>First fix the roles, then allow them to swap</summary>

For three aces and two kings, both rank roles are fixed. Only suits remain:

$$
\binom43\binom42=4\cdot6=24.
$$

If the two allowed ranks are aces and kings but their roles are not fixed, there are two rank assignments: aces as triple or kings as triple. The count becomes $2\cdot24=48$. This explains why the general full-house rank factor is $\binom{13}{2}\cdot2=13\cdot12$.

</details>
