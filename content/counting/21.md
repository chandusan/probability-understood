# Assign three different jobs

Choosing an executive team is more than choosing three names when the jobs are different. Alice as chairperson and Bob as treasurer is a different outcome from Bob as chairperson and Alice as treasurer, even if the team contains the same people.

## Let the positions tell you what matters

A committee has ten people. We must select a chairperson, a vice-chairperson, and a treasurer, with no person holding more than one of these jobs.

Write an outcome as an ordered triple:

$$
(\text{chairperson},\text{vice-chairperson},\text{treasurer}).
$$

There are ten choices for chairperson. After that, nine people remain for vice-chairperson. After those two assignments, eight remain for treasurer. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(10,3)=\frac{10!}{7!}=10\times9\times8=720.}
$$

We are not multiplying probabilities here. We are counting the leaves of a three-stage decision tree. Every first branch has nine second branches, and every second branch has eight third branches.

## An alternative route checks the answer

First select an unordered group of three people. There are $\binom{10}{3}=120$ such groups. Then assign the three jobs within the selected group in $3!=6$ ways:

$$
120\times6=720.
$$

This second route makes the role of order visible. The 120 groups identify who serves; the factor 6 identifies who does which job. If all three people had the same job title and the problem only asked for a three-person subcommittee, 120 would be the relevant answer.

## Assumptions belong to the count

We have assumed that every committee member is eligible for every role and that the roles must be held by distinct people. Restricting eligibility can change the branch counts. Allowing one person to hold multiple roles would instead produce $10^3$ assignments.

No randomness is needed to count 720 possibilities. To turn counts into probabilities later, we will additionally need a selection model.

## Check your understanding

If the chairperson has already been appointed, how many ways can the other two jobs be assigned from the remaining people?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the answer</summary>

There are nine choices for vice-chairperson and eight for treasurer:

$$
9\times8=72.
$$

The original 720 assignments split into ten equally sized groups, one for each possible chairperson, each containing 72 assignments.

</details>
