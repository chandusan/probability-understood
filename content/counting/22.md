# Count a pipeline with two constraints

Constraints often become manageable when you handle the easiest one first and remove the small set of remaining violations. Here, fixing the first feature makes the forbidden pair easy to spot.

## State exactly what a pipeline means

There are seven candidate features $A,B,C,D,E,F,G$. An outcome is an **ordered three-stage pipeline**. The first feature must be $A$ or $B$, and $C$ and $D$ cannot both appear.

The slide does not explicitly say whether a feature may be reused. In its permutations-without-replacement context, the natural interpretation is that the three selected features are distinct. We use that assumption for the main solution.

## Start with the first-stage requirement

Choose the first feature in two ways: $A$ or $B$. For either choice, six features remain for stage 2 and five for stage 3. Ignoring the $C,D$ restriction for a moment gives

$$
2\times6\times5=60.
$$

This counts every pipeline satisfying the first requirement, including the invalid ones.

## Subtract precisely the forbidden pipelines

Since stage 1 is $A$ or $B$, a pipeline contains both $C$ and $D$ only when stages 2 and 3 are $(C,D)$ or $(D,C)$. The complete invalid list is

$$
(A,C,D),\ (A,D,C),\ (B,C,D),\ (B,D,C).
$$

There are four invalid pipelines, hence

$$
\boxed{60-4=2(6\times5-2)=56.}
$$

Notice that the restriction says “not both.” A pipeline containing $C$ alone is allowed, as is one containing $D$ alone or neither.

## Explore the restrictions

Predict how the count changes when the forbidden pair is allowed. Then compare pipelines with no member of the pair, exactly one member, and both.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Verify 56 by splitting into disjoint cases</summary>

Fix stage 1, say to $A$. Besides $C,D$, the remaining ordinary choices are $B,E,F,G$.

If neither $C$ nor $D$ is used, there are $4\times3=12$ ordered pairs for the remaining stages. If exactly one is used, choose which one in 2 ways, its stage in 2 ways, and the other feature in 4 ways: $2\times2\times4=16$.

The cases cannot overlap, so add them: $12+16=28$. Either $A$ or $B$ may be first, giving $2\times28=56$.

</details>

## What if reusing features were allowed?

With replacement, each later stage would have seven choices, including the feature already chosen. The preliminary count would be $2\times7^2=98$. The same four pipelines contain both $C$ and $D$, so the answer would be

$$
98-4=94.
$$

This is why the replacement assumption matters: 56 and 94 answer different questions.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the count</summary>

The remaining feature cannot be $A$, $C$, or $D$, so choose it from $B,E,F,G$ in four ways. Place $C$ in stage 2 or 3 in two ways:

$$
4\times2=8.
$$

For example, $(A,C,B)$ and $(A,B,C)$ are separate pipelines.
