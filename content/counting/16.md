# Read the plate format before counting it

A plate is a sequence of character positions. The stated answer follows when two fixed positions contain letters and five fixed positions contain digits, with repetition allowed. The pattern and the repetition rules are part of the problem, not details we can leave implicit.

## The model behind the source answer

Treat the slide as a hypothetical format exercise, such as

$$\text{LLDDDDD},$$

where each $\text L$ is one of $26$ letters and each $\text D$ is one of the ten digits $0,1,\ldots,9$. Assume repeated characters are permitted, a digit position may contain zero even when it is the first digit, and a fixed separator does not create extra choices.

For example, a plate like $\text{AA00001}$ is allowed under this theoretical model. No claim about current Connecticut registration policy is needed; the slide explicitly asks for a theoretical count.

## Fill the positions one by one

The seven stages have counts

$$26,26,10,10,10,10,10.$$

Every partial string has the same number of choices for the next position. Therefore

$$\boxed{26^2\cdot10^5=676\cdot100{,}000=67{,}600{,}000.}$$

The five “numbers” in the source wording are interpreted as five single digits. Allowing arbitrary multi-digit numbers in each position would describe a different experiment.

## What if the letter positions are not fixed?

The phrase “exactly two letters and five digits” by itself could allow the letters anywhere in the seven positions. First choose the two letter locations. There are

$$\binom72=21$$

ways: choose two distinct positions, ignoring their selection order. Then fill the selected letter slots and remaining digit slots as before:

$$21\cdot26^2\cdot10^5=1{,}419{,}600{,}000.$$

The source's smaller answer therefore encodes a fixed-position assumption. For instance, $\text{AB12345}$ and $\text{1A2B345}$ would both qualify under free positions, but only the first fits the illustrative pattern $\text{LLDDDDD}$.

## Change one rule and follow its effect

If only the first digit is forbidden from being zero, its count becomes nine and the others stay ten, giving $26^2\cdot9\cdot10^4=60{,}840{,}000$ for the fixed pattern.

If letters must differ, the letter factor becomes $26\cdot25$. If all five digits must differ, the digit factor becomes $10\cdot9\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6$. Counting is sensitive to exactly which repetitions are forbidden.

## Check your understanding

For the fixed pattern of two letters followed by five digits, assume the letters must be different and all five digits must be different, but a leading zero is allowed. How many plates are possible?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Work through the answer</summary>

Choose letters in $26\cdot25=650$ ways. Choose the five ordered distinct digits in

$$10\cdot9\cdot8\cdot7\cdot6=30{,}240$$

ways. Their product is

$$\boxed{650\cdot30{,}240=19{,}656{,}000.}$$

There is no position-selection factor because the letter and digit locations are fixed. There is no factor of nine at the first digit because zero is allowed.
