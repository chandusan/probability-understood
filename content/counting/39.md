# Two, three, and four matches follow one construction

Rather than inventing a new method for every prize category, use the same two-pool recipe. Choose the required number from the six winning numbers, then fill the rest of the ticket from the 43 nonwinning numbers. The word “exactly” controls both choices.

## Two matches

Select two of the six winning numbers and four of the 43 others:

$$
N_2=\binom62\binom{43}{4}
=15\cdot123{,}410
=1{,}851{,}150.
$$

For instance, a winning set $\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ and a ticket $\{2,5,8,17,36,49\}$ share exactly two numbers. Each ticket has a unique winning pair and a unique nonwinning group of four.

Its category probability is

$$
P(K=2)=\frac{1{,}851{,}150}{13{,}983{,}816}
\approx0.1323780290.
$$

## Three matches

Now choose three numbers from each pool:

$$
N_3=\binom63\binom{43}{3}
=20\cdot12{,}341
=246{,}820,
$$

$$
P(K=3)=\frac{246{,}820}{13{,}983{,}816}
\approx0.0176504039.
$$

This is about $1.7650\%$. Under the slide's illustrative schedule, this exact category receives the listed \$10 prize.

## Four matches

Four matches leave only two nonwinning slots:

$$
N_4=\binom64\binom{43}{2}
=15\cdot903
=13{,}545,
$$

$$
P(K=4)=\frac{13{,}545}{13{,}983{,}816}
\approx0.0009686197.
$$

That is about $0.096862\%$, already much rarer than three matches. Although $\binom62=\binom64=15$, the counts of ways to fill the rest of the ticket are very different: $\binom{43}{4}$ versus $\binom{43}{2}$.

## From an exact category to a range of categories

The source's simplified prize event is at least three matches. Including the five-match and six-match counts developed next, its count is

$$
246{,}820+13{,}545+258+1=260{,}624.
$$

Consequently,

$$
P(K\ge3)=\frac{260{,}624}{13{,}983{,}816}
\approx0.0186375450,
$$

or about $1.8638\%$. An “at least” question adds disjoint exact categories; it does not change the construction within any one category.

## Check your understanding

Someone counts exactly three matches using $\binom63\binom{46}{3}$. What does the second pool incorrectly allow?

<summary>Keep all unselected winning numbers out</summary>

After selecting three winning numbers, there are 46 other numbers overall. But three of those are the remaining winning numbers. Choosing from all 46 would admit tickets with four, five, or six matches, and some such tickets would arise from multiple initial winning triples. For exactly three matches, all three remaining ticket numbers must come from the 43 nonwinning numbers. The correct count is $\binom63\binom{43}{3}=246{,}820$.
