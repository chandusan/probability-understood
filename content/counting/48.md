# Four of a kind and the two suited-straight categories

Some rare hands leave very few choices after their structure is fixed. Four of a kind forces all four suits of its repeated rank. A royal flush forces all five ranks. Counting becomes easier when you notice which choices have already been determined.

## Four of a kind

For pattern $\{x,x,x,x,y\}$, choose the repeated rank x in 13 ways. All four of its suits must appear, so there is just

$$
\binom44=1
$$

way to choose that group of cards.

The kicker rank y must differ from x, giving 12 choices, followed by four choices for its suit. Thus

$$
N_{\text{four of a kind}}
=13\cdot1\cdot12\cdot4
=624.
$$

Equivalently, after the four cards of rank x are fixed, any of the other 48 cards can be the kicker: $13\cdot48=624$.

The source prints $\binom{12}{1}=1$ on the kicker-rank line. That is a typographical error: $\binom{12}{1}=12$. Its final product correctly uses 12 and gives 624.

## Royal flush

A royal flush has ranks $10,J,Q,K,A$, all in one suit. The rank set is fixed. Choose hearts, diamonds, clubs, or spades, giving

$$
N_{\text{royal flush}}=4.
$$

There are no additional suit choices per card once the common suit is chosen. No ordering factor belongs here either.

## Nonroyal straight flush

There are ten consecutive rank patterns in total, including the ace-low pattern $A,2,3,4,5$ and the ace-high pattern $10,J,Q,K,A$. Excluding the royal pattern leaves nine.

The nine nonroyal starting ranks can be listed as

$$
A,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9.
$$

For each pattern, choose one common suit:

$$
N_{\text{nonroyal straight flush}}=9\cdot4=36.
$$

The two suited-straight categories together contain $36+4=40$ hands. If someone uses “straight flush” to include royal flushes, their count should be 40. Our summary separates the two categories, so its straight-flush row is 36.

All four queens are fixed. A heart kicker may be any heart except the queen of hearts, which is already in the hand. That leaves 12 choices.

For a straight flush in hearts, the suit is fixed and the ten permissible rank patterns remain. Thus there are ten: nine nonroyal straight flushes and one royal flush. The ace-low pattern counts; a wraparound pattern such as $Q,K,A,2,3$ does not.
