# Zero matches means all six choices come from outside

Matching nothing sounds like an absence, but it is still a concrete event we can count. Once the six winning numbers are fixed, 43 of the 49 numbers are outside that winning set. A zero-match ticket takes all six of its numbers from those 43.

## Count the event and the full space

The favorable count for zero matches is

$$
N_0=\binom60\binom{43}{6}
=1\cdot6{,}096{,}454
=6{,}096{,}454.
$$

The factor $\binom60=1$ says there is one way to choose no winning numbers: choose the empty set. It does not mean the whole ticket has no numbers; the other factor supplies all six.

Divide by the total number of equally likely six-number sets:

$$
P(K=0)=\frac{6{,}096{,}454}{13{,}983{,}816}
\approx0.4359649755.
$$

That is about $43.5965\%$. The slide's displayed approximation, $0.43596$, is correctly rounded to five decimal places.

## A sequential view gives the same answer

Imagine drawing the six ticket numbers one by one without replacement. To avoid the winning set on every draw, the conditional chances are

$$
\frac{43}{49},\quad\frac{42}{48},\quad\frac{41}{47},
\quad\frac{40}{46},\quad\frac{39}{45},\quad\frac{38}{44}.
$$

Multiplying gives

$$
P(K=0)=
\frac{43\cdot42\cdot41\cdot40\cdot39\cdot38}
{49\cdot48\cdot47\cdot46\cdot45\cdot44}
=\frac{\binom{43}{6}}{\binom{49}{6}}.
$$

The draws are not independent. After a losing number is selected, both the available losing count and total count decrease. The product works because each factor is the probability of the next successful avoidance **given** the earlier ones.

## Use the complement when it saves work

“At least one match” is the complement of zero matches:

$$
P(K\ge1)=1-P(K=0)\approx0.5640350245.
$$

This is easier than calculating and adding all six positive-match categories. But it is not the probability of a listed prize: under the slide's example, one and two matches still do not pay.

## Check your understanding

Why isn't the zero-match probability $(43/49)^6$? What experiment would that expression describe?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Look for replacement</summary>

The expression $(43/49)^6$ keeps the same pool for every draw. It describes six independent draws with replacement, where a previously drawn number is returned and can appear again. A valid six-number lottery ticket has no repetitions. Without replacement, the correct product updates the pools after each draw, or equivalently uses $\binom{43}{6}/\binom{49}{6}$.
