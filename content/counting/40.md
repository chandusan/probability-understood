# Five matches, six matches, and a bonus-number ambiguity

Near the jackpot, very few ticket choices remain. For five main-number matches, choose which one of the six main winners is missing and which outside number replaces it. For six matches, there is only one possible ticket: the winning set itself.

## Five matches in the main-number-only model

Choose five of the six main winners and one of the 43 other numbers:

$$
N_5=\binom65\binom{43}{1}=6\cdot43=258.
$$

Thus

$$
P(K=5)=\frac{258}{13{,}983{,}816}
\approx0.00001844990,
$$

or about $0.001845\%$. The six choices for the matched group can also be viewed as six choices for the main winning number that is absent.

## Six matches

To match all six, take the entire winning set and no outside numbers:

$$
N_6=\binom66\binom{43}{0}=1\cdot1=1.
$$

Therefore

$$
P(K=6)=\frac1{13{,}983{,}816}
\approx0.00000007151124.
$$

There is no factor $6!$: rearranging the six winning numbers does not create a different ticket.

## Resolve the slide's bonus-number wording

The source says the outside number is chosen “excluding the bonus number,” but then uses 43 choices. Those statements do not fit the same model if a separate bonus number has been selected from the 43 numbers outside the main winning set.

Here are the consistent alternatives:

| Event, given six main winners and one distinct bonus number | Count |
|---|---:|
| Exactly five main matches, regardless of the bonus | $\binom65\cdot43=258$ |
| Exactly five main matches, and the sixth ticket number is the bonus | $\binom65\cdot1=6$ |
| Exactly five main matches, and the sixth ticket number is not the bonus | $\binom65\cdot42=252$ |

The last two cases are disjoint and add to 258. Thus the source's **258 is correct for the main-six-only event**. If it truly intends to exclude a distinct bonus number, the count must be **252**. We keep these events separate instead of mixing their descriptions and counts.

This is a clarification of the slide's counting model, not a statement about current commercial lottery prize rules.

There are 258 tickets with exactly five main matches, and six of them contain the bonus. Their fraction is

$$
\frac6{258}=\frac1{43}.
$$

Once the five main winners are chosen, the remaining number ranges over 43 outside possibilities, exactly one of which is the bonus. The unconditional probability of the five-plus-bonus event is $6/13{,}983{,}816$; the fraction $1/43$ is conditional on already being in the exactly-five-main-matches group.
