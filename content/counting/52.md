# Ten coin tosses: choose the positions of the heads

The tosses happen in order, so an outcome is a length-ten sequence such as HHTTHTTTTT. To count sequences with exactly three heads, choose which three of the ten positions contain H. Once those positions are fixed, every other position must contain T.

## Set up the equally likely sample space

Assume the ten tosses are fair and independent. Each position has two outcomes, giving

$$
|S|=2^{10}=1{,}024
$$

possible sequences. Each particular sequence has probability $(1/2)^{10}=1/1{,}024$.

Fairness concerns each individual toss; independence justifies multiplying the ten probabilities. A process in which all tosses copied one fair initial result would have fair individual marginals but would not give the 1,024 sequences equal probabilities.

## Part a: exactly three heads

Choose three head positions from the ten:

$$
\binom{10}{3}
=\frac{10\cdot9\cdot8}{3\cdot2\cdot1}
=120.
$$

For example, choosing positions $\{1,2,5\}$ produces HHTTHTTTTT. Every sequence with three heads identifies exactly one such position set.

Therefore

$$
P(H=3)=\frac{\binom{10}{3}}{2^{10}}
=\frac{120}{1{,}024}
=\frac{15}{128}
=0.1171875.
$$

Here H denotes the total number of heads, rather than a particular single-toss outcome.

There is no additional factor of $3!$ for arranging the heads or $7!$ for arranging the tails. The three H symbols are identical, as are the seven T symbols. Their positions already specify the entire sequence. Equivalently, the count is $10!/(3!7!)$.

## Part b: three or fewer heads

This event includes the four disjoint exact-count cases $H=0,1,2,3$:

| Number of heads | Number of sequences |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | $\binom{10}{0}=1$ |
| 1 | $\binom{10}{1}=10$ |
| 2 | $\binom{10}{2}=45$ |
| 3 | $\binom{10}{3}=120$ |

Add their counts:

$$
P(H\le3)
=\frac{1+10+45+120}{1{,}024}
=\frac{176}{1{,}024}
=\frac{11}{64}
=0.171875.
$$

The phrase “three or fewer” includes zero heads. The all-tail sequence is a valid outcome and contributes the first term.

## Check your understanding

Find the probability of at least seven heads. Then find the probability of more than three heads. Explain why these are different uses of symmetry and complementation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Use a one-to-one flip of the symbols</summary>

Exchanging H and T maps every sequence with at most three heads to one with at least seven heads. Under the fair model those paired sequences have equal probability, so

$$
P(H\ge7)=P(H\le3)=\frac{11}{64}.
$$

The complement of at most three heads is more than three heads, giving

$$
P(H>3)=1-\frac{11}{64}=\frac{53}{64}=0.828125.
$$

The first result uses symmetry; the second uses an exhaustive split into an event and its complement.
