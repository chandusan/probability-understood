# Audit the complete poker distribution

A correct category table must do more than add to the right grand total. Each row must come from a valid construction, and the categories must cover every hand exactly once. The source contains two errors that cancel in the total, making this an instructive counting audit.

## The corrected counts and probabilities

Every probability below is the exact count divided by $N=\binom{52}{5}=2{,}598{,}960$. Displayed decimals are rounded to eight places.

The source labels high card “nothing.” That means none of the stronger listed structures; it does not mean the hand lacks cards or ranks. The source pie chart highlights that high card and one pair together account for most hands.

## Two source errors hide inside a correct grand total

The source prints **1,302,588** for high card and **3,696** for full house. The constructions give

$$
\left(\binom{13}{5}-10\right)(4^5-4)=1{,}302{,}540,
$$

$$
13\cdot12\binom43\binom42=3{,}744.
$$

So the source's high-card row has 48 too many hands, while its full-house row has 48 too few. Their errors cancel. The source's listed counts do sum to $2{,}598{,}960$, but two individual rows and their probabilities are still wrong.

The corrected high-card probability is approximately $0.50117739$, rather than $0.50119586$. The corrected full-house probability is approximately $0.00144058$, rather than $0.00142211$.

## Why these categories cover every hand

Look first at repeated-rank patterns. A five-card hand can have multiplicities

$$
2+1+1+1,\quad2+2+1,\quad3+1+1,\quad3+2,\quad4+1,
$$

or all five ranks distinct. Five of a kind is impossible in a single standard deck, since each rank has only four suits.

The five repeated-rank patterns give one pair, two pairs, three of a kind, full house, and four of a kind. They cannot form a five-card straight or flush. For distinct ranks, ask two yes/no questions: are the ranks a straight, and are the suits all the same? These produce high card, ordinary straight, ordinary flush, or a straight flush. Split the last into royal and nonroyal, and every hand belongs to exactly one row.

A hand cannot have both multiplicity patterns $3+2$ and $4+1$, so the two categories are disjoint. Their combined probability is

$$
\frac{3{,}744+624}{2{,}598{,}960}
=\frac{4{,}368}{2{,}598{,}960}
\approx0.00168067.
$$

Using the source's incorrect full-house count would give a wrong numerator. Its grand total still matches the denominator only because the unrelated high-card row has an offsetting error. Totals are necessary checks, but they do not replace checking each construction.
