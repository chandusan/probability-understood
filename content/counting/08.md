# One card can satisfy two descriptions

“Ace or diamonds” includes every ace, every diamond, and the ace of diamonds only once. The entire problem turns on noticing that two descriptions can point to the same physical card.

## Lay out the deck

A standard deck without jokers has four suits and thirteen ranks. The suits are clubs, diamonds, hearts, and spades. The ranks are ace, $2$ through $10$, jack, queen, and king. Each rank-suit combination names exactly one of $4\cdot13=52$ distinct cards.

Assume one card is selected uniformly. Let $A$ mean “ace,” $D$ mean “diamond,” and $W=A\cup D$. The word “or” is inclusive: satisfying either description, including both, qualifies.

## Count the overlap before adding

There are four aces and thirteen diamonds. The ace of diamonds belongs to both collections, so

$$|A\cap D|=1.$$

Adding $4+13$ counts it twice. The corrected union count is

$$|W|=4+13-1=16.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{P(W)=\frac4{52}+\frac{13}{52}-\frac1{52}
=\frac{16}{52}=\frac4{13}\approx0.3077.}$$

The subtraction removes the extra copy, not the card itself. The ace of diamonds remains a qualifying outcome.

## An alternative count with no overlap

Count the thirteen diamonds first. Then add only the three aces that are not diamonds. These two groups are disjoint, giving $13+3=16$.

This is the same logic in a different form. Inclusion–exclusion starts with overlapping categories and corrects them. Partitioning starts with disjoint categories and adds them directly.

## “Or” and “exactly one” are different events

“Ace or diamonds” includes the ace of diamonds. “Ace or diamonds, but not both” excludes it. The latter has $16-1=15$ cards. In the raw sum $4+13$, obtaining the exactly-one count requires subtracting the overlap twice: one removes the duplicate copy, and the second removes the card from the event.

## Check your understanding

What is the probability of drawing an ace or a red card? A standard deck has $26$ red cards.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Work through the answer</summary>

Two aces are red: the ace of diamonds and ace of hearts. Therefore

$$P(\text{ace or red})=\frac{4+26-2}{52}=\frac{28}{52}=\frac7{13}.$$

Equivalently, count the $26$ red cards and add the two black aces. Subtracting only one overlap would be wrong here because the intersection contains two cards.
