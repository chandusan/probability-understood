# Use suit symmetry without assuming independence

Exactly five hearts has the same probability as exactly five spades. A standard shuffled deck treats suits symmetrically, so exchanging the suit names pairs the two collections of hands one for one. Equal probabilities, however, do not imply that the two events are independent.

## Repeat the count with hearts as the chosen category

Define $B=\{X_H=5\}$. Choose five hearts from the 13 available and eight non-hearts from the other 39 cards:

$$|B|=\binom{13}{5}\binom{39}{8}=79{,}181{,}063{,}676.$$

Hence,

$$\boxed{P(B)=\frac{79{,}181{,}063{,}676}{635{,}013{,}559{,}600}
\approx0.1246919258.}$$

The non-hearts may include spades, diamonds, and clubs in any combination. In particular, a hand counted here can also contain exactly five spades; the two events are not disjoint.

## Prove the equality by constructing a pairing

Take any hand containing exactly five spades. Replace each spade by the heart of the same rank and each heart by the spade of the same rank; leave diamonds and clubs unchanged.

This produces a valid 13-card hand with exactly five hearts. Apply the same exchange again and the original hand returns. Thus the transformation is one-to-one and onto between the five-spade hands and the five-heart hands.

Under a uniform shuffle, paired hands have equal probability. Therefore $P(A)=P(B)$ without needing to recompute the large integers. This is a symmetry argument grounded in an explicit bijection, not merely a guess that two answers should look alike.

## What symmetry does and does not tell us

Symmetry determines the individual probabilities, sometimes called marginal probabilities. It does not determine the intersection $P(A\cap B)$.

Knowing that a hand already contains five spades leaves eight positions for hearts, diamonds, and clubs. The remaining eight cards form a uniformly selected subset of the 39 non-spades. Consequently,

$$P(B\mid A)=\frac{\binom{13}{5}\binom{26}{3}}{\binom{39}{8}}
\approx0.0543887541.$$

This is much smaller than the unconditional $P(B)\approx0.1246919258$. The fixed 13-card hand size couples the suit counts: allocating five cards to spades changes the room and pool available for hearts.

The conditional formula is a preview of the next slide. It counts five hearts and three minor-suit cards among the eight non-spades, then divides by all eight-card subsets of the non-spade pool.

## Equal, disjoint, and independent answer different questions

The statements $P(A)=P(B)$, $A\cap B=\varnothing$, and $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$ express three different properties. In this problem the first is true, while the latter two are false.

For an extreme example, define C to be “all 13 cards are spades” and D to be “all 13 cards are hearts.” Their probabilities are equal by symmetry, but the events are disjoint and have positive probabilities, so they cannot be independent.

Exactly five diamonds has the same probability because diamonds is another suit with 13 cards. Exactly five red cards uses a category of 26 hearts or diamonds and a complementary category of 26 black cards. Its probability is $\binom{26}{5}\binom{26}{8}/\binom{52}{13}$, a different formula. Suit symmetry does not equate categories with different sizes.
