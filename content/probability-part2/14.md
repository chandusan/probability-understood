# Arrange repeated objects without counting duplicates

An arrangement records which kind of object occupies each position. Swapping two objects of the same kind changes nothing visible. The multiset permutation formula starts by distinguishing everything, then divides out precisely those invisible swaps.

## What the formula counts

Suppose there are $n$ objects in total, separated into $r$ kinds with counts $n_1,\ldots,n_r$, where $n_1+\cdots+n_r=n$. Objects within a kind are indistinguishable for the question being asked. The number of different linear arrangements is

$$\boxed{\frac{n!}{n_1!n_2!\cdots n_r!}.}$$

The positions remain distinct: first, second, third, and so on. Repetition changes the identity of the objects, not the identity of the positions. This is why the answer is generally larger than one even though many objects look alike.

The formula counts all arrangements of a **fixed inventory**. It does not describe unrestricted choice of a letter at every position, where the number of times each letter appears is allowed to vary.

## A complete example: BANANA

The six letters contain three A's, two N's, and one B. Temporarily label them $A_1,A_2,A_3,N_1,N_2,B$. There are $6!=720$ orders of these six distinct objects.

Now erase the labels. Every visible arrangement has been counted $3!2!1!=12$ times: the three A-labels can be permuted among the A-positions in six ways, and the two N-labels in two ways. Therefore,

$$\frac{6!}{3!2!1!}=\frac{720}{12}=60.$$

Division works because **every** visible arrangement has exactly the same number of labeled versions. If different outcomes had different multiplicities, dividing by one common factor would not fix the count.

## A second proof: choose positions for each kind

Choose three of the six positions for A, then two of the remaining three for N. B takes the remaining position:

$$\binom63\binom32\binom11=20\cdot3\cdot1=60.$$

For general counts, this becomes

$$\binom n{n_1}\binom{n-n_1}{n_2}\cdots
=\frac{n!}{n_1!\cdots n_r!}.$$

The intermediate factorials cancel. The construction is unique: a finished arrangement tells us exactly which positions were chosen for each kind. No extra factor for “ordering the kinds” is needed.

## What changes when the question changes?

Six individually numbered tiles have $6!$ distinct physical orders, even if some display the same letter. Six displayed letters spelling BANANA have only 60 visible orders. The objects are not inherently distinguishable or indistinguishable; the recorded outcome determines which differences matter.

Also, this theorem concerns a line. Arranging objects around a circle, where rotations are identified, requires another argument. We cannot automatically apply the linear formula and divide by $n$ when repeated objects can create rotational symmetries.

## Check your understanding

How many arrangements can be made from A, A, B, B? Why does an absent category contribute $0!=1$?

<summary>Count directly and explain the boundary case</summary>

The answer is $4!/(2!2!)=6$: AABB, ABAB, ABBA, BAAB, BABA, BBAA. Equivalently, choose the two A-positions in $\binom42=6$ ways.

There is exactly one way to place zero objects of an absent kind: place none. Consequently $0!=1$ preserves the count. An absent letter must not turn the denominator into zero.
