# Two overlap puzzles: divisibility and long digit runs

Inclusion–exclusion applies whenever one outcome can satisfy several descriptions of success. An integer may have several required divisors; a long run of equal digits may contain several qualifying five-digit windows. The essential work is defining events whose intersections are countable.

## Example A: divisible by 2, 3, or 7

Choose an integer uniformly from $\{1,2,\ldots,2019\}$. Let A, B, and C be divisibility by 2, 3, and 7, respectively. All 2019 integers have equal weight, so calculate a union count and divide by 2019.

The number of multiples of a positive integer d up to 2019 is

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{2019}{d}\right\rfloor.
$$

The floor is needed because an incomplete final block contributes no additional multiple. Thus

$$
|A|=1009,\qquad |B|=673,\qquad |C|=288.
$$

An integer lies in an intersection when it is divisible by the **least common multiple** of the corresponding divisors. Here 2, 3, and 7 are pairwise coprime, so the relevant least common multiples are their products:

| Intersection | Required divisor | Count |
|---|---:|---:|
| $A\cap B$ | 6 | 336 |
| $A\cap C$ | 14 | 144 |
| $B\cap C$ | 21 | 96 |
| $A\cap B\cap C$ | 42 | 48 |

Therefore

$$
|A\cup B\cup C|=1009+673+288-336-144-96+48=1442,
$$

and the requested probability is

$$
\boxed{\frac{1442}{2019}\approx0.71421496.}
$$

This confirms the answer printed on the slide. Do not always multiply divisors when forming intersections: “divisible by 4 and 6” means divisible by 12, not necessarily by 24.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Check the arithmetic with complete blocks of 42</summary>

Each block of 42 consecutive integers contains $21+14+6-7-3-2+1=30$ integers divisible by at least one of 2, 3, or 7. Since $2019=48\cdot42+3$, the first 2016 integers contribute $48\cdot30=1440$. The last three are 2017, 2018, and 2019; 2018 is even and 2019 is divisible by 3, adding two. The total is again 1442.

</details>

## Example B: five or more consecutive equal digits

Choose uniformly from all length-seven strings of digits 0 through 9. Leading zeroes are allowed, because the source asks for a **string**, not a seven-digit positive integer. There are $10^7$ equally likely strings.

A run of at least five equal digits must contain an equal-digit window in positions 1–5, 2–6, or 3–7. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_1&=\{X_1=X_2=X_3=X_4=X_5\},\\
W_2&=\{X_2=X_3=X_4=X_5=X_6\},\\
W_3&=\{X_3=X_4=X_5=X_6=X_7\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The desired event is $W_1\cup W_2\cup W_3$. Each individual event allows ten choices for the repeated digit and ten choices for each of two outside positions, so $|W_i|=10^3=1000$.

Those outside positions are unrestricted. They may equal the repeated digit, producing a run of length six or seven, which the problem explicitly allows.

## Understand each overlapping window

The overlap $W_1\cap W_2$ forces positions 1–6 to be equal: the windows share positions, so their repeated digits must agree. The seventh digit is free, giving $10^2=100$ strings. Likewise, $|W_2\cap W_3|=100$.

The windows for $W_1$ and $W_3$ overlap in positions 3–5 and together cover all seven positions. Hence their intersection forces all seven digits to agree:

$$
|W_1\cap W_3|=10.
$$

The triple intersection also has ten strings. Therefore

$$
|W_1\cup W_2\cup W_3|
=3000-(100+100+10)+10=2800.
$$

The exact probability is

$$
\boxed{\frac{2800}{10^7}=0.00028=0.028\%.}
$$

The result is about 28 qualifying strings per 100,000 random strings. “Five consecutive equal digits” is different from “the same digit occurs five times somewhere”; the positions must be adjacent.

## Verify by the longest run

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Partition into maximum run lengths 5, 6, and 7</summary>

A run of exactly seven gives ten strings. A maximum run of six can start at position 1 or 2; the remaining end digit must differ, giving $2\cdot10\cdot9=180$ strings.

For maximum run five, a run starting at position 1 requires position 6 to differ, while position 7 is free: $10\cdot9\cdot10=900$. A run starting at position 3 similarly gives 900. A run starting at position 2 requires both adjacent end digits to differ, giving $10\cdot9^2=810$. These cases total $2610$.

Adding the disjoint maximum-length categories gives $2610+180+10=2800$. The agreement checks both the intersections and the interpretation of “at least.”

</details>
