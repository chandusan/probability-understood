# Ten dice: make every face appear at least once

Requiring all six faces at once sounds like an intersection problem, but its complement is simpler: at least one face is missing. Inclusion–exclusion counts the overlapping ways faces can be missing and gives an exact probability for complete coverage.

## Label the dice and define the bad events

Assume the ten standard dice are fair and independent. Number them from 1 to 10. There are $6^{10}$ equally likely ordered outcomes; rolling simultaneously does not make the dice indistinguishable.

For $j=1,\ldots,6$, let

$$
M_j=\{\text{face }j\text{ does not appear on any die}\}.
$$

The event that every face appears is $(M_1\cup\cdots\cup M_6)^c$. Defining the missing-face events is the main strategic step. A restriction saying “avoid these faces” gives every die the same set of allowed values.

## Count a specified intersection

If face 1 is missing, each die has five choices, so $|M_1|=5^{10}$. If faces 1 and 2 are both missing, each die has four choices, so $|M_1\cap M_2|=4^{10}$.

For any **specified** set of k missing faces, the count is

$$
(6-k)^{10}.
$$

There are $\binom6k$ choices for that set of missing faces. This binomial coefficient counts which restrictions we impose. The power counts dice outcomes after imposing them.

Crucially, these intersections mean that the specified faces are missing; other faces may be missing as well. Inclusion–exclusion corrects those overlaps. They are not counts for “exactly k missing faces.”

## Alternate the corrections

Starting with all outcomes and excluding the union of missing-face events gives

$$
P(\text{all six faces appear})
=\frac{1}{6^{10}}\sum_{k=0}^{6}(-1)^k\binom6k(6-k)^{10}.
$$

The $k=0$ term is the unrestricted sample space. Written out, the favorable count is

$$
6^{10}-6\cdot5^{10}+15\cdot4^{10}-20\cdot3^{10}
+15\cdot2^{10}-6\cdot1^{10}.
$$

The final $k=6$ term is zero because ten dice cannot take values from an empty set. The numerical calculation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
N={}&60{,}466{,}176-58{,}593{,}750+15{,}728{,}640\\
&-1{,}180{,}980+15{,}360-6\\
={}&16{,}435{,}440.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore the exact answer is

$$
\boxed{\frac{16{,}435{,}440}{60{,}466{,}176}
=\frac{38{,}045}{139{,}968}
\approx0.2718121285.}
$$

Even with ten rolls, complete coverage occurs only about $27.18\%$ of the time. Repeated faces consume rolls without introducing a missing face.

## Explore how coverage changes with the number of dice

<div class="interactive-slot" data-interactive="rules-coverage"></div>

For n dice, replace the exponent 10 with n in the same formula. Fewer than six dice make coverage impossible. With exactly six dice, each face must appear once, so the favorable count is $6!$ and the probability is $6!/6^6$. As the number of independent rolls grows, the coverage probability approaches one.

The missing-face events are dependent. For instance, knowing that face 1 never appears changes the distribution of the remaining observed faces. We therefore cannot multiply six “this face appears” probabilities to obtain the coverage probability.

## Verify the count by building sequences

<details markdown="1">
<summary>A recurrence gives an independent exact check</summary>

Let $a_{r,k}$ be the number of length-r dice sequences using exactly k distinct faces. Start with $a_{0,0}=1$, and take impossible states to have count zero. To form a sequence using k faces after the next roll, either reuse one of k faces already seen, or introduce one of the $7-k$ faces absent from a sequence that previously used $k-1$ faces. Thus

$$
a_{r+1,k}=k\,a_{r,k}+(7-k)a_{r,k-1}.
$$

Iterating to $r=10$ gives $a_{10,6}=16{,}435{,}440$, matching inclusion–exclusion. Equivalently, distribute the ten labeled positions into six nonempty groups and label the groups by the six faces: the count is $6!S(10,6)$, where $S(10,6)=22{,}827$ is a Stirling number of the second kind.

</details>

## Check your understanding

Why does $6!\,6^4$ fail as a favorable count for ten dice?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>The proposed construction adds an unrequested condition</summary>

It forces the first six dice to show every face, then leaves the last four free. That is a valid subset of successful outcomes, but it misses outcomes whose sixth distinct face first appears on die 7, 8, 9, or 10. Choosing one representative position for each face instead creates a different problem: outcomes with repeated faces can have many possible representative choices and become overcounted.

</details>
