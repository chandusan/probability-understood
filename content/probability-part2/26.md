# Count the overlap: five spades and five hearts

A hand in the intersection must satisfy both exact suit counts at once. Five spades and five hearts use ten cards, leaving three cards that must be diamonds or clubs. Partitioning the deck into those three categories gives the intersection probability directly.

## Build the hand from three disjoint pools

Let $A=\{X_S=5\}$ and $B=\{X_H=5\}$. A qualifying hand is formed by choosing:

1. Five cards from the 13 spades.
2. Five cards from the 13 hearts.
3. Three cards from the 26 diamonds and clubs.

Every such triple of choices gives one valid hand. Conversely, every valid hand has exactly one spade subset, one heart subset, and one minor-suit subset. Thus there are no missing hands or duplicate constructions:

$$|A\cap B|=\binom{13}{5}\binom{13}{5}\binom{26}{3}.$$

There is no restriction on how the final three cards divide between diamonds and clubs. All four possibilities, 3–0, 2–1, 1–2, and 0–3, are included in $\binom{26}{3}$.

Choosing the last three from all 42 cards remaining after the first ten would be wrong. That pool still contains eight spades and eight hearts. Selecting any of them would raise one of the prescribed suit counts above five and leave the intersection event.

## Evaluate the count and probability

The factors are

$$\binom{13}{5}=1{,}287,\qquad
\binom{26}{3}=\frac{26\cdot25\cdot24}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=2{,}600.$$

Therefore,

$$|A\cap B|=1{,}287^2\cdot2{,}600=4{,}306{,}559{,}400,$$

and

$$\boxed{P(A\cap B)=\frac{4{,}306{,}559{,}400}{635{,}013{,}559{,}600}
\approx0.00678183849.}$$

This is about **0.6782%**, not 6.782%. A probability near 0.0068 corresponds to fewer than one successful hand per hundred hands on average.

## Correct a numerical typo on the source slide

Here the correct third factor is **2,600**. The correct expanded line is

$$1{,}287\cdot1{,}287\cdot2{,}600=4{,}306{,}559{,}400.$$

Reading each factor as a choice catches this error immediately: the last stage chooses three cards from 26, not eight cards from 39.

## “And” does not automatically mean multiply probabilities

Multiplying the two marginal probabilities would give

$$P(A)P(B)\approx0.0155480764,$$

which is not the intersection probability. That multiplication would require independence, and the two suit-count events are dependent.

The valid probability multiplication is conditional:

$$\begin{aligned}
P(A\cap B)&=P(A)P(B\mid A)\\
&=\frac{\binom{13}{5}\binom{39}{8}}{\binom{52}{13}}
\frac{\binom{13}{5}\binom{26}{3}}{\binom{39}{8}}.
\end{aligned}$$

Canceling $\binom{39}{8}$ recovers the direct count. The counting product and the conditional probability product agree, but they justify their multiplication in different ways.

The remaining card must be a club because $5+5+2+1=13$. The favorable count is $\binom{13}{5}^2\binom{13}{2}\binom{13}{1}$. Divide by $\binom{52}{13}$ for its probability. Summing over zero, one, two, or three diamonds recovers $\binom{13}{5}^2\binom{26}{3}$ by partitioning the three minor-suit cards according to their diamond count.
