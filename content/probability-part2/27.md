# At least one major suit has exactly five cards

The final bridge question combines the two exact-five events. A hand with five spades and five hearts belongs to both, so subtract its probability once when forming the union. The result is about 24.2602%, with an important distinction between “exactly five” and “five or more.”

## Combine the exact counts before rounding

For $A=\{X_S=5\}$ and $B=\{X_H=5\}$,

$$\begin{aligned}
P(A\cup B)
&=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\\
&=\frac{2\binom{13}{5}\binom{39}{8}-\binom{13}{5}^2\binom{26}{3}}
{\binom{52}{13}}\\
&=\frac{154{,}055{,}567{,}952}{635{,}013{,}559{,}600}\\
&\approx\boxed{0.2426020132}.
\end{aligned}$$

The numerator adds the $79{,}181{,}063{,}676$ five-spade hands and the same number of five-heart hands, then removes one duplicate copy of the $4{,}306{,}559{,}400$ hands satisfying both conditions.

The event is inclusive: a hand with five cards in each major suit is a successful outcome and must remain counted once.

## See all four regions of the bridge problem

| Region | Probability, approximately |
|---|---:|
| Exactly five spades, but not exactly five hearts | $0.1179100873$ |
| Exactly five hearts, but not exactly five spades | $0.1179100873$ |
| Exactly five of both suits | $0.0067818385$ |
| Neither suit has exactly five cards | $0.7573979868$ |

The first three rows sum to the union. The first two alone give the probability that **exactly one** of the two major suits has exactly five cards:

$$P(\text{exactly one of A and B})\approx0.2358201747.$$

Notice that “neither has exactly five” does not mean “both have fewer than five.” A hand with six spades and four hearts lies in the neither region, as does a hand with four spades and four hearts.

## Clarify the source's “five-card major” wording

The source's calculation establishes that at least one major suit has length **equal to five**. Here “at least one” counts how many of the two events occur; it does not change their internal condition from $=5$ to $\geq5$.

If the question instead means a major suit of **five or more** cards, define

$$C=\{X_S\geq5\},\qquad D=\{X_H\geq5\}.$$

These are larger events. For example, a hand with six spades and four hearts belongs to $C\cup D$ but not to $A\cup B$. We must include the extra suit lengths in the calculation.

## Compute the minimum-five version as an extension

By symmetry,

$$P(C)=P(D)=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{s=5}^{13}\binom{13}{s}\binom{39}{13-s}}{\binom{52}{13}}.$$

For their overlap, both suit counts must be at least five and their sum cannot exceed thirteen:

$$P(C\cap D)=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{s=5}^{8}\sum_{h=5}^{13-s}
\binom{13}{s}\binom{13}{h}\binom{26}{13-s-h}}{\binom{52}{13}}.$$

The addition rule then gives

$$P(C\cup D)=2P(C)-P(C\cap D)\approx0.3433140909.$$

Thus the minimum-five interpretation gives about **34.3314%**, while the displayed source calculation gives about **24.2602%** for the exact-five interpretation. Defining the event resolves the ambiguity before it affects the answer.
