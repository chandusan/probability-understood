# Write both sampling distributions and compare their spread

With $a$ group-A and $b$ group-B objects, let $N=a+b$ and let $X$ count group-A selections in $n$ draws. The two formulas differ because replacement changes which samples are possible and how their probabilities behave.

## With replacement

Under independent uniform draws,

$$\boxed{P(X=x)=\binom nx\left(\frac aN\right)^x
\left(\frac bN\right)^{n-x},\qquad x=0,\ldots,n.}$$

The coefficient counts positions for the successes. The powers give the probability of one pattern. The number of distinct A objects does not restrict the number of A selections.

## Without replacement

For a uniform subset of $n\le N$ distinct objects,

$$\boxed{P(X=x)=\frac{\binom ax\binom b{n-x}}{\binom Nn}.}$$

The support must obey both category capacities:

$$0\le x\le a,\qquad 0\le n-x\le b.$$

Combining these gives

$$\max(0,n-b)\le x\le\min(n,a).$$

A binomial coefficient is interpreted as zero when its selection count is impossible.

## The same mean does not mean the same distribution

Let $p=a/N$. Both experiments have

$$E[X]=np.$$

To see why without replacement, write $X=I_1+\cdots+I_n$, where $I_j$ is one if draw $j$ is A and zero otherwise. Each $E[I_j]=p$. Linearity of expectation does not require independence, so the mean is still $np$.

For $N>1$, the variances are

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)_{\text{replacement}}=np(1-p),$$

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)_{\text{no replacement}}
=np(1-p)\frac{N-n}{N-1}.$$

The factor $(N-n)/(N-1)$ is the finite-population correction. Removing a success reduces the fraction of successes remaining, so later draws tend to offset earlier deviations.

## Check the extreme case

If $n=N$ without replacement, we inspect the entire population. Then $X=a$ with certainty and the variance is zero. The correction factor correctly becomes zero. With replacement, even $N$ selections need not cover every object, so the count generally still varies.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Derive the finite-population correction explicitly</summary>

Let $I_i$ indicate success on draw $i$. Each indicator has variance $p(1-p)$. For different positions $i,j$, uniform sampling without replacement gives

$$E[I_iI_j]=P(I_i=1,I_j=1)=\frac aN\frac{a-1}{N-1}.$$

The covariance measures the difference from multiplying the marginals:

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,I_j)
&=p\frac{a-1}{N-1}-p^2\\
&=p\frac{a-1-p(N-1)}{N-1}\\
&=-\frac{p(1-p)}{N-1},
\end{aligned}$$

where we used $a=pN$. It is negative because drawing one success leaves fewer for later positions.

Expanding the square of $X=\sum_i I_i$ gives $n$ variance terms and $n(n-1)$ ordered covariance terms:

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X)
&=np(1-p)-n(n-1)\frac{p(1-p)}{N-1}\\
&=np(1-p)\left(1-\frac{n-1}{N-1}\right)\\
&=np(1-p)\frac{N-n}{N-1}.
\end{aligned}$$

This derives the correction from the actual sampling probabilities.

</details>
