# Condition on an even first-six roll

Let $X$ be the roll of the first six in independent fair-die rolls. We know the first-six roll was even. What is the probability that it was also divisible by three? The condition changes which stopping times are available and how their probabilities are normalized.

## Name the events and find their intersection

Let $A=\{X\text{ divisible by }3\}$ and $B=\{X\text{ divisible by }2\}$. Their intersection is

$$A\cap B=\{X\text{ divisible by }6\}.$$

Thus the definition of conditional probability gives

$$P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(X\text{ divisible by }6)}{P(X\text{ even})}.$$

The denominator is $5/11$. We still need the numerator.

## Sum the multiples of six

With $p=1/6$ and $q=5/6$,

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\text{ divisible by }6)
&=P(X=6)+P(X=12)+P(X=18)+\cdots\\
&=pq^5+pq^{11}+pq^{17}+\cdots\\
&=pq^5(1+q^6+q^{12}+\cdots)\\
&=\frac{pq^5}{1-q^6}.
\end{aligned}$$

The first term has five failures followed by a success. Advancing to the next qualifying stopping time adds six failures, explaining the common ratio $q^6$.

Substituting the fractions,

$$\frac{(1/6)(5/6)^5}{1-(5/6)^6}
=\frac{5^5}{6^6-5^6}
=\frac{3125}{31031}.$$

## Finish the conditional probability

$$\boxed{P(A\mid B)=\frac{3125/31031}{5/11}
=\frac{625}{2821}\approx0.221553.}$$

The numerator keeps the probability of stopping times that satisfy both requirements. Dividing by $P(B)$ rescales the surviving even-roll outcomes so their conditional probabilities total one. It does not make those even values equally likely.
