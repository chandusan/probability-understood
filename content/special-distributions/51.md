# Count defective cars in a sample without replacement

Ten cars have just left a production line. Exactly three are defective and seven are defect-free. Choose four distinct cars uniformly at random and let $X$ count the defective cars in the sample. Find the entire probability distribution of $X$.

## Establish the equally likely outcomes

An outcome is a set of four labeled cars. Every set is equally likely under uniform sampling, so there are

$$\binom{10}{4}=210$$

outcomes in the denominator. The outcomes are individual samples, not merely the four possible defect counts; those counts are not equally likely.

## Build a sample with exactly $x$ defects

Choose $x$ of the three defective cars, then choose the other $4-x$ cars from the seven good cars. The multiplication rule gives

$$\binom3x\binom7{4-x}$$

favorable samples. Each sample appears once: its defective and good parts identify it uniquely. Therefore

$$\boxed{P(X=x)=\frac{\binom3x\binom7{4-x}}{210},\qquad x=0,1,2,3.}$$

Four defects are impossible because the entire population contains only three.

| Defects $x$ | Favorable samples | Probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $1\cdot35=35$ | $1/6$ |
| $1$ | $3\cdot35=105$ | $1/2$ |
| $2$ | $3\cdot21=63$ | $3/10$ |
| $3$ | $1\cdot7=7$ | $1/30$ |

The counts sum to $35+105+63+7=210$, checking that every sample has been included.

## Translate a question into table entries

“At least two defective cars” means $X=2$ or $X=3$. These alternatives are disjoint, so

$$P(X\ge2)=\frac3{10}+\frac1{30}=\frac13.$$

“At least one” is most quickly calculated using the complement:

$$P(X\ge1)=1-P(X=0)=\frac56.$$

The expected defect count is $4(3/10)=1.2$. Its variance is

$$4\left(\frac3{10}\right)\left(\frac7{10}\right)\frac{10-4}{10-1}=0.56.$$

<details markdown="1"><summary>Why not use a binomial probability with p = 0.3?</summary>

After drawing a defective car, the next defect probability is $2/9$; after drawing a good car it is $3/9$. The remaining population changes, so the draws are dependent. Replacement would restore the population and produce the binomial model instead.

</details>
