# Find an MGF by taking an ordinary expectation

Suppose $P(X=1)=1/3$ and $P(X=2)=2/3$, with no other possible values. We want the moment generating function $m_X(t)$ and then a check that it really contains the familiar moments.

## Transform the values, preserving their probabilities

When $X=1$, the transformed variable $e^{tX}$ equals $e^t$. When $X=2$, it equals $e^{2t}$. Therefore

$$\boxed{m_X(t)=\frac13e^t+\frac23e^{2t}.}$$

This is an expectation, so the two terms are added. We are averaging over mutually exclusive possible values of $X$, not multiplying probabilities of successive events.

Because the sum has only two finite terms, it is finite for every real $t$. In particular, it exists around zero.

## Check normalization

At zero,

$$m_X(0)=\frac13+\frac23=1.$$

This is a useful check on any proposed MGF. A function with $m(0)\ne1$ cannot be the MGF of a probability distribution.

## Retrieve the mean and second moment

Differentiate with respect to $t$:

$$m_X'(t)=\frac13e^t+\frac43e^{2t},\qquad
m_X''(t)=\frac13e^t+\frac83e^{2t}.$$

Setting $t=0$ gives

$$E[X]=\frac53,\qquad E[X^2]=3.$$

These agree with direct calculation: $1(1/3)+2(2/3)=5/3$ and $1^2(1/3)+2^2(2/3)=3$.

Finally,

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-E[X]^2=3-\frac{25}{9}=\frac29.$$

Notice that $m_X''(0)$ gives the second raw moment, **not** the variance. Subtracting the squared mean is a separate step.
