# Set up four questions about the first six

Roll a fair die independently until the first six appears. Let $X$ be the number of that roll, including the successful roll. Find the probabilities of at most five rolls, at least ten rolls, an even-numbered first six, and a first-six roll divisible by three given that it is even.

## Specify the distribution

Success means rolling a six, so $p=1/6$ and $q=5/6$. The geometric PMF is

$$P(X=x)=\frac16\left(\frac56\right)^{x-1},\qquad x=1,2,\ldots.$$

The four events are

$$X\le5,\qquad X\ge10,\qquad X\in\{2,4,6,\ldots\},$$

and the conditional event $3\mid X$ given $2\mid X$, where divisibility means an exact integer multiple.

## The threshold questions are easiest by complements

“At most five rolls” means at least one six in the first five rolls. Its complement is five failures, giving

$$P(X\le5)=1-q^5\approx0.598122.$$

“At least ten rolls” means no six in the first nine rolls, giving

$$P(X\ge10)=q^9\approx0.193807.$$

No condition is imposed on the tenth roll in the second event: the first six may occur on roll ten or later.

## The divisibility questions need selected terms

Even values contribute $pq+pq^3+pq^5+\cdots$, so

$$P(X\text{ even})=\frac{pq}{1-q^2}=\frac q{1+q}=\frac5{11}.$$

For the conditional question, a number that is both even and divisible by three is divisible by six. Therefore

$$P(X\text{ divisible by }3\mid X\text{ even})=\frac{P(X\text{ divisible by }6)}{P(X\text{ even})}=\frac{625}{2821}\approx0.221553.$$
