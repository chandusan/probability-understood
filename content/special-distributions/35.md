# Model an event count with a Poisson distribution

A Poisson variable counts events in a specified interval or region and has probability mass function $P(X=x)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!$ for $x=0,1,2,\ldots$. The parameter $\lambda>0$ is the expected count in that interval.

## A count in time is not automatically Poisson

The source motivates the model using events occurring over time. Additional assumptions are needed for a homogeneous Poisson-process interpretation: disjoint time intervals have independent counts; the rate is constant; and over a very short interval, one event has probability approximately proportional to its length while two or more are much less likely.

Events that occur in clusters, have changing rates, or influence one another may not satisfy those assumptions. In an exercise, “$X$ is Poisson with parameter $\lambda$” directly specifies the distribution without requiring us to estimate it from observations.

## Understand the parameter and units

If events occur at rate $r$ per hour and the interval has length $T$ hours, then

$$\lambda=rT.$$

A rate of three events per hour gives $\lambda=6$ over two hours. The PMF parameter is the expected count over the chosen interval; it is not the probability of an individual event.

## Interpret a few values

For $X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(2)$,

$$P(X=0)=e^{-2},\qquad P(X=1)=2e^{-2},\qquad P(X=2)=2e^{-2}.$$

Zero belongs in the support. There is no fixed maximum count, although large counts may be very unlikely.

The parameter may be noninteger: a mean of $2.4$ events is meaningful even though observed counts are integers.

## Where the formula comes from

One route splits the interval into many small, approximately independent opportunities, each with a small success probability. A binomial count then approaches a Poisson count when the mean number of successes stays fixed. We will derive that limit explicitly later in the chapter.
