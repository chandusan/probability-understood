# When sampling without replacement resembles independent trials

The slide says that sampling with and without replacement becomes equivalent for a large population. The useful qualification is that the sample must be small relative to the population. Large $N$ alone does not justify a binomial approximation.

## See why depletion becomes negligible

Let the original success fraction be $p=a/N$. After $j$ draws containing $s$ successes, the next success probability without replacement is

$$\frac{a-s}{N-j}.$$

Its difference from the original fraction is

$$\frac{a-s}{N-j}-\frac aN=\frac{pj-s}{N-j}.$$

Since $0\le s\le j$, the absolute difference is at most $j/(N-j)$. When the entire sample size $n$ is tiny compared with $N$, each conditional success probability remains close to $p$. Independent Bernoulli draws then become a useful approximation.

## Derive the limiting PMF

Write the falling factorial as $(a)_x=a(a-1)\cdots(a-x+1)$. Rearranging the hypergeometric PMF gives

$$\frac{\binom ax\binom b{n-x}}{\binom Nn}
=\binom nx\frac{(a)_x(b)_{n-x}}{(N)_n}.$$

For fixed $n$, as $N\to\infty$ with $a/N\to p\in(0,1)$,

$$\frac{(a)_x}{N^x}\to p^x,\qquad
\frac{(b)_{n-x}}{N^{n-x}}\to(1-p)^{n-x},\qquad
\frac{(N)_n}{N^n}\to1.$$

Therefore

$$P(X=x)\longrightarrow\binom nxp^x(1-p)^{n-x}.$$

This is a limit statement, not exact equality for a finite population.

## Check whether the spreads are close

The hypergeometric-to-binomial variance ratio is

$$\frac{N-n}{N-1}.$$

If $N=1000$ and $n=10$, the ratio is $990/999\approx0.991$: the variances are close. If $n=900$, the ratio is $100/999\approx0.100$: a binomial model greatly overstates the spread despite the large population.

If we sample all $N$ objects, the actual success count is known exactly. That is the clearest counterexample to using “large population” as the only condition.

A small sampling fraction is a useful diagnostic, not a universal guarantee of relative accuracy for every extremely rare event. When an exact hypergeometric calculation is readily available, use it.
