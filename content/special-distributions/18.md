# Count heads in seven fair tosses

Toss a fair coin seven times independently, and let $X$ be the number of heads. We want the complete probability distribution. Each toss is a Bernoulli trial with success defined as heads, so $X\sim\operatorname{Binom}(7,1/2)$.

## Find one count probability

For exactly $x$ heads, choose their positions in $\binom7x$ ways. Every seven-toss sequence has probability $(1/2)^7=1/128$. Therefore

$$\boxed{P(X=x)=\frac{\binom7x}{128},\qquad x=0,1,\ldots,7.}$$

For example, $HHHTTTT$ is one of $\binom73=35$ sequences containing three heads, so $P(X=3)=35/128$.

## The full distribution

| $x$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $\binom7x$ | 1 | 7 | 21 | 35 | 35 | 21 | 7 | 1 |
| $P(X=x)$ | $1/128$ | $7/128$ | $21/128$ | $35/128$ | $35/128$ | $21/128$ | $7/128$ | $1/128$ |

The numerators sum to 128, so the probabilities sum to one.

## Explain the symmetry by pairing outcomes

Swapping every head with a tail pairs a sequence with $x$ heads with a sequence having $7-x$ heads. This pairing is reversible and preserves probability for a fair coin. Hence $P(X=x)=P(X=7-x)$.

Because seven is odd, no sequence has exactly half heads. Every outcome is paired between “at most three heads” and “at least four heads,” so each event has probability $1/2$.
