# Read the Poisson table as probability mass

This table gives selected probabilities for $X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(13.1)$. Each entry is the probability of exactly one integer count. To find a probability over a range, add the entries corresponding to every count in that range.

## Mark the rows relevant to each question

For $P(X\le8)$, add the left-side entries from zero through eight. For the complement used in $P(X\ge10)$, include the entry at nine as well.

For the conditional numerator $P(10\le X\le15)$, add the right-side entries from ten through fifteen. This sum is approximately $0.595385$.

## Why the conditional answer is larger than that numerator

The numerator is a probability among all model years. The condition restricts us to the approximately $84.07\%$ of years with at least ten hurricanes. Dividing $0.595385$ by $0.840692$ expresses the same qualifying mass as a proportion of that smaller population.

No individual table entry changes until we condition. After conditioning on $X\ge10$, every surviving entry becomes $p(x)/P(X\ge10)$ and all entries for $x<10$ become zero. This rescaled distribution again sums to one.
