# Package a distribution into a function

A moment generating function, or MGF, transforms the values of a random variable using an exponential and then takes their probability-weighted average. It is useful because derivatives of exponentials bring down powers of the original values.

## Start from a familiar weighted average

If $X$ takes values $x_i$ with probabilities $p_i$, then

$$E[X]=\sum_i x_i p_i,\qquad E[X^2]=\sum_i x_i^2p_i.$$

These are called the first and second **raw moments**. “Raw” means powers of $X$ itself, rather than powers of the deviation $X-E[X]$.

Now replace $x_i$ by $e^{tx_i}$, where $t$ is an adjustable real number:

$$m_X(t)=\sum_i e^{tx_i}p_i.$$

For every fixed $t$, this is just another expectation. The randomness is in $X$; $t$ is a number we choose.

## Why the exponential is useful

Differentiating with respect to $t$ gives

$$\frac{d}{dt}e^{tx}=xe^{tx},\qquad
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}e^{tx}=x^2e^{tx}.$$

At $t=0$, the exponential becomes $1$. Thus the derivatives leave behind $x$ and $x^2$. After averaging, the corresponding quantities are $E[X]$ and $E[X^2]$.

This is the meaning of “moment generating”: differentiating the function generates moments. It does not mean that the MGF itself is a probability density or cumulative probability.

## One warning before using the package

An expectation of an exponential can be infinite even when $E[X]$ exists. We will require finiteness on a whole interval around zero before using the usual MGF results. That condition is what makes the derivative calculations valid beyond finite discrete examples.
