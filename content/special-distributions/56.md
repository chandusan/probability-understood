# Derive the multinomial coefficient by choosing positions

For $n$ independent categorical trials, suppose we require $x_1$ occurrences of category 1, $x_2$ of category 2, and so on, with total $n$. The multinomial coefficient counts the ordered category sequences satisfying those totals.

## Choose positions one category at a time

There are $\binom n{x_1}$ ways to choose category 1's positions. Once those are occupied, choose category 2's positions from the remaining $n-x_1$ places:

$$\binom n{x_1}\binom{n-x_1}{x_2}\binom{n-x_1-x_2}{x_3}\cdots.$$

Expand the first factors:

$$\frac{n!}{x_1!(n-x_1)!}
\frac{(n-x_1)!}{x_2!(n-x_1-x_2)!}\cdots.$$

The remaining-position factorials cancel, leaving

$$\boxed{\frac{n!}{x_1!x_2!\cdots x_k!}.}$$

The last category fills whatever positions remain. It contributes one choice, not another independent permutation of all $n$ positions.

## Work a complete four-trial example

Suppose three categories C, R, N have probabilities $0.3,0.5,0.2$. We want exactly two C, one R, and one N in four trials.

Choose two C positions: $\binom42=6$. Choose one R position among the two remaining: $\binom21=2$. The final position must be N. There are $6\cdot2=12$ sequences.

One particular sequence, CCRN, has probability

$$0.3\cdot0.3\cdot0.5\cdot0.2=0.009.$$

All 12 sequences have this same probability, so

$$P(X_C=2,X_R=1,X_N=1)=12(0.009)=\boxed{0.108}.$$

## Why the factorials divide out repeated labels

If all four positions were filled with distinguishable labels $C_1,C_2,R,N$, there would be $4!$ arrangements. Swapping $C_1$ and $C_2$ changes the labeled arrangement but does not change the category sequence. Each sequence was counted $2!$ times, giving $4!/2!=12$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Why is the answer not simply 1/12?</summary>

The 12 sequences are only the favorable ones. There are $3^4$ category sequences overall, and they are not equally likely because the category probabilities differ. We sum the correctly weighted favorable outcomes; we do not choose count patterns uniformly.

</details>
