# Understand the conditional answer instead of guessing one third

For the first-six roll $X$, we found $P(X\text{ divisible by }3\mid X\text{ even})=625/2821\approx0.221553$. The answer is below one third because the earliest even stopping times, 2 and 4, are more likely than 6.

## Simplify the ratio before inserting numbers

Using $P(X\text{ even})=pq/(1-q^2)$,

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\text{ divisible by }3\mid X\text{ even})
&=\frac{pq^5/(1-q^6)}{pq/(1-q^2)}\\
&=\frac{q^4(1-q^2)}{1-q^6}.
\end{aligned}$$

Factor $1-q^6=(1-q^2)(1+q^2+q^4)$ to get

$$\boxed{\frac{q^4}{1+q^2+q^4}.}$$

At $q=5/6$, multiplying numerator and denominator by $6^4$ gives

$$\frac{625}{1296+900+625}=\frac{625}{2821}.$$

## See the unequal weights within blocks of even rolls

Group the even stopping times as $(2,4,6)$, then $(8,10,12)$, and so on. Within each group, their probabilities are proportional to

$$1:q^2:q^4.$$

Only the last value in each group is divisible by three. Its share of each group's probability is $q^4/(1+q^2+q^4)$. All groups have that same share, so their union has it too.

This gives a counting-like explanation with the necessary probability weights attached. We cannot use one third because those three positions do not carry equal mass.

## A useful limiting check

When success is extremely rare, $p\to0$ and $q\to1$, the weights $1,q^2,q^4$ become nearly equal. The conditional probability then approaches $1/3$. For the die's nonzero success probability, later stopping times are still less likely, giving a smaller answer.
