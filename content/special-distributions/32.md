# Sum the probability that the first six is on an even roll

For independent fair-die rolls, let $X$ be the first-six roll. We want $P(X\in\{2,4,6,\ldots\})$. Although half of the positive integers are even in a counting sense, geometric waiting times assign different probabilities to those integers.

## Write the first few qualifying probabilities

With $p=1/6$ and $q=5/6$,

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\text{ even})
&=P(X=2)+P(X=4)+P(X=6)+\cdots\\
&=pq+pq^3+pq^5+\cdots\\
&=pq(1+q^2+q^4+\cdots).
\end{aligned}$$

The ratio between successive terms inside the parentheses is $q^2$, not $q$, because successive even stopping times are two trials apart.

## Apply the geometric-series formula

$$P(X\text{ even})=\frac{pq}{1-q^2}.$$

Factor the denominator:

$$1-q^2=(1-q)(1+q)=p(1+q).$$

Cancel $p$ to obtain

$$\boxed{P(X\text{ even})=\frac q{1+q}=\frac{5/6}{11/6}=\frac5{11}.}$$

The odd-roll probability is the complement, $6/11$.

## Explain why odd rolls have more probability

Pair roll $1$ with roll $2$, roll $3$ with roll $4$, and so on. In each pair, the even-roll probability is $q$ times the preceding odd-roll probability:

$$P(X=2j)=qP(X=2j-1).$$

Because $q<1$, the odd term is larger in every pair. Summing preserves this relation: $P(\text{even})=qP(\text{odd})$. Together with their probabilities totaling one, this again gives $q/(1+q)$.

The calculation demonstrates why a pattern among labels does not establish equal probability: the probability weights, not just the number of labels, determine the answer.
