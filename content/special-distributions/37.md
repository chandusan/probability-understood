# Set up the hurricane-count exercise

The source's classroom model says that the number $X$ of Caribbean hurricanes in one year is Poisson with parameter $13.1$. We want its PMF, the probability of at most eight, the probability of at least ten, and the probability of at most fifteen given at least ten.

## Specify the PMF and support

The stated model gives

$$\boxed{P(X=x)=e^{-13.1}\frac{13.1^x}{x!},\qquad x=0,1,2,\ldots.}$$

The value $13.1$ is a mean annual count in this exercise. It is not presented here as a current empirical estimate or a claim that real hurricane occurrences necessarily satisfy every Poisson-process assumption.

## Translate each question before calculating

“At most eight” includes $0$ through $8$:

$$P(X\le8)=\sum_{x=0}^8e^{-13.1}\frac{13.1^x}{x!}.$$

“At least ten” includes $10,11,\ldots$, so its complement is $0$ through $9$:

$$P(X\ge10)=1-\sum_{x=0}^9e^{-13.1}\frac{13.1^x}{x!}.$$

For the conditional question, let $A=\{X\le15\}$ and $B=\{X\ge10\}$. Their intersection is $10\le X\le15$. Consequently,

$$P(X\le15\mid X\ge10)
=\frac{\sum_{x=10}^{15}e^{-13.1}13.1^x/x!}{P(X\ge10)}.$$

## What conditioning does here

We discard years with fewer than ten hurricanes. Among the remaining model outcomes, we ask what fraction have counts from ten through fifteen. Counts below ten cannot contribute to the numerator, even though they satisfy “at most fifteen” when considered alone.
