# Use the logarithm to make independent sums additive

The cumulant generating function is $K_X(t)=\log m_X(t)$. Its first derivative at zero is the mean, its second is the variance, and its third is the third central moment. The logarithm turns products of MGFs into sums.

## Derive the first two derivatives

Because $m_X(0)=1$,

$$K_X'(t)=\frac{m_X'(t)}{m_X(t)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K_X'(0)=E[X].$$

Differentiate the quotient:

$$K_X''(t)=\frac{m_X''(t)}{m_X(t)}-\left(\frac{m_X'(t)}{m_X(t)}\right)^2.$$

At zero this becomes

$$\boxed{K_X''(0)=E[X^2]-E[X]^2=\operatorname{Var}(X).}$$

The subtraction needed for variance is built into the logarithm's second derivative.

## The third derivative measures asymmetry

A further differentiation gives

$$K_X^{(3)}(0)=E[X^3]-3E[X]E[X^2]+2E[X]^3.$$

Writing $\mu=E[X]$ and expanding $(X-\mu)^3$ shows that this equals $E[(X-\mu)^3]$. Positive and negative deviations keep their signs when cubed, so this moment records an imbalance between the two tails. Standardized skewness divides it by $\operatorname{Var}(X)^{3/2}$ when the variance is positive.

## Why cumulants are useful for sums

For independent variables, $m_{X+Y}=m_Xm_Y$. Hence

$$K_{X+Y}(t)=\log(m_X(t)m_Y(t))=K_X(t)+K_Y(t).$$

Differentiating shows that their means, variances, and higher cumulants add. In particular, additivity of variance here depends on independence.

## Higher cumulants are different from central moments

The fourth cumulant is

$$K_X^{(4)}(0)=E[(X-\mu)^4]-3\operatorname{Var}(X)^2.$$

So the pattern “derivative equals central moment” stops after the third derivative. Also, CGF means **cumulant generating function**, not cumulative distribution function: it is not a CDF and does not represent $P(X\le t)$.
