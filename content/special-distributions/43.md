# Compare the birthday approximation with the exact answer

Under independent uniform birthdays over 365 dates, $X\sim\operatorname{Binom}(500,1/365)$ counts September-first birthdays. We will calculate $P(X\le1)$ exactly and compare it with the Poisson approximation using the same mean.

## Exact answer: zero matches plus one match

Write $p=1/365$ and $q=364/365$. No matches has probability $q^{500}$. Exactly one match has $500$ possible matching people, each corresponding to a disjoint pattern with probability $pq^{499}$. Hence

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\le1)&=q^{500}+500pq^{499}\\
&=\left(\frac{364}{365}\right)^{500}
+500\left(\frac1{365}\right)\left(\frac{364}{365}\right)^{499}\\
&=\boxed{0.602104614\text{ approximately}.}
\end{aligned}$$

## Poisson approximation

With $\lambda=np=500/365$,

$$\boxed{P(X\le1)\approx e^{-\lambda}(1+\lambda)=0.602281184.}$$

The absolute difference is

$$0.602281184-0.602104614\approx0.000176570,$$

or about $0.017657$ percentage points. In this example, the approximation is very close and slightly larger than the exact probability.

## Avoid rounding the parameter too early

The source displays $\lambda\approx1.37$ while giving the result calculated with the more precise $500/365$. If we literally use $1.37$ in the formula, the result is approximately $0.602233494$.

Both are close approximations, but intermediate precision and displayed precision are different. To reproduce the source's $0.6022812$, retain $500/365$ until the final calculation.

## Why the approximation works here

Each person's matching probability is only about $0.00274$, and there are five hundred independent opportunities. These are precisely the many-trials, small-probability conditions motivating the limit. The finite mean $np$ captures how many matches are typical even while individual matches are rare.
