# Prove the probability that a Poisson count is even

Let $X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$. We want to prove that $P(X\text{ is even})=(1+e^{-2\lambda})/2$. Zero is an even integer, so it must be included in the event.

## Write exactly the terms we want

The even counts are $0,2,4,\ldots$, giving

$$P(X\text{ even})=e^{-\lambda}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2j}}{(2j)!}.$$

We need a way to keep the even powers in the exponential series and remove the odd powers.

## Add the series at opposite inputs

Write the expansions side by side:

$$e^{\lambda}=1+\lambda+\frac{\lambda^2}{2!}+\frac{\lambda^3}{3!}+\cdots,$$

$$e^{-\lambda}=1-\lambda+\frac{\lambda^2}{2!}-\frac{\lambda^3}{3!}+\cdots.$$

On addition, every odd power cancels and every even power appears twice:

$$e^{\lambda}+e^{-\lambda}=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2j}}{(2j)!}.$$

Therefore

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\text{ even})
&=e^{-\lambda}\frac{e^{\lambda}+e^{-\lambda}}2\\
&=\boxed{\frac{1+e^{-2\lambda}}2}.
\end{aligned}$$

The complementary odd-count probability is $(1-e^{-2\lambda})/2$.

## An equivalent probability argument using a sign

The variable $(-1)^X$ is $+1$ when $X$ is even and $-1$ when it is odd. Its expectation is therefore

$$E[(-1)^X]=P(\text{even})-P(\text{odd})=e^{-2\lambda},$$

where the final equality follows from the same exponential series. Combining this difference with $P(\text{even})+P(\text{odd})=1$ gives the answer.

## Check the extremes

As $\lambda\to0$, almost all probability is at zero, so the answer tends to one. As $\lambda$ becomes large, $e^{-2\lambda}$ becomes tiny and the even and odd probabilities approach one half. They are not exactly equal for a finite positive $\lambda$.
