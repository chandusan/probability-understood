# Solve the treatment event using a binomial count and its complement

Four independent patients each have probabilities $0.3$ of cure, $0.5$ of reduced pain, and $0.2$ of no change. With corresponding counts $X,Y,Z$, calculate $P(X+Y\ge Z)$. We can collapse the three-category model into a two-category model because the question only compares improvement with no change.

## Identify the one count that determines the event

There are exactly four patients, so $X+Y=4-Z$. Therefore

$$X+Y\ge Z\iff Z\le2.$$

Equality is included: two patients improving and two showing no change satisfy the event. Replacing $\ge$ with $>$ would incorrectly exclude that case.

## Find the distribution of Z

For each patient, “no change” has probability $0.2$ and “some improvement” has probability $0.3+0.5=0.8$. Under independence,

$$Z\sim\operatorname{Bin}(4,0.2).$$

Consequently,

$$P(Z=z)=\binom4z(0.2)^z(0.8)^{4-z}.$$

## Calculate the two excluded cases

The complement of $Z\le2$ is $Z=3$ or $Z=4$:

$$P(Z=3)=\binom43(0.2)^3(0.8)=4(0.008)(0.8)=0.0256,$$

$$P(Z=4)=\binom44(0.2)^4=0.0016.$$

Thus

$$\boxed{P(X+Y\ge Z)=1-0.0256-0.0016=0.9728.}$$

## Verify directly from the multinomial outcomes

Failure means $(X,Y,Z)$ is one of

$$(1,0,3),\quad(0,1,3),\quad(0,0,4).$$

Their probabilities are respectively

$$4(0.3)(0.2)^3=0.0096,$$

$$4(0.5)(0.2)^3=0.016,$$

$$(0.2)^4=0.0016.$$

The first two add to $0.0256$, matching the binomial $Z=3$ term. Grouping “cure” and “reduced pain” into “improvement” merely performs this addition earlier; it does not change the experiment.

<details markdown="1"><summary>What if the question required strictly more improvement than no change?</summary>

Then $4-Z>Z$, so $Z<2$. The probability is $P(Z=0)+P(Z=1)=0.4096+0.4096=0.8192$. The difference $0.1536$ is exactly the tie probability $P(Z=2)$.

</details>
