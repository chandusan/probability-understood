# A complete reference for the main discrete distributions

This table collects the PMFs, possible values, means, and variances from the chapter. Read the experiment and the support together: a formula is useful only after its variable and assumptions match the problem. In particular, the geometric and negative-binomial variables below count total trials, including the final success.

## The distribution reference

Write $q=1-p$. For the waiting-time models assume $0<p\le1$, and let $r$ be a positive integer. For the hypergeometric model, $N$ is population size, $K$ is its number of successes, and $n$ is sample size.

| Distribution | PMF $P(X=x)$ | Support | Mean | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bernoulli | $p^xq^{1-x}$ | $0,1$ | $p$ | $pq$ |
| Binomial | $\binom nxp^xq^{n-x}$ | $0,\ldots,n$ | $np$ | $npq$ |
| Poisson | $e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!$ | $0,1,\ldots$ | $\lambda$ | $\lambda$ |
| Geometric, total trials | $pq^{x-1}$ | $1,2,\ldots$ | $1/p$ | $q/p^2$ |
| Negative binomial, total trials | $\binom{x-1}{r-1}p^rq^{x-r}$ | $r,r+1,\ldots$ | $r/p$ | $rq/p^2$ |
| Discrete uniform on integers $a,\ldots,b$ | $1/m$, where $m=b-a+1$ | $a,\ldots,b$ | $(a+b)/2$ | $(m^2-1)/12$ |
| Hypergeometric | $\binom Kx\binom{N-K}{n-x}/\binom Nn$ | $\max(0,n-N+K),\ldots,\min(n,K)$ | $nK/N$ | $n(K/N)(1-K/N)(N-n)/(N-1)$ |

Every PMF is zero outside its support. The hypergeometric variance formula assumes $N>1$; when $N=1$, any permitted sample count is deterministic and has variance zero. In the discrete-uniform row, $m$ is the number of supported integers; the source calls this quantity $N$ without defining it beside that row. The source leaves the hypergeometric support and moments blank; they are completed here.

## Why the waiting-time means and variances fit together

Waiting for $r$ successes can be split into $r$ independent stretches: wait for the first success, then the next, and continue. Each stretch has a geometric distribution with mean $1/p$ and variance $q/p^2$. Adding the independent stretches gives mean $r/p$ and variance $rq/p^2$.

If $Y$ instead counts failures before success number $r$, then $Y=X-r$. Its mean is $r/p-r=rq/p$, its support starts at 0, and its variance is unchanged. Always check this convention before using a table or software function.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Derive the discrete-uniform variance</summary>

Let $U$ be uniform on $1,\ldots,m$. Using the finite-sum identities,

$$E[U]=\frac1m\frac{m(m+1)}2=\frac{m+1}2,$$

$$E[U^2]=\frac1m\frac{m(m+1)(2m+1)}6=\frac{(m+1)(2m+1)}6.$$

Subtract the square of the mean:

$$\operatorname{Var}(U)=\frac{(m+1)(2m+1)}6-\frac{(m+1)^2}4
=\frac{(m+1)(m-1)}{12}.$$

Moving the support from $1,\ldots,m$ to $a,\ldots,b$ only adds the constant $a-1$, so variance stays $(m^2-1)/12$.

</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Derive the geometric mean and variance from its MGF</summary>

For $X$ counting trials to the first success, start with the entire expectation:

$$M_X(t)=\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}e^{tx}pq^{x-1}
=pe^t\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(qe^t)^j
=\frac{pe^t}{1-qe^t},$$

provided $qe^t<1$. When $p=1$, $X=1$ with certainty and $M_X(t)=e^t$ for every $t$.

For $0<p<1$, take the logarithm to simplify the differentiation:

$$K_X(t)=\log p+t-\log(1-qe^t).$$

The chain rule gives

$$K_X'(t)=1+\frac{qe^t}{1-qe^t}=\frac1{1-qe^t},$$

$$K_X''(t)=\frac{qe^t}{(1-qe^t)^2}.$$

Since $1-q=p$, evaluating at zero gives

$$E[X]=K_X'(0)=\frac1p,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(X)=K_X''(0)=\frac q{p^2}.$$

Why does the second derivative of the logarithm give variance? Because $K''=M''/M-(M'/M)^2$ and $M(0)=1$, so $K''(0)=E[X^2]-E[X]^2$.

</details>
