# Check the Poisson probabilities and derive its moments

For $X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$, $P(X=x)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!$. The exponential power series verifies the total probability and makes the MGF particularly simple.

## Normalize using the exponential series

Recall

$$e^z=\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^x}{x!}.$$

Set $z=\lambda$ and multiply by $e^{-\lambda}$:

$$\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}P(X=x)
=e^{-\lambda}\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^x}{x!}
=e^{-\lambda}e^{\lambda}=1.$$

Thus the factor $e^{-\lambda}$ supplies exactly the normalization that makes the weights $\lambda^x/x!$ total one.

## Derive the MGF

$$\begin{aligned}
m_X(t)&=\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}e^{tx}e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^x}{x!}\\
&=e^{-\lambda}\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda e^t)^x}{x!}\\
&=e^{-\lambda}e^{\lambda e^t}
=\boxed{e^{\lambda(e^t-1)}}.
\end{aligned}$$

It is finite for every real $t$.

## Use the cumulant generating function

Taking logarithms gives

$$K_X(t)=\lambda(e^t-1).$$

Both its first and second derivatives equal $\lambda e^t$. Therefore

$$\boxed{E[X]=\lambda,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(X)=\lambda.}$$

Equality of mean and variance is a feature of this model, not a property of all counts.

## Independent Poisson counts add

If $X$ and $Y$ are independent Poisson variables with means $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$, their MGFs multiply to

$$e^{\lambda_1(e^t-1)}e^{\lambda_2(e^t-1)}
=e^{(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)(e^t-1)}.$$

This is the MGF of a Poisson variable with parameter $\lambda_1+\lambda_2$. Thus $X+Y$ has that distribution. The expected counts add, while independence permits us to identify the entire sum distribution this way.
