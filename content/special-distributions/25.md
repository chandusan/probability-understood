# Derive the binomial third central moment

For $X\sim\operatorname{Binom}(n,p)$, find its cumulant generating function and $E[(X-E[X])^3]$. The MGF is $(1-p+pe^t)^n$, so taking its logarithm simplifies the differentiation.

## Begin with the whole function

Write $q=1-p$. Then

$$\boxed{K_X(t)=\log m_X(t)=n\log(q+pe^t).}$$

This is a **cumulant generating function**; the source's phrase “cumulant distribution function” should be read as “cumulant generating function.”

## Introduce a short expression to expose the derivatives

Let

$$r(t)=\frac{pe^t}{q+pe^t}.$$

Differentiating the logarithm gives $K_X'(t)=nr(t)$. Use the quotient rule to differentiate $r$:

$$r'(t)=\frac{pe^t(q+pe^t)-pe^t(pe^t)}{(q+pe^t)^2}
=\frac{pq e^t}{(q+pe^t)^2}.$$

Since $1-r(t)=q/(q+pe^t)$, this is

$$r'(t)=r(t)(1-r(t)).$$

Therefore

$$K_X''(t)=nr(t)(1-r(t)).$$

## Differentiate once more

Using the product rule,

$$\begin{aligned}
K_X^{(3)}(t)
&=n\{r'(t)(1-r(t))-r(t)r'(t)\}\\
&=nr'(t)(1-2r(t))\\
&=nr(t)(1-r(t))(1-2r(t)).
\end{aligned}$$

At zero, $r(0)=p/(q+p)=p$. The third cumulant equals the third central moment, so

$$\boxed{E[(X-np)^3]=np(1-p)(1-2p).}$$

## Interpret the sign

When $p=1/2$, the moment is zero, consistent with symmetry about $n/2$. When $p<1/2$, it is positive: the distribution has a longer tail toward larger success counts. When $p>1/2$, it is negative.

This is an unstandardized measure. For $0<p<1$, dividing by $(npq)^{3/2}$ gives skewness $(1-2p)/\sqrt{npq}$.
