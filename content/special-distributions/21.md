# Solve the true-or-false guessing exercise

A student guesses on ten true-or-false questions, choosing independently and giving each answer probability $1/2$. Let $X$ be the number answered correctly. Find the probability of exactly six correct answers, and the probability of passing when a score of at least 60% is required.

## Identify the random variable and its distribution

Each question is a Bernoulli trial with success meaning a correct answer. There are ten fixed trials, and the stated independence and equal guessing probabilities give

$$X\sim\operatorname{Binom}(10,1/2).$$

All $2^{10}=1024$ answer-correctness sequences have equal probability. A count of $k$ correct answers has $\binom{10}{k}$ such sequences.

## Part a: exactly six correct answers

Choose the six questions answered correctly. Every choice determines which four were answered incorrectly, so

$$\boxed{P(X=6)=\binom{10}{6}\left(\frac12\right)^{10}
=\frac{210}{1024}=0.205078125.}$$

The coefficient counts the locations of the six correct answers; it does not assume that the first six questions were correct.

## Part b: a passing grade

At least 60% of ten means at least six correct answers:

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\ge6)&=\frac{\binom{10}6+\binom{10}7+\binom{10}8+\binom{10}9+\binom{10}{10}}{1024}\\
&=\frac{210+120+45+10+1}{1024}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{386}{1024}=0.376953125.}
\end{aligned}$$

Thus the probability of passing by guessing is about 37.70%, larger than the probability of exactly six correct.

## Check using symmetry

A fair guess gives $P(X=k)=P(X=10-k)$. The outcomes below five correct and above five correct therefore have equal total probability. Removing the middle outcome gives

$$P(X\ge6)=\frac{1-P(X=5)}2
=\frac{1-252/1024}{2}=\frac{386}{1024}.$$

The middle probability prevents this tail from being exactly one half.
