# Calculate Poisson probabilities without repeated factorials

For the annual hurricane count $X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(13.1)$, every PMF value is $p(x)=e^{-13.1}13.1^x/x!$. A simple recurrence lets us build the probability table from its first entry.

## Derive the recurrence from adjacent terms

For any Poisson parameter $\lambda$,

$$\frac{p(x+1)}{p(x)}
=\frac{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{x+1}/(x+1)!}{e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!}
=\frac{\lambda}{x+1}.$$

Thus

$$\boxed{p(0)=e^{-\lambda},\qquad p(x+1)=p(x)\frac{\lambda}{x+1}.}$$

For the example,

$$p(0)=e^{-13.1}\approx0.000002045,$$

$$p(1)=13.1p(0)\approx0.000026793,$$

$$p(2)=\frac{13.1}{2}p(1)\approx0.000175491.$$

## Use the ratio to locate the largest probability

The probabilities increase while $\lambda/(x+1)>1$ and decrease after that ratio drops below one. With $\lambda=13.1$, the increase continues from $p(12)$ to $p(13)$, because $13.1/13>1$. The next step decreases because $13.1/14<1$.

Thus the most likely count is thirteen, even though the mean is $13.1$.

## Keep enough precision for accumulated probabilities

Rounding each very small term too early can lose probability. In practice, retain the unrounded numbers during the recurrence and round only the displayed results. This matters when summing a lower tail and then subtracting it from one.

For the requested calculations, the exact finite-sum expressions remain the definitions. Recurrence, software, and a table are different evaluation methods for those same sums.
