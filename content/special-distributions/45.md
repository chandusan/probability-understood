# Wait until the rth success

Repeat independent Bernoulli trials with success probability $p>0$ until the $r$th success, where $r$ is a positive integer. Let $X$ be the total number of trials used, including the final successful trial. This is the negative binomial waiting-time model.

## Establish the support first

At least $r$ trials are needed to obtain $r$ successes. Failures can prolong the experiment without a fixed upper limit, so

$$X\in\{r,r+1,r+2,\ldots\}.$$

For $r=1$, this is the geometric experiment. Here $r$ is the target number of successes, not a number of trials fixed in advance.

## Translate X = x into two requirements

To finish on trial $x$:

1. Among the first $x-1$ trials there must be exactly $r-1$ successes.
2. Trial $x$ must be a success.

The first condition guarantees that the target was not achieved earlier. The last success completes it exactly at trial $x$.

The first $x-1$ trials have a binomial success count, and the last trial is independent of them. Therefore

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X=x)
&=\binom{x-1}{r-1}p^{r-1}(1-p)^{(x-1)-(r-1)}p\\
&=\boxed{\binom{x-1}{r-1}p^r(1-p)^{x-r}.}
\end{aligned}$$

## Why not choose r positions out of x?

The factor $\binom xr$ would allow the last trial to be a failure. Such a sequence would have obtained its $r$th success earlier and would already have stopped. The final position is fixed, leaving only $r-1$ success positions to choose among the first $x-1$.

## State the convention

Some references define the negative binomial variable as failures before the $r$th success. That variable is $Y=X-r$ and begins at zero. In this chapter, $X$ counts all trials and begins at $r$. Both conventions are valid, but their PMFs must be paired with the correct random variable.
