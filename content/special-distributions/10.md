# Find the uniform mean with the MGF—and see the extra work

For $X$ uniform on $[0,1]$, the MGF is $(e^t-1)/t$ for $t\ne0$, with $m_X(0)=1$. We want to recover $E[X]=m_X'(0)$ without treating an undefined quotient as a value.

## Differentiate the full expression

For $t\ne0$, the quotient rule gives

$$m_X'(t)=\frac{t e^t-(e^t-1)}{t^2}=\frac{t e^t-e^t+1}{t^2}.$$

At zero, numerator and denominator both tend to zero. To evaluate the limit, differentiate numerator and denominator for L'Hôpital's rule:

$$\frac{d}{dt}(te^t-e^t+1)=e^t+te^t-e^t=te^t,$$

$$\frac{d}{dt}t^2=2t.$$

Therefore

$$\lim_{t\to0}m_X'(t)=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{te^t}{2t}=\frac12.$$

The MGF is smooth near zero, so this limit agrees with its derivative there.

## A direct derivative-at-zero calculation

The definition of the derivative gives a second route:

$$m_X'(0)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{m_X(h)-m_X(0)}h
=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^h-1-h}{h^2}.$$

Using $e^h=1+h+h^2/2+O(h^3)$, the quotient tends to $1/2$. This route explicitly uses the known value $m_X(0)=1$.

## Compare with the simpler expectation calculation

$$E[X]=\int_0^1x\,dx=\left[\frac{x^2}2\right]_0^1=\frac12.$$

The MGF method is correct, but it creates more algebra for this particular problem. Its strength appears when a closed-form MGF makes repeated moments or independent sums easy to handle.

L'Hôpital's rule is one option here, not a logical necessity: a power-series argument or differentiation under the bounded integral also works.
