# Derive the two geometric tail probabilities

Roll a fair die until the first six and let $X$ count rolls. With $p=1/6$ and $q=5/6$, we will solve $P(X\le5)$ and $P(X\ge10)$ while showing why their exponents differ.

## At most five rolls: add the five possible stopping times

The first six may occur on roll 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5. These are mutually exclusive, so

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\le5)&=p+pq+pq^2+pq^3+pq^4\\
&=p\frac{1-q^5}{1-q}\\
&=1-q^5,
\end{aligned}$$

where the last step uses $1-q=p$. Therefore

$$\boxed{P(X\le5)=1-\left(\frac56\right)^5\approx0.598122.}$$

The complement gives the same result more directly: $X>5$ means that none of the first five rolls was a six, with probability $q^5$.

## At least ten rolls: identify the last forbidden early success

The event $X\ge10$ permits the first six on roll ten. It only forbids successes on rolls 1 through 9. Therefore

$$\boxed{P(X\ge10)=\left(\frac56\right)^9\approx0.193807.}$$

Equivalently,

$$P(X\ge10)=1-P(X\le9)=1-(1-q^9)=q^9.$$

## Compare with nearby events

Exactly ten rolls has probability $q^9p\approx0.032301$. More than ten rolls has probability $q^{10}\approx0.161506$. Adding these gives $q^9p+q^{10}=q^9(p+q)=q^9$, as required for “at least ten.”

<details markdown="1"><summary>Check: is at least ten rolls the complement of at most ten?</summary>

No. The complement of $X\le10$ is $X>10$, which for integer $X$ means $X\ge11$. The complement of $X\ge10$ is $X\le9$.

</details>
