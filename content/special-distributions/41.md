# Derive Poisson as a limit of rare independent successes

Suppose $X_n\sim\operatorname{Binom}(n,p_n)$, where $p_n=\lambda/n$ and $\lambda>0$ is fixed. As the number of trials increases, each trial becomes rarer while the mean count $np_n=\lambda$ stays unchanged. We will derive the limiting probability of a fixed count $k$.

## Start with the complete binomial probability

$$P(X_n=k)=\binom nk\left(\frac\lambda n\right)^k
\left(1-\frac\lambda n\right)^{n-k}.$$

Separate the parts whose limits we know:

$$P(X_n=k)=\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}
\underbrace{\frac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{n^k}}_{\text{counting correction}}
\underbrace{\left(1-\frac\lambda n\right)^n}_{\text{no-success limit}}
\underbrace{\left(1-\frac\lambda n\right)^{-k}}_{\text{fixed-endpoint correction}}.$$

Here $k$ remains fixed while $n$ grows. For $k=0$, the empty counting product equals one.

## Take the limits one factor at a time

The counting correction is

$$1\left(1-\frac1n\right)\cdots\left(1-\frac{k-1}{n}\right)\longrightarrow1.$$

It has only a fixed number of factors. Also,

$$\left(1-\frac\lambda n\right)^n\longrightarrow e^{-\lambda},\qquad
\left(1-\frac\lambda n\right)^{-k}\longrightarrow1.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{P(X_n=k)\longrightarrow e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!}.}$$

This is the Poisson PMF.

## What the approximation preserves

The binomial mean is $np=\lambda$, and its variance is $np(1-p)=\lambda(1-p)$. When $p$ is small, that variance is close to the Poisson variance $\lambda$.

The approximation requires many independent opportunities with small success probability. A large $n$ alone is insufficient: a binomial count with $p=0.8$ does not fit the rare-success argument.

Poisson assigns some probability above $n$, whereas the original binomial cannot exceed $n$. In a good approximation those impossible-count probabilities are negligible for the question being asked. Whenever an exact binomial calculation is easy, it can also be used to check the approximation.
