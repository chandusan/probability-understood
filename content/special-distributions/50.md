# Interpret every part of the hypergeometric formula

For a uniform sample of $n$ distinct objects without replacement from $N=a+b$ objects, let $X$ count the $a$ category-A objects selected. The PMF is a ratio of counts, and its support records both sample-size limits and population limits.

## The complete probability model

$$\boxed{P(X=x)=\frac{\binom ax\binom b{n-x}}{\binom{a+b}{n}},\qquad
\max(0,n-b)\le x\le\min(n,a).}$$

The parameters satisfy $a,b\ge0$, $N=a+b$, and $0\le n\le N$, all integers. The allowed values $x$ are integers; all other values have probability zero.

| Factor | What it counts |
|---|---|
| $\binom ax$ | Which A objects are selected |
| $\binom b{n-x}$ | Which B objects are selected |
| $\binom Nn$ | Every possible sample of the required size |

## Explain both endpoints

The upper limit is $\min(n,a)$: we cannot select more A objects than either the sample can hold or the population contains.

The lower limit is $\max(0,n-b)$: counts cannot be negative, and if B objects cannot fill the whole sample, the remaining places must be A.

For example, with $a=7$, $b=3$, and $n=6$, the support is $3,4,5,6$. Zero, one, and two A objects are impossible because there are only three B objects available.

## Derive the mean without a complicated sum

Let $I_j$ indicate whether draw $j$ selects A. Each draw has marginal probability $a/N$ by symmetry, even though the draws are dependent. Since $X=\sum_{j=1}^nI_j$,

$$\boxed{E[X]=n\frac aN.}$$

Linearity of expectation does not require independence.

For $N>1$, the variance is

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X)=n\frac aN\frac bN\frac{N-n}{N-1}.}$$

The final factor reduces the binomial variance because sampling without replacement limits how much the category count can fluctuate. When the whole population is sampled, $n=N$, the variance is zero: $X=a$ is then known exactly.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Derive the finite-population variance correction</summary>

Write $p=a/N$. For two different draws $i,j$, uniform sampling without replacement gives

$$E[I_iI_j]=P(I_i=1,I_j=1)=\frac aN\frac{a-1}{N-1}.$$

Therefore

$$\operatorname{Cov}(I_i,I_j)=\frac{a(a-1)}{N(N-1)}-\frac{a^2}{N^2}
=-\frac{p(1-p)}{N-1}.$$

Selecting an A object slightly reduces the availability of A objects on the other draw, explaining the negative sign. There are $n$ individual variances and $n(n-1)$ ordered covariance terms in the variance of the sum. Hence

$$\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X)
&=np(1-p)-n(n-1)\frac{p(1-p)}{N-1}\\
&=np(1-p)\frac{N-n}{N-1}.
\end{aligned}$$

This derivation assumes $N>1$. If $N=1$, any permissible fixed-size sample has a deterministic category count and variance zero.

</details>
