# Choose a distribution by describing the experiment

A special distribution is a reusable probability model for a particular kind of experiment. The purpose of this chapter is to recognize that experiment, derive its probabilities, and understand what its parameters mean before using a formula.

## What are we actually measuring?

Suppose independent attempts succeed with probability $p$. Three different questions produce three different random variables:

| Question | Random variable | Distribution |
|---|---|---|
| Does one attempt succeed? | $0$ or $1$ | Bernoulli |
| How many successes in $n$ attempts? | A count between $0$ and $n$ | Binomial |
| On which attempt does the first success occur? | A waiting time $1,2,\ldots$ | Geometric |

The same success/failure mechanism can therefore produce different distributions. The definition of the random variable matters as much as the physical experiment.

## The other models in this chapter

A negative binomial variable counts trials until a specified number of successes. A hypergeometric variable counts a category in a sample drawn **without replacement**. A multinomial vector counts several categories across a fixed number of independent trials. A Poisson variable models an event count with a parameter equal to its mean count.

For each model, ask four questions: what values can occur; what assumptions make the model appropriate; why does its probability formula have those factors; and how do we translate the event being asked about?

## Why begin with moment generating functions?

Means and variances describe a distribution through weighted averages. A moment generating function packages those averages into one function. Differentiating it retrieves moments; multiplying such functions handles independent sums. We will derive those facts rather than treat this package as a new unexplained formula.

**Study habit:** write the random variable in a sentence before writing its distribution. “$X$ counts heads in seven independent fair tosses” determines the support, assumptions, and parameters immediately.
