# Find the full earthquake-insurance distribution

Suppose 30% of homeowners have earthquake insurance. Select four homeowners, and let $X$ count those insured. We use independent selections with a common insured probability $0.3$, or the usual approximation when four are sampled from a very large population.

## Part a: the probability distribution

Under this model, $X\sim\operatorname{Binom}(4,0.3)$, so

$$P(X=x)=\binom4x(0.3)^x(0.7)^{4-x},\qquad x=0,1,2,3,4.$$

| Insured homeowners $x$ | Calculation | Probability |
|---|---|---:|
| 0 | $0.7^4$ | 0.2401 |
| 1 | $4(0.3)(0.7)^3$ | 0.4116 |
| 2 | $6(0.3)^2(0.7)^2$ | 0.2646 |
| 3 | $4(0.3)^3(0.7)$ | 0.0756 |
| 4 | $0.3^4$ | 0.0081 |

The entries sum to one.

## Parts b and c: two insured, and the bars

Exactly two has probability $\boxed{0.2646}$. The factor $6=\binom42$ accounts for which two of the four people are insured.

The bar plot puts the number insured on the horizontal axis and its probability on the vertical axis. The bar heights are exactly the probabilities in the table.

## Part d: the most likely count

The highest probability is $0.4116$ at $x=1$. Thus the unique mode is $\boxed{1}$. The mean is $4(0.3)=1.2$; a most likely value and an average are different concepts.

## Part e: at least two insured

$$\begin{aligned}
P(X\ge2)&=P(X=2)+P(X=3)+P(X=4)\\
&=0.2646+0.0756+0.0081=\boxed{0.3483}.
\end{aligned}$$

Equivalently, $1-P(X=0)-P(X=1)=1-0.2401-0.4116=0.3483$.

If sampling is strictly without replacement from a specified finite population, the exact model is hypergeometric; the 30% figure alone does not specify that population's size.
