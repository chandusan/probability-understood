# Turn a distance question into an area question

A point is chosen uniformly from a square of side length 1. Let $X$ be its distance from the lower-left corner. To find the cumulative distribution function $F(x)=P(X\le x)$, we must identify which points give a distance at most a specified number $x$.

## State the experiment precisely

Place the square on coordinate axes:

$$
S=\{(u,v):0\le u\le1,\ 0\le v\le1\}.
$$

The lower-left corner is $O=(0,0)$. If the sampled point is $(U,V)$, the Pythagorean theorem gives the random variable

$$
X=\sqrt{U^2+V^2}.
$$

Uppercase $X$ denotes the random distance; lowercase $x$ is a fixed threshold that we choose when evaluating the CDF. The event $X\le x$ contains all outcomes whose distance meets that threshold.

**Uniform selection means that a region's probability equals its area divided by the square's total area.** Since the square has area $1\times1=1$, this becomes

$$
P((U,V)\in A)=\frac{\operatorname{area}(A)}{1}
=\operatorname{area}(A)
$$

for a region $A$ inside the square. This assumption is essential: if points were sampled more often near one corner, plain area would no longer give the probability.

We are not dividing counts of individual points. Both the square and many of its subregions contain infinitely many points. Area is the appropriate probability measure for this uniform experiment.

## Translate the event into a picture

For $x\ge0$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\{X\le x\}
&=\{\sqrt{U^2+V^2}\le x\}\\
&=\{U^2+V^2\le x^2\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The inequality $u^2+v^2\le x^2$ describes a disk of radius $x$ centered at $O$. Our outcomes must also lie in $S$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{F(x)=\operatorname{area}\bigl(S\cap\{u^2+v^2\le x^2\}\bigr)}.
$$

The shaded part of each source diagram is precisely this intersection. The disk can extend outside the square, but those outside points are not outcomes of our experiment and contribute no probability.

## Why the picture changes at 1 and at $\sqrt2$

For $0\le x\le1$, the part of the disk in the first quadrant fits inside the square. The shaded region is a quarter disk.

For $1<x<\sqrt2$, the circle crosses the top and right sides. A quarter disk would include some area outside the square, so its full area would overcount the probability. The shaded region is the square with a curved region near its upper-right corner excluded.

The farthest point is $(1,1)$, whose distance is

$$
\sqrt{1^2+1^2}=\sqrt2.
$$

For $x\ge\sqrt2$, every point in the square qualifies and $F(x)=1$. For $x<0$, no nonnegative distance qualifies and $F(x)=0$.

## Uniform points do not produce uniform distances

At $x=1/2$, the qualifying area is a quarter disk of radius $1/2$:

$$
F(1/2)=\frac{\pi(1/2)^2}{4}=\frac{\pi}{16}\approx0.19635.
$$

At $x=1$, the probability is $\pi/4\approx0.78540$. Doubling the radius from $1/2$ to 1 multiplies this area by four. Equal intervals of distance therefore need not have equal probabilities, even though equal-area regions in the square do.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Check: does the circle boundary contribute extra probability?</summary>

No. A circle has zero area in the plane, so $P(X=x)=0$ for every fixed $x$. Consequently $P(X<x)=P(X\le x)$ in this example. Including the boundary makes the CDF convention precise without changing the shaded area's probability.
