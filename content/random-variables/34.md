# Count physical faces when die labels repeat

Repeated labels do not remove physical faces. To derive the sum distribution of these unusual dice, start with the 36 equally likely pairs of faces, then collect the pairs that produce each numerical sum.

## State the experiment and its assumptions

Die one has labels $1,2,2,3,3,4$. Die two has labels $1,3,4,5,6,8$. Roll both dice once, independently, with each of the six physical faces on each die equally likely. Let

$$
A=\text{number on die one},\qquad
B=\text{number on die two},\qquad X=A+B.
$$

The questions are to specify the sample space and derive the PMF of $X$. Fair physical faces and independent rolls are the assumptions that justify a common denominator of 36.

## Give the sample space without merging outcomes

Temporarily distinguish the repeated faces of die one as $2_a,2_b,3_a,3_b$. The subscripts identify faces; both $2_a$ and $2_b$ still contribute the number two to a sum. An elementary sample space is

$$
\Omega=\{1,2_a,2_b,3_a,3_b,4\}
\times\{1,3,4,5,6,8\}.
$$

There are $6\times6=36$ ordered pairs. Each has probability $(1/6)(1/6)=1/36$. Order identifies the dice: the first entry always belongs to die one.

It is also possible to record only the numerical pair $(A,B)$. That produces $4\times6=24$ distinct numerical pairs, but they are **not equally likely**. For example,

$$
P(A=1,B=3)=\frac1{36},\qquad
P(A=2,B=3)=\frac2{36},
$$

because two different physical faces of die one show two. Counting each of those 24 numerical pairs once would give the wrong probability model.

## List every sum

Each cell represents one physical face pair and shows its sum:

| Die one face / die two label | 1 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $1$ | 2 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 9 |
| $2_a$ | 3 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 10 |
| $2_b$ | 3 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 10 |
| $3_a$ | 4 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 11 |
| $3_b$ | 4 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 11 |
| $4$ | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 12 |

The repeated rows are necessary: they represent different elementary outcomes even though their sums match cell by cell.

For example, a sum of five comes from numerical pairs $(1,4)$, $(2,3)$, and $(4,1)$. These correspond to $1+2+1=4$ physical face pairs, so $P(X=5)=4/36$.

## Collect the cells into the PMF

| Sum $x$ | Number of cells | $p_X(x)=P(X=x)$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2 | 1 | $1/36$ |
| 3 | 2 | $2/36$ |
| 4 | 3 | $3/36$ |
| 5 | 4 | $4/36$ |
| 6 | 5 | $5/36$ |
| 7 | 6 | $6/36$ |
| 8 | 5 | $5/36$ |
| 9 | 4 | $4/36$ |
| 10 | 3 | $3/36$ |
| 11 | 2 | $2/36$ |
| 12 | 1 | $1/36$ |

Set $p_X(x)=0$ for all other values. The counts sum to

$$
1+2+3+4+5+6+5+4+3+2+1=36,
$$

so all elementary outcomes are accounted for and the masses sum to one.

## Notice what the result does and does not identify

This is exactly the sum distribution of two ordinary fair dice labeled one through six. The match follows from the explicit counts, even though the individual dice have different label distributions. Observing only the sum distribution therefore does not uniquely determine how the two dice are labeled.

## Check your understanding

Why is $P(X=3)=2/36$ rather than $1/36$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Count faces behind the numerical pair</summary>

The only numerical pair producing three is $(2,1)$, but there are two elementary outcomes: $(2_a,1)$ and $(2_b,1)$. Each has probability $1/36$, so their disjoint union has probability $2/36$.

</details>
