# CDF differences measure precisely defined intervals

Increasing a cutoff never removes an outcome from the event being counted. This proves that every CDF is nondecreasing and explains exactly which interval is represented by the difference of two CDF values.

## Split the larger event into disjoint pieces

Take two real numbers $a<b$. Every outcome with $X\le b$ falls into exactly one of two cases: either $X\le a$, or $a<X\le b$. Thus

$$
\{X\le b\}=\{X\le a\}\cup\{a<X\le b\}.
$$

The two events on the right are disjoint. In particular, the first includes $X=a$, while the second excludes it. Adding their probabilities gives

$$
F(b)=F(a)+P(a<X\le b).
$$

Since the added term cannot be negative,

$$
\boxed{F(b)\ge F(a).}
$$

This is **nondecreasing**, not necessarily strictly increasing. If there is no probability in $(a,b]$, the two CDF values are equal. In the four-value distribution at $3,5,8,10$, for example, $F(5)=F(7)=0.5$.

## Subtraction tells us which endpoints survive

Rearrange the same equation:

$$
\boxed{P(a<X\le b)=F(b)-F(a).}
$$

Starting from all probability at or below $b$, subtract all probability at or below $a$. This removes the mass at $a$ and keeps the mass at $b$. The endpoint pattern follows from the event subtraction; it is not an arbitrary convention.

For the table with $p(3)=0.3$, $p(5)=0.2$, $p(8)=0.3$, and $p(10)=0.2$,

$$
P(5<X\le8)=F(8)-F(5)=0.8-0.5=0.3.
$$

Only eight qualifies. Using this difference for $P(5\le X\le8)$ would incorrectly discard the additional mass $0.2$ at five.

## Use left limits to include or exclude an endpoint

Write

$$
F(c-)=\lim_{u\uparrow c}F(u)=P(X<c).
$$

The minus sign means approaching $c$ from smaller cutoffs. It does not mean subtracting one or any fixed amount. The next lesson proves the equality to $P(X<c)$.

For $a<b$, the four interval formulas are

| Requested event | Probability |
|---|---|
| $a<X\le b$ | $F(b)-F(a)$ |
| $a\le X\le b$ | $F(b)-F(a-)$ |
| $a<X<b$ | $F(b-)-F(a)$ |
| $a\le X<b$ | $F(b-)-F(a-)$ |

Each row follows the same rule: choose the cumulative probability that includes exactly the desired upper endpoint behavior, then remove everything below the desired interval. To keep $a$, subtract only values strictly below $a$.

For example, $F(5-)=0.3$ and $F(8-)=0.5$, so

$$
P(5\le X\le8)=0.8-0.3=0.5,
$$

$$
P(5<X<8)=0.5-0.5=0.
$$

There are no supported values strictly between five and eight.

## Tail probabilities use the same distinction

The complement of $X>c$ is $X\le c$, whereas the complement of $X\ge c$ is $X<c$. Therefore

$$
P(X>c)=1-F(c),
\qquad P(X\ge c)=1-F(c-).
$$

When there is no mass at an endpoint, its left limit equals its CDF value, and the corresponding strict and inclusive probabilities agree. This applies at many points of a discrete distribution and at every point of a distribution with a density.
