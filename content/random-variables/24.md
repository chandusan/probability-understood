# Expand the product rule in the distance CDF

The difficult CDF branch contains a square root and a product involving an inverse tangent. On $1<x<\sqrt2$, we can differentiate these pieces carefully, keeping the product-rule terms separate until the chain rule reveals the cancellation.

## Identify the terms and their dependencies

Write the branch as

$$
F(x)=\sqrt{x^2-1}
+\frac\pi4x^2
-x^2\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}.
$$

There are three terms. The first is a composition: take $x$, form $x^2-1$, then take the square root. The second is a constant times $x^2$. The third is a product of two functions of $x$, with a minus sign in front.

It would be incorrect to treat $\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}$ as a constant while differentiating that product. The angle changes when the radius changes.

## Differentiate the square root using the chain rule

For an inner function $w(x)>0$,

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{w(x)}
=\frac1{2\sqrt{w(x)}}\,w'(x).
$$

Here $w(x)=x^2-1$ and $w'(x)=2x$. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x^2-1}
&=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x^2-1}}(2x)\\
&=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The factor $2x$ is essential: it accounts for the rate at which the expression inside the square root changes.

## Differentiate both factors of the product

Let

$$
q(x)=\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}.
$$

The product rule states $(uv)'=u'v+uv'$. With $u=x^2$ and $v=q(x)$,

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(x^2q(x)\bigr)
=2xq(x)+x^2q'(x).
$$

The minus sign in $F$ applies to **both** terms:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(-x^2q(x)\bigr)
=-2xq(x)-x^2q'(x).
$$

Meanwhile,

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac\pi4x^2\right)=\frac{\pi x}{2}.
$$

Combining the three derivatives gives

$$
\boxed{
F'(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}
+\frac{\pi x}{2}
-2x\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}
-x^2q'(x)}.
$$

This is the expanded line in the source. Every term has a specific origin: square-root derivative, polynomial derivative, first product-rule term, and second product-rule term.

## Finish the calculation without losing a factor

The inverse-tangent chain rule gives

$$
q'(x)
=\frac{1}{1+(\sqrt{x^2-1})^2}
\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}
=\frac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}.
$$

The next lesson explains this simplification in detail. Substituting it here yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
F'(x)
&=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}+\frac{\pi x}{2}
-2x\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}
-\frac{x^2}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}\\
&=\frac{\pi x}{2}-2x\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last term becomes $-x/\sqrt{x^2-1}$ and cancels the first one exactly.

## Why we stated an open interval

The unsimplified terms involve division by $\sqrt{x^2-1}$, which is zero at $x=1$. The calculations above are valid for $x>1$, where those denominators are nonzero. To determine behavior at 1, simplify first and take a limit. The resulting density approaches $\pi/2$, not infinity.
