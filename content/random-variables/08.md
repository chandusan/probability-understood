# Derive the complete probability mass function of a dice sum

For two independent fair six-sided dice, let $X$ be their sum. There are 36 equally likely ordered outcomes. To find $p(x)=P(X=x)$, list the pairs that sum to $x$, count them, and divide by 36. The counts increase from one to six and then decrease back to one.

## Start with the first three sums

For $X=2$, both dice must be 1, so

$$p(2)=P(\{(1,1)\})=\frac1{36}.$$

For $X=3$, the ordered pairs are $(1,2)$ and $(2,1)$:

$$p(3)=\frac2{36}.$$

For $X=4$, the pairs are $(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)$, giving $p(4)=3/36$.

We add the cell probabilities because distinct ordered pairs are mutually exclusive outcomes. Independence of the dice was used to make each cell's probability $1/36$; the events for different pairs need not be called independent.

## List every possible sum and its source outcomes

| Sum $x$ | Ordered pairs with sum $x$ | Count | $p(x)$ |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 2 | $(1,1)$ | 1 | $1/36$ |
| 3 | $(1,2),(2,1)$ | 2 | $2/36$ |
| 4 | $(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)$ | 3 | $3/36$ |
| 5 | $(1,4),(2,3),(3,2),(4,1)$ | 4 | $4/36$ |
| 6 | $(1,5),(2,4),(3,3),(4,2),(5,1)$ | 5 | $5/36$ |
| 7 | $(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)$ | 6 | $6/36$ |
| 8 | $(2,6),(3,5),(4,4),(5,3),(6,2)$ | 5 | $5/36$ |
| 9 | $(3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3)$ | 4 | $4/36$ |
| 10 | $(4,6),(5,5),(6,4)$ | 3 | $3/36$ |
| 11 | $(5,6),(6,5)$ | 2 | $2/36$ |
| 12 | $(6,6)$ | 1 | $1/36$ |

For all other real values, $p(x)=0$. This includes numbers between the integers: the dice cannot produce a sum of 7.5.

## Explain the rising and falling counts

For an integer $x$ from 2 through 7, the first die can show $1,2,\ldots,x-1$. Its partner must then show $x-1,x-2,\ldots,1$, respectively. All these values are between 1 and 6, so there are $x-1$ pairs.

For an integer $x$ from 8 through 12, the first die cannot be too small: its partner would need to exceed 6. The first die can range from $x-6$ through 6, giving

$$6-(x-6)+1=13-x$$

choices. Once the first face is chosen, the second is fixed by the required sum.

Therefore

$$
p(x)=\begin{cases}
(x-1)/36,&x\in\{2,3,4,5,6,7\},\\
(13-x)/36,&x\in\{8,9,10,11,12\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

## Check the total and calculate an interval event

The numerator counts add to

$$1+2+3+4+5+6+5+4+3+2+1=36,$$

so the masses sum to one. Every original outcome appears in exactly one row.

For example, “the sum is between 6 and 8 inclusive” consists of the disjoint events $X=6$, $X=7$, and $X=8$:

$$P(6\le X\le8)=\frac5{36}+\frac6{36}+\frac5{36}=\frac49.$$

## Check your understanding

What is $P(X\ge10)$? Why is dividing three possible sums by eleven possible sums incorrect?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the reasoning</summary>

$P(X\ge10)=p(10)+p(11)+p(12)=(3+2+1)/36=1/6$. The three sums 10, 11, and 12 do not carry equal weight to every other sum. The equally likely objects are the 36 ordered die outcomes, not the 11 sum labels.

</details>
