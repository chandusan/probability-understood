# Normalize an infinite PMF, then compute its tail

An infinite list of possible values can still have total probability one. The normalization constant adjusts all the proposed masses together; once it is known, a tail probability can often be found by subtracting a few terms from one.

## State the complete model

Let $X$ be the number of pages a user views during a website session before leaving. The proposed PMF is

$$
p(x)=c\,x\left(\frac13\right)^x,
\qquad x=1,2,3,\ldots,
$$

with $p(x)=0$ elsewhere. Thus the model assumes every session includes at least one page. The questions are (a) which value of $c$ makes this a valid PMF, and (b) what is $P(X>2)$?

The factor $x$ means this is not simply a geometric series. Its first unnormalized terms are

$$
\frac13,\quad\frac29,\quad\frac3{27},\quad\frac4{81},\quad\ldots.
$$

We must sum this weighted series before choosing $c$.

## Part (a): derive the required series

For $0<r<1$, define

$$
T(r)=r+2r^2+3r^3+4r^4+\cdots.
$$

This series converges: the ratio of successive terms is $r(x+1)/x$, which tends to $r<1$. Multiply by $r$ to shift the powers one place:

$$
rT(r)=r^2+2r^3+3r^4+\cdots.
$$

Subtract the second convergent series from the first. The coefficient of every positive power becomes one:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1-r)T(r)
&=r+(2-1)r^2+(3-2)r^3+\cdots\\
&=r+r^2+r^3+\cdots\\
&=\frac{r}{1-r}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Dividing by $1-r$ gives the identity

$$
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}xr^x=\frac{r}{(1-r)^2}.
$$

At $r=1/3$, this becomes

$$
\sum_{x=1}^{\infty}x\left(\frac13\right)^x
=\frac{1/3}{(2/3)^2}=\frac34.
$$

Normalization requires $c(3/4)=1$, hence

$$
\boxed{c=\frac43,\qquad p(x)=\frac{4x}{3^{x+1}}\quad(x=1,2,\ldots).}
$$

Every mass is nonnegative and the sum is one, so both PMF requirements hold. The constant $c$ may exceed one because it is a scale factor, not itself an event probability.

## Part (b): use the small complement

The complement of $X>2$ is $X\le2$, which here contains exactly one and two. Their masses are

$$
p(1)=\frac49,\qquad p(2)=\frac8{27}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X>2)&=1-p(1)-p(2)\\
&=1-\frac49-\frac8{27}\\
&=\boxed{\frac7{27}\approx0.259259}.
\end{aligned}
$$

“More than two” begins at three. If the question instead asked for at least two, we would remove only $p(1)$, giving $5/9$.

## Check the tail directly

Write every value above two as $x=j+3$, with $j=0,1,2,\ldots$. Using the same series identity,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{x=3}^{\infty}p(x)
&=\frac43r^3\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+3)r^j\\
&=\frac43r^3\left(\frac{r}{(1-r)^2}+\frac3{1-r}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $r=1/3$ gives

$$
\frac4{81}\left(\frac34+\frac92\right)
=\frac4{81}\frac{21}{4}=\frac7{27},
$$

confirming the complement calculation without truncating the infinite tail.

What is the probability of exactly three pages, and why is it smaller than $P(X>2)$?

<summary>Separate one mass from the whole tail</summary>

$P(X=3)=12/81=4/27$. The tail $P(X>2)=7/27$ also includes four, five, and all larger page counts. Their combined probability is $7/27-4/27=1/9$.
