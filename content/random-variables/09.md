# Read the height of each bar as a probability mass

The graph shows the probability mass function of the sum $X$ of two independent fair six-sided dice. A bar at the integer $x$ has height $p(x)=P(X=x)$. The tallest bar occurs at 7 because six ordered die outcomes produce that sum; the end sums 2 and 12 each have only one supporting outcome.

## Connect the graph to the counting table

The bar heights are

$$\frac1{36},\frac2{36},\frac3{36},\frac4{36},\frac5{36},\frac6{36},
\frac5{36},\frac4{36},\frac3{36},\frac2{36},\frac1{36}$$

at $x=2,3,\ldots,12$, respectively. Some useful readings are:

| Sum | Exact height | Approximate height |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 or 12 | $1/36$ | $0.0278$ |
| 4 or 10 | $3/36$ | $0.0833$ |
| 6 or 8 | $5/36$ | $0.1389$ |
| 7 | $6/36$ | $0.1667$ |

The decimal vertical axis on the source graph reaches about $0.18$, just above the maximum probability $1/6$. That axis measures probability, not a count of outcomes.

## Explain the symmetry with an explicit pairing

Replace each die face $i$ by $7-i$. This changes an outcome $(i,j)$ into $(7-i,7-j)$. Its sum changes from $i+j$ to

$$14-(i+j).$$

This is a one-to-one correspondence between equally likely ordered outcomes. Thus sums $x$ and $14-x$ have equal probability: 2 matches 12, 3 matches 11, and so on. Sum 7 stays at the center.

The graph's symmetry therefore comes from the dice model, not merely from drawing equal-looking bars. Loaded dice need not have this same distribution.

## Add heights when an event includes several values

For “the sum is at least 9,” add the bars at 9, 10, 11, and 12:

$$P(X\ge9)=\frac{4+3+2+1}{36}=\frac{10}{36}=\frac5{18}.$$

For “the sum is 6 or 8,” add just those two bars:

$$P(X\in\{6,8\})=\frac5{36}+\frac5{36}=\frac5{18}.$$

These different events happen to have equal probability. One involves four possible sums and the other only two, illustrating again that counting sum labels is insufficient.

## A PMF bar is not a continuous density

The horizontal width of a bar is a drawing choice; its **height** represents the probability of the single value. A wide-looking bar near 7 does not mean that the sum can be 7.2 or 7.8. For the dice sum, every noninteger value has mass zero.

Likewise, drawing a line between bar tops would not create probability at the intermediate values. This is a discrete distribution with masses at specified integers. Later density graphs will use area over intervals instead of pointwise bar heights.

The graph is the theoretical PMF. A histogram from finitely many simulated rolls will usually differ somewhat because observed frequencies fluctuate. The exact fractions come from the 36-outcome model, not from requiring a simulation to produce a perfectly symmetric picture.

## Check your understanding

Is $P(6<X<8)$ the sum of the bars at 6, 7, and 8? What is its value?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the reasoning</summary>

No. The strict inequalities exclude 6 and 8. Since $X$ is integer-valued, only 7 satisfies the condition. Thus $P(6<X<8)=p(7)=6/36=1/6$.

</details>
