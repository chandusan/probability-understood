# Find the distance density from the piecewise CDF

Return to a point chosen uniformly from the unit square, and let $X$ be its distance from the lower-left corner. We have already derived $F(x)$ from shaded area. Its derivative now tells us how much probability is added per unit increase in the distance threshold.

## Start with the complete CDF

The geometric calculation gave

$$
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x<0,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{\pi x^2}{4},&0\le x\le1,\\[5pt]
\sqrt{x^2-1}+x^2\left(\dfrac\pi4-\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}\right),
&1<x<\sqrt2,\\[5pt]
1,&x\ge\sqrt2.
\end{cases}
$$

We differentiate on the interiors of these ranges first. The transition points will need their own checks, since the derivative rules for an open branch do not automatically settle its boundary values.

## Differentiate the simple branches

For $x<0$ and $x>\sqrt2$, the CDF is constant, so $f(x)=0$. Those distances carry no probability.

For $0<x<1$,

$$
f(x)=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\pi x^2}{4}\right)
=\frac\pi4(2x)=\frac{\pi x}{2}.
$$

Geometrically, increasing the radius adds a thin quarter-annulus. Its area per unit of radius approaches the quarter-circle arc length, $\pi x/2$, which agrees with this derivative.

## Organize the harder branch before differentiating

For $1<x<\sqrt2$, set

$$
a=\sqrt{x^2-1},\qquad a'=\frac{x}{a}.
$$

The branch becomes $F(x)=a+x^2(\pi/4-\arctan a)$. Applying the product and chain rules gives

$$
F'(x)
=\frac{x}{a}
+2x\left(\frac\pi4-\arctan a\right)
-x^2\frac{a'}{1+a^2}.
$$

Since $1+a^2=1+(x^2-1)=x^2$,

$$
x^2\frac{a'}{1+a^2}
=x^2\frac{x/a}{x^2}=\frac{x}{a}.
$$

The first and last terms cancel. Therefore

$$
f(x)=2x\left(\frac\pi4-\arctan a\right)
=\frac{\pi x}{2}-2x\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}.
$$

The next two lessons expand these product-rule and chain-rule steps individually, including why the apparently singular terms cancel.

## State the PDF and assign its endpoints

A convenient version of the density is

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x<0,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{\pi x}{2},&0\le x\le1,\\[5pt]
\dfrac{\pi x}{2}-2x\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1},&1<x<\sqrt2,\\[5pt]
0,&x\ge\sqrt2.
\end{cases}}
$$

As $x$ approaches 1 from above, the inverse tangent approaches zero, and the density approaches $\pi/2$, matching the branch from below. At $\sqrt2$, the inverse tangent approaches $\pi/4$, giving density zero. At zero, the first curved branch also approaches zero.

These assignments make the PDF continuous. The CDF is differentiable at these joins as well; the matching derivative limits establish this even though the unsimplified derivative contains individual terms that fail at $x=1$.

The CDF must increase wherever this density is positive. The density itself is free to rise or fall: it measures the rate of accumulation, not the accumulated total. After radius 1, the portion of the circular boundary remaining inside the square shrinks, so the graph of the density need not resemble the increasing CDF graph.

## Check sign and total probability

On the harder range, $0<\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}<\pi/4$. Thus $\pi/2-2\arctan\sqrt{x^2-1}>0$, so the density is nonnegative.

Its area splits into

$$
\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=\frac\pi4,
\qquad
\int_1^{\sqrt2} f(x)\,dx=1-\frac\pi4.
$$

These follow from the corresponding changes in $F$. They add to 1, matching the total square area and confirming that this is a valid PDF.
