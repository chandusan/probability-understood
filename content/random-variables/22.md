# Find the density of a polynomial CDF

The example gives $F_X(x)=(x^2+x^3)/12$ for $0\le x\le2$ and asks for the PDF. We must first complete the CDF outside this interval, then differentiate its branches and verify that the resulting density carries total probability 1.

> Calculus refresher: [the power rule and constant factors](../appendix/calculus.html#the-differentiation-rules-you-need).

## Complete the distribution on the entire number line

At the given endpoints,

$$
F_X(0)=0,\qquad F_X(2)=\frac{4+8}{12}=1.
$$

A CDF is nondecreasing and stays between 0 and 1. For $x<0$, it must satisfy $0\le F_X(x)\le F_X(0)=0$, forcing it to be zero. For $x>2$, it must satisfy $1=F_X(2)\le F_X(x)\le1$, forcing it to be one.

The full CDF is therefore

$$
F_X(x)=
\begin{cases}
0,&x<0,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{x^2+x^3}{12},&0\le x\le2,\\[5pt]
1,&x>2.
\end{cases}
$$

The polynomial describes only the middle branch. Extending it beyond 2 would produce values greater than 1, and extending it to negative values would not preserve the intended CDF.

## Differentiate each branch

On $0<x<2$, the constant factor $1/12$ remains, and the power rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_X(x)=F_X'(x)
&=\frac1{12}\left(\frac{d}{dx}x^2+\frac{d}{dx}x^3\right)\\
&=\frac{2x+3x^2}{12}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Outside this interval the CDF is constant, so its derivative is zero. One valid PDF is

$$
\boxed{
f_X(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2x+3x^2}{12},&0<x<2,\\[5pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$

The numerator factors as $x(2+3x)$, which is nonnegative on the required interval. Thus the candidate satisfies the sign requirement.

## Verify the area and recover the CDF

The density's total integral is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_X(t)\,dt
&=\int_0^2\frac{2t+3t^2}{12}\,dt\\
&=\left[\frac{t^2+t^3}{12}\right]_0^2\\
&=\frac{4+8}{12}-0=1.
\end{aligned}
$$

For an upper endpoint $x$ between 0 and 2, the same antiderivative gives $(x^2+x^3)/12$. Below 0 the accumulated integral is zero; above 2 it is one. Thus integration reproduces every branch of the CDF, confirming that no probability has been omitted.

## Handle the endpoint without inventing a point mass

At $x=2$, the left-hand slope is

$$
\frac{2(2)+3(2)^2}{12}=\frac43,
$$

whereas the right-hand slope is zero. Hence the CDF is not differentiable at 2. This is allowed: the PDF's value at that single point can be assigned arbitrarily without changing an integral. We chose $f_X(2)=0$.

The CDF itself is continuous at 2, so $P(X=2)=0$. The value $F_X(2)=1$ means that all probability has accumulated by 2; it does not put all probability at 2. Also, density heights approaching $4/3$ are legitimate: only the integrated probability must be at most 1.

## Use either function to answer interval questions

For example,

$$
P(1\le X\le2)=F_X(2)-F_X(1)
=1-\frac{1+1}{12}=\frac56.
$$

Integrating $(2x+3x^2)/12$ from 1 to 2 gives the same result. Endpoint inclusion is immaterial because this distribution has a density.
