# Recover a density from its total area and its mean

Suppose $f(x)=a+bx^2$ for $0\le x\le1$, with density zero elsewhere, and we are told $E(X)=3/5$. Find the constants $a$ and $b$. Two unknowns require two constraints: the total probability must be one, and the probability-weighted average must be $3/5$.

## The two integrals answer different questions

Normalization asks for the entire area:

$$
\int_0^1(a+bx^2)\,dx=1.
$$

The mean adds an extra factor $x$:

$$
\int_0^1x(a+bx^2)\,dx=\frac35.
$$

That factor matters. The normalization integral counts probability, while the expectation integral weights probability by its location.

## Translate both constraints into algebra

For normalization,

$$
\left[ax+\frac b3x^3\right]_0^1
=a+\frac b3=1.
$$

For the mean,

$$
\left[\frac a2x^2+\frac b4x^4\right]_0^1
=\frac a2+\frac b4=\frac35.
$$

Multiplying the first equation by $3$ and the second by $20$ gives

$$
3a+b=3,\qquad 10a+5b=12.
$$

Substitute $b=3-3a$:

$$
10a+5(3-3a)=12
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad -5a=-3.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{a=\frac35,\qquad b=\frac65.}
$$

## A solution of equations must still be a density

Our candidate is $f(x)=3/5+(6/5)x^2$. It is positive throughout $[0,1]$, and

$$
\int_0^1f(x)\,dx=\frac35+\frac25=1,
\qquad
\int_0^1xf(x)\,dx=\frac3{10}+\frac3{10}=\frac35.
$$

The density rises as $x$ increases, so placing the mean to the right of $1/2$ is reasonable.

The two conditions identify this density within the specified family $a+bx^2$. A mean of $3/5$ alone does not identify an arbitrary distribution; many different distributions can share that mean.
