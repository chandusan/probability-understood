# Turn uniform moments into standard deviation and shape measures

For $X$ uniform on $[0,1]$, the mean is $1/2$, and its second, third, and fourth central moments are $1/12$, $0$, and $1/80$. We can now calculate its standard deviation, skewness, and excess kurtosis while keeping every normalization explicit.

## Standard deviation is the square root of variance

The second central moment is variance:

$$\sigma^2=\mu_2^0=\frac1{12}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\sigma=\frac1{\sqrt{12}}=\frac1{2\sqrt3}\approx0.2887.}$$

The square root is positive. Standard deviation measures distance, so a negative root would have no role in its definition.

## Skewness divides the third central moment by $\sigma^3$

Since $\mu_3^0=0$ and $\sigma>0$,

$$\boxed{\gamma_1=\frac{\mu_3^0}{\sigma^3}=0.}$$

Here the zero has a direct symmetry explanation: opposite deviations from $1/2$ have equal density and their cubes cancel. The known shape gives the symmetry; the numerical zero alone would not prove it for an arbitrary model.

## Kurtosis uses the square of variance

First calculate the denominator:

$$\sigma^4=(\sigma^2)^2=\left(\frac1{12}\right)^2=\frac1{144}.$$

Then ordinary kurtosis is

$$\beta_2=\frac{1/80}{1/144}=\frac{144}{80}=\frac95=1.8.$$

Subtract the normal reference value three:

$$\boxed{\gamma_2=\beta_2-3=\frac95-3=-\frac65=-1.2.}$$

The source's formula subtracts three, so its value is **excess kurtosis**. We use $\gamma_2$ for this quantity to distinguish it from the skewness coefficient $\gamma_1$.

## Keep the final quantities organized

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Mean | $1/2$ |
| Variance | $1/12$ |
| Standard deviation | $1/\sqrt{12}$ |
| Third central moment | $0$ |
| Fourth central moment | $1/80$ |
| Skewness | $0$ |
| Kurtosis | $9/5$ |
| Excess kurtosis | $-6/5$ |

Changing to a uniform distribution on any nondegenerate interval changes the mean and scale, but leaves standardized skewness and kurtosis the same. Centering and dividing by standard deviation remove those changes of location and scale.
