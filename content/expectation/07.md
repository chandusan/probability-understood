# A decreasing density: find its mean and CDF

Let $X$ have density $f(x)=\frac32(1-x^2)$ for $0\le x\le1$, and $f(x)=0$ elsewhere. The problem asks for $E(X)$ and the full cumulative distribution function $F(x)=P(X\le x)$. These require different integrands even though both involve integration.

## Verify that this is a density

On $[0,1]$, $1-x^2\ge0$, so the density is nonnegative. Its total area is

$$
\int_0^1\frac32(1-x^2)\,dx
=\frac32\left[x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1
=\frac32\left(1-\frac13\right)=1.
$$

The density decreases from $1.5$ to $0$. A density height greater than $1$ is allowed; total area, rather than height, must equal $1$.

## Find the weighted average value

Expectation multiplies the density by the value being averaged:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(X)&=\int_0^1 x\frac32(1-x^2)\,dx\\
&=\frac32\int_0^1(x-x^3)\,dx\\
&=\frac32\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac32\left(\frac12-\frac14\right)=\boxed{\frac38}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The answer is below the interval's midpoint $1/2$, consistent with greater probability density toward the left.

## Find accumulated probability instead

For $0\le x\le1$, use a dummy integration variable $t$ so that the upper cutoff $x$ remains clear:

$$
F(x)=\int_0^x\frac32(1-t^2)\,dt
=\frac32x-\frac12x^3.
$$

The complete CDF is

$$
\boxed{F(x)=\begin{cases}
0,&x<0,\\
\frac32x-\frac12x^3,&0\le x\le1,\\
1,&x>1.
\end{cases}}
$$

It begins at $0$, ends at $1$, and has derivative $f(x)$ inside the support. For example, $P(X\le1/2)=3/4-1/16=11/16$. This is a probability, whereas $E(X)=3/8$ is an average value; neither substitutes for the other.
