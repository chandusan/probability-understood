# The length of a best-of-seven series

Two evenly matched teams play independent games until one team reaches four wins. Let $X$ be the number of games actually played. Find the distribution of $X$ and its expectation. The phrase “seven-game series” means at most seven games: the series stops immediately after the fourth win by either team.

## Identify the possible lengths

A team cannot get four wins before game four, and by game seven at least one team has four wins. Thus $X\in\{4,5,6,7\}$.

For the series to end at game $k$, its eventual winner must have exactly three wins in the first $k-1$ games, then win game $k$. The other team has $k-4$ wins before the last game.

## Count valid paths of a specified length

Choose which team wins the series: $2$ choices. Choose the positions of its first three wins among the first $k-1$ games: $\binom{k-1}{3}$ choices. Its last win is forced to occur at game $k$.

Every such path has probability $(1/2)^k$, by independence. Neither team can have won earlier: the eventual winner had only three wins, and the loser has at most three. Therefore

$$
\boxed{P(X=k)=2\binom{k-1}{3}\left(\frac12\right)^k,
\qquad k=4,5,6,7.}
$$

| $k$ | Valid length-$k$ paths | $P(X=k)$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $4$ | $2\binom33=2$ | $1/8$ |
| $5$ | $2\binom43=8$ | $1/4$ |
| $6$ | $2\binom53=20$ | $5/16$ |
| $7$ | $2\binom63=40$ | $5/16$ |

Their sum is $2/16+4/16+5/16+5/16=1$.

## Average the length

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(X)&=4\frac18+5\frac14+6\frac5{16}+7\frac5{16}\\
&=\frac8{16}+\frac{20}{16}+\frac{30}{16}+\frac{35}{16}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{93}{16}=5.8125\text{ games}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Check using the chance each game is played

The first four games always occur. Game five occurs with probability $7/8$, game six with probability $5/8$, and game seven with probability $5/16$. Count one game each time it is played:

$$E(X)=4+\frac78+\frac58+\frac5{16}=\frac{93}{16}.$$

For example, the chance of reaching game six is $P(X\ge6)=5/16+5/16=5/8$. This agrees with the direct weighted average and connects the mean length to survival past successive stopping points.
