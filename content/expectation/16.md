# Build the full distribution of monthly outage cost

The outage count $X$ has probabilities $0.15,0.35,0.25,0.15,0.10$ at $0,1,2,3,4$. Monthly cost is $C(X)=500X^2+1000$ dollars. Find its expectation by first writing the cost incurred in every possible case, rather than substituting the mean count into the cost formula.

## Map counts to costs and retain the probabilities

| $x$ | $C(x)$ | $p(x)$ | $C(x)p(x)$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $1000$ | $0.15$ | $150$ |
| $1$ | $1500$ | $0.35$ | $525$ |
| $2$ | $3000$ | $0.25$ | $750$ |
| $3$ | $5500$ | $0.15$ | $825$ |
| $4$ | $9000$ | $0.10$ | $900$ |

For example, $C=5500$ happens precisely when $X=3$, so it has probability $0.15$. The chance of that month is unchanged; we are simply recording dollars instead of outages.

Sum the last column:

$$
\boxed{E[C(X)]=150+525+750+825+900=3150\text{ dollars}.}
$$

## Separate the fixed and variable portions

Using the same five cases,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[C(X)]
&=\sum_x(500x^2+1000)p(x)\\
&=500\sum_xx^2p(x)+1000\sum_xp(x)\\
&=500E(X^2)+1000.
\end{aligned}
$$

The fixed cost contributes exactly $1000$ because it is incurred in every possible month and the probabilities sum to $1$. It is not added once per possible outcome without weighting.

## Why rare high-cost months matter

Four-outage months occur only $10\%$ of the time, but their contribution to the mean cost is $900$ dollars, the largest contribution in the table. This does not mean the company pays only $900$ in such a month; it pays $9000$. The product spreads that large cost across all months in the average.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Why is the expected cost not $2445$ dollars?</summary>

That is $C(E(X))=500(1.7)^2+1000$. The expectation requires $500E(X^2)+1000$, and $E(X^2)=4.3$ while $[E(X)]^2=2.89$. Squaring before averaging retains the effect of variation in outages.

</details>
