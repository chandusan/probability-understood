# Derive every raw moment of a uniform variable

Let $X$ be uniform on $[0,1]$, with density $f(x)=1$ inside the interval and zero elsewhere. Instead of calculating its moments separately, we can derive one formula for every nonnegative integer order $k$: $E(X^k)=1/(k+1)$.

## Insert the chosen function into the expectation integral

For the $k$th raw moment, the quantity being averaged is $g(x)=x^k$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_k=E(X^k)
&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^kf(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_0^1x^k\,dx\\
&=\left[\frac{x^{k+1}}{k+1}\right]_0^1\\
&=\boxed{\frac1{k+1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $k\ge0$, the antiderivative is valid and the lower endpoint contributes zero. The zeroth moment corresponds to integrating the constant function $1$ and equals $1$.

## Read several moments from the formula

| Order $k$ | Raw moment $\mu_k$ |
|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $1$ |
| $1$ | $1/2$ |
| $2$ | $1/3$ |
| $3$ | $1/4$ |
| $4$ | $1/5$ |

For instance, $E(X^2)=1/3$ while $[E(X)]^2=1/4$. Their difference is the variance:

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=\frac13-\frac14=\frac1{12}.$$

## Why the moments decrease with the order

For $0\le x\le1$, multiplying by another factor $x$ cannot increase the value: $x^{k+1}\le x^k$. The corresponding averages therefore decrease too. This is consistent with $1/(k+2)\le1/(k+1)$.

This behavior depends on the support being within $[0,1]$. If values exceed $1$, higher powers can grow very quickly instead.

## Raw moments are not shape coefficients yet

Although the uniform distribution is symmetric around $1/2$, its third raw moment is $1/4$, not zero. Symmetry cancels odd powers of **deviations from the center**, not odd powers of the original nonnegative values.

To study that cancellation, the next calculation averages $(X-1/2)^k$ rather than $X^k$.
