# Work through the expectation integral one step at a time

For $f(x)=\frac32(1-x^2)$ on $[0,1]$, we want the mean $E(X)$. Start with the entire expression being integrated, then expand it, integrate each power, and apply both endpoints. This makes clear what the density, the extra factor $x$, and the integration each contribute.

## Write the complete quantity first

The expectation definition gives

$$
E(X)=\int_0^1 x f(x)\,dx
=\int_0^1 x\left[\frac32(1-x^2)\right]dx.
$$

The factor $x$ is the value whose average we seek. The small quantity $f(x)\,dx$ represents probability in a narrow interval near that value. Their product is that interval's contribution to the average.

## Expand before applying the power rule

Distribute $x$ through the bracket:

$$
x(1-x^2)=x-x^3.
$$

Pull the constant $3/2$ outside:

$$
E(X)=\frac32\left(\int_0^1x\,dx-\int_0^1x^3\,dx\right).
$$

The antiderivatives are $x^2/2$ and $x^4/4$, because differentiating them returns $x$ and $x^3$.

## Evaluate upper endpoint minus lower endpoint

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(X)
&=\frac32\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac32\left[\left(\frac{1^2}{2}-\frac{1^4}{4}\right)
-\left(\frac{0^2}{2}-\frac{0^4}{4}\right)\right]\\
&=\frac32\left(\frac12-\frac14\right)
=\boxed{\frac38=0.375}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The lower endpoint contributes zero here. Writing it explicitly prevents errors when later examples have a nonzero lower bound.

## A second check using the CDF

For a nonnegative variable bounded by $1$, the mean also equals the area under its tail probability $P(X>t)$. Here $F(t)=3t/2-t^3/2$, so

$$
\int_0^1[1-F(t)]\,dt
=\left[t-\frac34t^2+\frac18t^4\right]_0^1
=\frac38.
$$

Why can this work? A realized value $x$ is the total length of thresholds $t$ between $0$ and $x$. Averaging those lengths means, at each threshold, counting how often $X$ reaches beyond it. The first integral remains the direct method required by the slide; the tail calculation supplies a useful independent check.
