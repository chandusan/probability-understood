# Solve the variance of a reciprocal transformation

Let $X$ have density $f(x)=x^2(2x+3/2)$ on $0<x<1$, zero elsewhere. Find $\operatorname{Var}(2/X+3)$. The constant $3$ shifts the transformed values but cannot change their spread; the factor $2$ multiplies their variance by four.

## Name the inner transformation

Set $U=1/X$. Then $Y=2U+3$, so

$$\operatorname{Var}(Y)=4\operatorname{Var}(U)
=4\big(E(U^2)-[E(U)]^2\big).$$

We need $E(1/X)$ and $E(1/X^2)$. Neither is obtained by taking a reciprocal of a moment of $X$.

## Calculate the first reciprocal moment

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(1/X)
&=\int_0^1\frac1x\,x^2\left(2x+\frac32\right)dx\\
&=\int_0^1\left(2x^2+\frac32x\right)dx\\
&=\frac23+\frac34=\frac{17}{12}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculate the second reciprocal moment

The square cancels the density's $x^2$ factor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(1/X^2)
&=\int_0^1\frac1{x^2}\,x^2\left(2x+\frac32\right)dx\\
&=\int_0^1\left(2x+\frac32\right)dx\\
&=1+\frac32=\frac52.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both improper integrals converge at zero after simplification. This matters because the transformed variable is unbounded even though $X$ is bounded.

## Assemble the variance

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(U)
&=\frac52-\left(\frac{17}{12}\right)^2\\
&=\frac{360}{144}-\frac{289}{144}=\frac{71}{144}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac2X+3\right)
=4\frac{71}{144}=\frac{71}{36}\approx1.9722.}$$

## Verify by calculating the transformed second moment

Expand $Y^2=(2/X+3)^2=4/X^2+12/X+9$. Hence

$$E(Y^2)=4\frac52+12\frac{17}{12}+9=36.$$

From the earlier expectation calculation, $E(Y)=35/6$. Therefore

$$E(Y^2)-[E(Y)]^2
=36-\frac{1225}{36}=\frac{71}{36},$$

confirming the answer. The transformed standard deviation, if requested, is $\sqrt{71}/6$. The shift $3$ affects the mean and second raw moment, but cancels out of the final variance.
