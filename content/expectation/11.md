# Solve the density coefficients and verify their meaning

For the model $f(x)=a+bx^2$ on $[0,1]$ with mean $3/5$, the probability and expectation constraints reduce to $3a+b=3$ and $10a+5b=12$. This page completes the algebra and checks that the resulting function actually satisfies the probability problem.

## Eliminate one unknown explicitly

From the first equation,

$$b=3-3a.$$

Insert this expression wherever $b$ occurs in the second:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10a+5b&=12,\\
10a+5(3-3a)&=12,\\
10a+15-15a&=12,\\
-5a&=-3,\\
a&=\frac35.
\end{aligned}
$$

Recover the other coefficient:

$$
b=3-3\left(\frac35\right)
=\frac{15}{5}-\frac95=\frac65.
$$

Thus the full density is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}
\frac35+\frac65x^2,&0\le x\le1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
$$

## Check three requirements

First, both coefficients are positive, so the function is nonnegative on its support. Second,

$$
\int_0^1f(x)\,dx=\frac35+\frac{6/5}{3}
=\frac35+\frac25=1.
$$

Third,

$$
E(X)=\frac{3/5}{2}+\frac{6/5}{4}
=\frac3{10}+\frac3{10}=\frac35.
$$

Passing the algebra alone would not suffice if the candidate density became negative somewhere. Here all three requirements hold.

## An additional interpretation as a mixture

Rewrite the density as

$$
f(x)=\frac35(1)+\frac25(3x^2),\qquad 0\le x\le1.
$$

Both $1$ and $3x^2$ are themselves normalized densities on $[0,1]$. This model can therefore be generated by choosing the uniform density with probability $3/5$, and the increasing density $3x^2$ with probability $2/5$.

Their means are $1/2$ and $3/4$. Weight those two means:

$$
\frac35\frac12+\frac25\frac34=\frac35.
$$

This supplies a second interpretation of the same mean and explains how extra probability toward larger values shifts the uniform mean upward.
