# Derive the MGF of a uniform variable, including zero

Let $X$ be uniform on $[0,1]$. Its MGF is $M_X(t)=\int_0^1e^{tx}\,dx$. We integrate with respect to $x$, treating $t$ as a fixed number. The resulting quotient is valid for $t\ne0$; the value at zero comes directly from the definition.

## Integrate when $t$ is nonzero

Because

$$\frac{d}{dx}e^{tx}=t e^{tx},$$

an antiderivative of $e^{tx}$ is $e^{tx}/t$ when $t\ne0$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_X(t)&=\int_0^1e^{tx}\,dx\\
&=\left[\frac{e^{tx}}t\right]_{x=0}^{x=1}\\
&=\frac{e^t-e^0}{t}
=\frac{e^t-1}{t}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The integration variable $x$ disappears after evaluating the endpoints. What remains is a function of $t$.

## Evaluate $t=0$ from the original expression

At zero, every exponential is one:

$$M_X(0)=\int_0^1e^{0x}\,dx=\int_0^1 1\,dx=1.$$

Thus the complete formula is

$$
\boxed{M_X(t)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{e^t-1}{t},&t\ne0,\\
1,&t=0.
\end{cases}}
$$

The symbol $0/0$ obtained by substituting into the quotient is not the value of the MGF. It indicates that this algebraic expression requires a continuous extension at that point.

## Why the pieces agree smoothly

For small $t$, the exponential expansion begins

$$e^t=1+t+\frac{t^2}{2}+\frac{t^3}{6}+\cdots.$$

Subtract one and divide by $t$:

$$M_X(t)=1+\frac t2+\frac{t^2}{6}+\frac{t^3}{24}+\cdots.$$

The limit as $t\to0$ is one, exactly matching the definition. The coefficient of $t$ also suggests $M_X'(0)=1/2$, the known uniform mean.

There is no convergence obstacle for real $t$ here: $X$ lies in a bounded interval, so $e^{tX}$ is bounded for each fixed $t$. The MGF exists for every real input.
