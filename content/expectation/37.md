# Prove that scaling and shifting commute with expectation

For fixed constants $a,b$ and a random variable with finite mean, $E(aX+b)=aE(X)+b$. Scaling every observation by $a$ scales its average by $a$; adding $b$ to every observation adds $b$ to its average. This property is called linearity of expectation.

## Prove it directly from the weighted sum

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(aX+b)&=\sum_x(ax+b)p(x)\\
&=a\sum_xx p(x)+b\sum_xp(x)\\
&=\boxed{aE(X)+b}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last step uses $\sum_xp(x)=1$. Distributing multiplication across a sum is the entire mechanism.

For a density, the same reasoning uses integrals:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(aX+b)&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(ax+b)f(x)\,dx\\
&=a\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xf(x)\,dx
+b\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx\\
&=aE(X)+b.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Apply the rule to a new measurement scale

If temperature $X$ in Celsius has mean $20$, its Fahrenheit value is $Y=(9/5)X+32$. Then

$$E(Y)=\frac95(20)+32=68.$$

We need no additional information about the shape of the temperature distribution to convert its mean.

## Linearity also handles several terms

When the expectations are finite,

$$E(aX+bY+c)=aE(X)+bE(Y)+c.$$

Independence is not required. For example, if $Y=X$, then $E(X+Y)=2E(X)$ despite complete dependence. Averaging a sum means averaging each contribution and adding the results.

## Keep nonlinear operations separate

Linearity does not say $E(X^2)=[E(X)]^2$, or $E(1/X)=1/E(X)$. It does allow

$$E(500X^2+1000)=500E(X^2)+1000,$$

because the expression is linear in the random quantity $X^2$. The remaining task is to average that squared quantity correctly.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Does $E(XY)=E(X)E(Y)$ follow from linearity?</summary>

No. Products are a different operation. Independence is a sufficient condition for that product rule when the required expectations exist. Taking $Y=X$ already shows the issue: $E(XY)=E(X^2)$ generally differs from $[E(X)]^2$.

</details>
