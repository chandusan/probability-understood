# Derive the variance shortcut without skipping the square

Variance is defined by $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[(X-\mu)^2]$, where $\mu=E(X)$. Expanding the full square and applying linearity produces the useful formula $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2$. The second raw moment alone is not variance unless the mean is zero.

## Expand the complete difference

$$
(X-\mu)^2=X^2-2\mu X+\mu^2.
$$

The middle term $-2\mu X$ is essential. Omitting it would turn the variance identity into an incorrect sum.

Take expectations term by term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X)
&=E(X^2)-2\mu E(X)+E(\mu^2)\\
&=E(X^2)-2\mu^2+\mu^2\\
&=\boxed{E(X^2)-\mu^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $E(X)=\mu$ by definition, and $E(\mu^2)=\mu^2$ because the mean is a fixed number. This proof works for discrete and continuous variables with finite second moment.

## See the same steps inside an integral

When a density exists,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(X)
&=\int(x-\mu)^2f(x)\,dx\\
&=\int x^2f(x)\,dx
-2\mu\int xf(x)\,dx
+\mu^2\int f(x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$

The three integrals become $E(X^2)$, $\mu$, and $1$, respectively, giving the same result. Every integral is over the full support.

## Interpret the subtraction

Rearrange the formula:

$$E(X^2)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\mu^2.$$

Average squared distance from zero contains two components: the squared location of the center, and average squared spread around that center. Subtracting $\mu^2$ removes the effect of location.

For the outage model, $E(X^2)=4.30$ and $\mu=1.70$, so variance is $4.30-2.89=1.41$.

Since variance is nonnegative, the identity also establishes $E(X^2)\ge[E(X)]^2$. Equality holds exactly when $X$ is constant with probability one. If numerical rounding gives a tiny negative variance, revisit the precision; a genuine negative variance is impossible.
