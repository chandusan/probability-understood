# Prove how the MGF stores and generates moments

Assume $M_X(t)=E(e^{tX})$ is finite on an open interval containing zero. We will derive its value at zero, explain why each derivative generates one raw moment, and identify exactly where the factorials in its power series come from.

## Property 1: its value at zero is one

For every possible real value of $X$, $e^{0X}=1$. Hence

$$\boxed{M_X(0)=E(e^{0X})=E(1)=1.}$$

This check works before any differentiation or series expansion.

## Property 2: differentiating brings down powers

For a fixed value $x$,

$$\frac{d^k}{dt^k}e^{tx}=x^ke^{tx}.$$

Under the stated MGF condition, differentiation may pass through expectation near zero:

$$M_X^{(k)}(t)=E(X^ke^{tX}).$$

Set $t=0$:

$$\boxed{M_X^{(k)}(0)=E(X^k)=\mu_k.}$$

For finite discrete support this can be checked term by term in a finite sum. The neighborhood-of-zero condition is what supports the corresponding operations for more general distributions.

## Property 3: the power-series coefficients contain moments

The exponential series is

$$e^{tX}=1+tX+\frac{t^2X^2}{2!}+\frac{t^3X^3}{3!}+\cdots.$$

Taking expectations term by term in a sufficiently small neighborhood of zero gives

$$
\boxed{M_X(t)=1+\mu_1t+\frac{\mu_2}{2!}t^2
+\frac{\mu_3}{3!}t^3+\cdots.}
$$

The coefficient of $t^k$ is $\mu_k/k!$, not $\mu_k$. Taking $k$ derivatives of $t^k$ produces the factor $k!$, cancelling that denominator when evaluated at zero. Lower powers disappear under enough derivatives, while higher powers still contain a positive power of $t$ and vanish at zero.

## Read the uniform moments correctly

For $X$ uniform on $[0,1]$,

$$M_X(t)=1+\frac t2+\frac{t^2}{6}+\frac{t^3}{24}+\cdots.$$

Thus

$$E(X)=\frac12,\quad E(X^2)=2!\frac16=\frac13,
\quad E(X^3)=3!\frac1{24}=\frac14.$$

These agree with direct integration. In particular, reading the coefficient $1/6$ as $E(X^2)$ would forget the factorial and produce an incorrect variance.
