# Derive the two equations for the unknown density

The density has the form $f(x)=a+bx^2$ on $[0,1]$, and its mean is $3/5$. Before solving for $a,b$, understand precisely why normalization produces $a+b/3=1$ while the mean produces $a/2+b/4=3/5$.

## First equation: all probability totals one

The area under a valid density is one:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1&=\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_0^1(a+bx^2)\,dx\\
&=a\int_0^1 1\,dx+b\int_0^1x^2\,dx\\
&=a\left[x\right]_0^1+b\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1\\
&=a+\frac b3.
\end{aligned}
$$

This equation constrains the total amount of probability. It does not specify where that probability is concentrated.

## Second equation: the required balance point

The supplied expectation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac35&=\int_0^1x f(x)\,dx\\
&=\int_0^1(ax+bx^3)\,dx\\
&=a\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1
+b\left[\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1\\
&=\frac a2+\frac b4.
\end{aligned}
$$

Probability near larger $x$ receives larger weight in this equation. That is why the same density appears with different coefficients.

## Clear fractions without changing the equations

Multiply $a+b/3=1$ by $3$:

$$3a+b=3.$$

Multiply $a/2+b/4=3/5$ by $20$, a common multiple of $2,4,5$:

$$10a+5b=12.$$

The pair has the unique solution $a=3/5$, $b=6/5$. Substituting into the original equations is more informative than only checking the cleared versions: it directly verifies total probability $1$ and mean $3/5$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Why is $\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=3/5$ the wrong equation?</summary>

That would make the total probability $3/5$, leaving probability $2/5$ unaccounted for. The number $3/5$ is the desired average location, not the probability that $X$ is somewhere in its support.

</details>
