# Standardized skewness compares shape across scales

The third central moment depends on the units used to measure $X$. Divide by the cube of its standard deviation to express deviations on a common scale. The resulting skewness coefficient is $\gamma_1=E[(X-\mu)^3]/\sigma^3$, provided the third absolute moment is finite and $\sigma>0$.

## Start with a standardized deviation

Let $Z=(X-\mu)/\sigma$. It measures how far $X$ lies from the mean, in standard-deviation units. Then

$$
\boxed{\gamma_1=E(Z^3)
=\frac{E[(X-\mu)^3]}{\sigma^3}.}
$$

Since $\sigma^2=E[(X-\mu)^2]$, another expression is

$$
\gamma_1=\frac{E[(X-\mu)^3]}
{\{E[(X-\mu)^2]\}^{3/2}}.
$$

The denominator is positive; it rescales magnitude without changing the sign.

## Finish the two-point example

For $P(X=0)=3/4$ and $P(X=4)=1/4$, we found mean $1$, variance $3$, and third central moment $6$. Therefore

$$
\gamma_1=\frac6{(\sqrt3)^3}
=\frac6{3\sqrt3}=\boxed{\frac2{\sqrt3}\approx1.1547}.
$$

This quantifies the positive imbalance in cubed standardized deviations. It does not mean that $1.1547$ is a probability; skewness is not restricted to $[-1,1]$.

## Why a change of scale cancels

For $Y=aX+b$ with $a>0$, its deviation is $a(X-\mu)$ and its standard deviation is $a\sigma$. Hence

$$
\frac{Y-E(Y)}{\sigma_Y}
=\frac{a(X-\mu)}{a\sigma}=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}.
$$

Skewness is unchanged. For $a<0$, reflection reverses all standardized deviations, so skewness changes sign. Adding a constant has no effect.

No. Symmetry makes opposite cubic contributions cancel, so it implies zero skewness when the required moments exist. But cancellation alone does not require matching probabilities at every pair of opposite positions. The next page gives an explicit asymmetric distribution with zero third central moment.
