# Recover the uniform mean and choose the simplest method

For $X$ uniform on $[0,1]$, the MGF is $(e^t-1)/t$ when $t\ne0$ and equals $1$ at zero. Its derivative at zero should give the mean $1/2$. The quotient looks awkward there, but the underlying function is smooth; the apparent singularity comes from the chosen formula.

## Use the definition of the derivative at zero

Because $M_X(0)=1$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_X'(0)
&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{M_X(h)-M_X(0)}h\\
&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(e^h-1)/h-1}{h}\\
&=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^h-1-h}{h^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The numerator and denominator both approach zero. L'Hôpital's rule, applied twice, evaluates this limit:

$$
\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^h-1-h}{h^2}
=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^h-1}{2h}
=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^h}{2}
=\boxed{\frac12}.
$$

Each use differentiates numerator and denominator separately to evaluate a limit; it is not the quotient rule for differentiating their ratio.

## Connect this with the derivative shown on the source

For $t\ne0$, the quotient rule gives

$$M_X'(t)=\frac{te^t-e^t+1}{t^2}.$$

Its limit at zero is also $1/2$. One application of L'Hôpital gives $te^t/(2t)$, which simplifies to $e^t/2$ for nonzero $t$. Since this bounded-variable MGF is smoothly differentiable at zero, the limiting derivative agrees with the derivative calculated directly above.

## An alternative that needs no L'Hôpital rule

Expand the exponential:

$$e^h=1+h+\frac{h^2}{2}+\frac{h^3}{6}+\cdots.$$

Then

$$\frac{e^h-1-h}{h^2}=\frac12+\frac h6+\cdots\longrightarrow\frac12.$$

Thus L'Hôpital's rule is one useful method, not a necessity.

## For this particular problem, direct expectation is shorter

The density is simply one on $[0,1]$, so

$$E(X)=\int_0^1x\,dx=\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^1=\frac12.$$

An MGF is valuable when its compact form makes multiple moments or sums easier to analyze. It need not be the quickest tool for every individual expectation. Choose the method that exposes the structure with the fewest opportunities for error, while keeping the definition and assumptions clear.
