# Build an MGF from the expectation-of-a-function rule

The moment-generating function is an ordinary transformed expectation with $g(x)=e^{tx}$. For each fixed input $t$, use the original probabilities or density to average that function. Once this average is expressed as a function of $t$, derivatives at zero recover raw moments.

## Discrete and continuous definitions

For a discrete variable,

$$\boxed{M_X(t)=E(e^{tX})=\sum_xe^{tx}p(x).}$$

If $X$ has a density,

$$\boxed{M_X(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{tx}f(x)\,dx.}$$

The range of $t$ for which the sum or integral is finite is part of the answer. The standard moment-generating results require finiteness in a neighborhood of zero.

## What changing $t$ does

When $t>0$, the exponential increasingly emphasizes larger positive values of $x$. When $t<0$, it emphasizes smaller values. At $t=0$, every possible value transforms to one, so

$$M_X(0)=E(1)=1.$$

This is a useful check on any proposed formula. It is not a normalization over $t$; there is no requirement that the area under $M_X(t)$ equal one.

## Differentiate a finite discrete sum explicitly

For finite support, differentiation can be performed term by term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_X'(t)&=\sum_xx e^{tx}p(x),\\
M_X''(t)&=\sum_xx^2e^{tx}p(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating at zero removes the exponential:

$$M_X'(0)=\sum_xxp(x)=E(X),$$

$$M_X''(0)=\sum_xx^2p(x)=E(X^2).$$

Thus variance is

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X)=M_X''(0)-[M_X'(0)]^2.}$$

The second derivative alone gives the second **raw** moment, not variance.

## Read one simple example

If $P(X=-1)=P(X=1)=1/2$, then

$$M_X(t)=\frac{e^{-t}+e^t}{2}.$$

Its first derivative at zero is $(-1+1)/2=0$, and its second derivative at zero is $(1+1)/2=1$. Hence the mean is zero and variance one. The calculation also shows how opposite values can cancel in odd derivatives while contributing positively to even ones.
