# What “nothing” and “everything” do to a set

Union asks whether at least one test succeeds. Intersection asks whether both succeed. Apply those questions to a test that always fails, $\varnothing$, and one that always succeeds within the model, $S$, and the four identities become inevitable.

## Four rules, with their reasons

Assume $A\subseteq S$.

| Identity | Membership explanation |
|---|---|
| $A\cup\varnothing=A$ | Adding no outcomes introduces nothing new |
| $A\cap\varnothing=\varnothing$ | No outcome can pass an empty event's test |
| $A\cup S=S$ | Every possible outcome is already accepted by $S$ |
| $A\cap S=A$ | Requiring a member of $A$ also to be possible removes nothing |

The operation matters. The empty set leaves a union unchanged but empties an intersection. The sample space fills a union but leaves an intersection unchanged.

## See all four on one die

Let

$$
S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\},\qquad A=\{2,4,6\}.
$$

Then

$$
\{2,4,6\}\cup\varnothing=\{2,4,6\},
$$

$$
\{2,4,6\}\cap\varnothing=\varnothing,
$$

$$
\{2,4,6\}\cup\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}=S,
$$

$$
\{2,4,6\}\cap\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}=\{2,4,6\}.
$$

Try reading each line as a sentence about one outcome. The face 5 fails $A$, but it passes $A\cup S$ because it belongs to $S$. It still fails $A\cap S$ because the intersection also demands membership in $A$.

## Use them to simplify a longer expression

Consider

$$
(A\cup\varnothing)\cap(B\cup S).
$$

The first bracket is $A$ and the second is $S$, so the result is $A\cap S=A$. The extra symbols never changed the final membership test.

These are sometimes called identity and domination laws: adding nothing or intersecting with everything preserves a set; intersecting with nothing or joining everything dominates the result.

## Check your understanding

Simplify $(A\cap\varnothing)\cup(B\cap S)$. Explain without listing any specific outcomes.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the simplification</summary>

$A\cap\varnothing=\varnothing$ and $B\cap S=B$, so the expression is $\varnothing\cup B=B$. The result holds for every pair of events within the same sample space.

</details>
