# A complement needs a surrounding world

The complement of an event contains every allowed outcome that fails its test. “Not in A” is meaningful only after we specify which outcomes are allowed in the first place.

## Keep the sample space in the definition

The notations $A^c$ and $\overline A$ both mean the complement of $A$, relative to the specified sample space $S$:

$$
A^c=S\setminus A=\{e\in S:e\notin A\}.
$$

The shaded region in the picture is everything inside the sample-space rectangle but outside $A$. The unbounded area beyond the rectangle is not part of the complement.

For a die,

$$
S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\},\qquad A=\{2,4,6\}
$$

gives

$$
A^c=\{1,3,5\}.
$$

The number 7 is not in $A$, but it is not in this complement either, because it is not in $S$.

## Change the world and the complement changes

Keep $A=\{2,4\}$ fixed. Inside $S_1=\{1,2,3,4\}$, its complement is $\{1,3\}$. Inside $S_2=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, its complement is $\{1,3,5,6\}$.

The event's listed elements did not change. The universe of alternatives did. This is why leaving the surrounding sample space implicit can cause errors.

The same issue appears with real-valued observations. If $S=[0,10]$ and $A=(2,5)$, then

$$
A^c=[0,2]\cup[5,10].
$$

Both endpoints 2 and 5 belong to the complement because the original interval excluded them.

## The two pieces exhaust the possibilities

Every outcome in $S$ is either in $A$ or outside $A$, and cannot be both:

$$
A\cup A^c=S,\qquad A\cap A^c=\varnothing.
$$

Consequently, once probabilities are assigned,

$$
P(A^c)=1-P(A).
$$

This often makes “at least one” questions easier: “not at least one” means “none.” However, we must first take the complement of the whole statement, rather than negate whichever word seems convenient.

## Check your understanding

For $S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, let $A$ mean “at least 4.” What is $A^c$? Does it mean “at most 4”?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the answer</summary>

$A=\{4,5,6\}$, so $A^c=\{1,2,3\}$, meaning “less than 4” or “at most 3.” “At most 4” would wrongly include 4, an outcome already in $A$. The complement must leave no overlap and no gap.

</details>
