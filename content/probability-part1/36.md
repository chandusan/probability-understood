# A union needs just one successful membership test

A large union symbol is an instruction to search a family of sets: **keep an outcome if you can find at least one set containing it**. The family may have a last set, or it may continue indefinitely. The membership rule stays the same.

## Read the finite notation

For k events,

$$
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}E_i
=E_1\cup E_2\cup E_3\cup\cdots\cup E_k.
$$

The index i runs from 1 through k. The symbol $\cdots$ means “continue the same pattern”; it is not an extra event. The slide's font renders this continuation mark poorly. The intended expression is the ordinary ellipsis shown here.

In logical language,

$$
x\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}E_i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{there exists }i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\text{ such that }x\in E_i.
$$

“Exists” is the decisive word. You may find one successful membership test or several. In the source's three-circle diagram, the entire area covered by any circle is shaded, including all overlaps.

## Extend the list without a last event

For a countably infinite sequence,

$$
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_i
=E_1\cup E_2\cup E_3\cup\cdots.
$$

An outcome belongs if it lies in $E_i$ for **some positive integer i**. Infinity is not a final numbered event that we eventually reach.

For example, on $S=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$, let $E_i=\{i\}$. Each set contains just one positive integer, yet

$$
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\{i\}=S.
$$

Any candidate integer m has a witness: it belongs to $E_m$. We do not need to finish listing infinitely many sets to prove the equality.

## Endpoints can survive or disappear

On $S=\mathbb R$, let $E_i=[0,1-1/i]$. The first few are $\{0\}$, $[0,1/2]$, and $[0,2/3]$. Then

$$
\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_i=[0,1).
$$

Zero belongs to every set. Any $x<1$ with $x\ge0$ eventually lies in one of the intervals: choose i large enough that $1/i\le1-x$. But 1 never belongs to any $E_i$, so the union does not contain it. **Getting arbitrarily close to a point is not the same as including it.**

## Check your understanding

Let $E_i=\{i,i+1\}$ for $i=1,2,3,\ldots$. Find the countable union. Is an outcome required to belong to infinitely many of these events?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Find a witness for each outcome</summary>

The union is $\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$. Each positive integer m belongs to $E_m$, so every positive integer is included. No nonpositive or noninteger outcome appears in any set. Membership in one event suffices: 1 belongs only to $E_1$, while each integer m greater than 1 belongs to $E_{m-1}$ and $E_m$. Neither pattern requires infinitely many memberships.
