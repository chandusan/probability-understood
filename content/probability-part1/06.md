# Two dice: count outcomes before probabilities

Two dice produce an ordered pair. Keeping track of which die shows which face turns a vague list of possibilities into a simple six-by-six grid containing $36$ distinct outcomes.

## Why an ordered pair?

Imagine one red die and one blue die. Write

$$(D_1,D_2)=(\text{red result},\text{blue result}).$$

Then $(1,6)$ differs from $(6,1)$: the red die shows a different face. Even if the dice look identical, we can label them by position or by the order in which they are rolled.

The sample space is

$$S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\times\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}.$$

For each of six first-die results, there are six second-die results, giving $6\times6=36$ ordered pairs.

## Counting is separate from weighting

If both dice are fair **and independent**, then

$$P(D_1=i,D_2=j)=\frac16\frac16=\frac1{36}.$$

Fairness of each die alone does not guarantee equally likely pairs. Imagine a mechanism that always forces the second die to copy the first. Each die could individually be uniform, while only the six doubles are possible together. Independence rules out that dependence.

## The sum is a summary of the outcome

Let $T=D_1+D_2$. Its possible values are $2,3,\ldots,12$, but they are not equally likely. One ordered pair gives $T=2$, whereas six give $T=7$:

$$(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1).$$

Therefore, under the fair independent model,

$$P(T=2)=\frac1{36},\qquad P(T=7)=\frac6{36}=\frac16.$$

This is our first glimpse of a **random variable**: a numerical rule applied to an underlying outcome. Several outcomes can map to the same number. Losing the pair information changes the list of possible recorded results and their weights.

## Check your understanding

What is the probability that the dice show the same number? What is the probability their sum is $4$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Work through the answer</summary>

There are six doubles, $(1,1)$ through $(6,6)$, so their probability is $6/36=1/6$. Sum $4$ arises from $(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)$, giving $3/36=1/12$. Both computations use equally likely ordered pairs, justified by fairness and independence. Merely counting the possible sums would give the wrong weights.
