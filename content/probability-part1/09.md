# An outcome can be an entire path

The stock-price picture expands our idea of an outcome. We can record a single future price, but we can also record the whole sequence of prices over the next $100$ days. One outcome can contain many numbers.

## Read the paths as possible futures

The source starts with a price of $125.50$ and sketches several possible future paths. Each path is one possible evolution, not a collection of equally likely choices and not a forecast supplied with probabilities.

If we observe once per day, write an outcome as

$$\omega=(p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_{100}),$$

with the common starting value $p_0=125.50$. In a simple model allowing nonnegative prices, the ambient outcome space can be $[0,\infty)^{100}$. A more specific model may constrain which paths are possible, and actual quoted prices may lie on a discrete tick grid.

If we record only the day-$100$ price, our outcome is just $p_{100}$. Both experiments are legitimate; they retain different information.

## Events are questions about paths

Consider three possible questions:

- Does the final price exceed $150$?
- Does the price ever fall below $100$ during the period?
- Is the final price above its initial $125.50$?

The first and third can be answered from the endpoint. The second requires the path: two histories can end at the same price while one dipped below $100$ and the other never did.

Mathematically, “ever below $100$” means that at least one coordinate satisfies $p_t<100$. Later, unions of events will give us a compact way to express that “at least one” condition.

## The picture is not yet a probability model

A sample space says what can happen. A probability model says how probability is assigned to those possibilities. To calculate a chance from the picture, we would need assumptions about the price process, dependence between days, and other relevant information. Counting the few illustrative lines in the diagram would not establish a probability.

This distinction applies beyond finance: hourly temperatures, queues throughout a day, and a user's sequence of website actions can all be treated as random paths.
