# From two events to a whole collection

A rule involving two events should still make sense when there are three, a hundred, or a countable sequence of events. This transition slide extends two familiar questions: **Did at least one event happen? Did every event happen?**

## Think of events as tests on the same outcome

Roll a die once, so $S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$. Consider three tests:

$$
E_1=\{2,4,6\},\qquad
E_2=\{4,5,6\},\qquad
E_3=\{3,6\}.
$$

These mean “even,” “at least four,” and “a multiple of three.” A single observed outcome is checked against all three sets. The indices label events; they do not automatically mean three separate die rolls.

| Outcome | In the union? | In the intersection? |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Yes: it passes the first test. | No: it fails the other two. |
| 4 | Yes: it passes the first and second tests. | No: it fails the third. |
| 6 | Yes: it passes all three tests. | Yes: it passes all three tests. |
| 1 | No: it fails every test. | No. |

Consequently,

$$
E_1\cup E_2\cup E_3=\{2,3,4,5,6\},
\qquad
E_1\cap E_2\cap E_3=\{6\}.
$$

## The meaning survives a longer list

A union accepts an outcome when **at least one** test accepts it. Two or more successes are allowed: “or” is inclusive. An intersection accepts an outcome only when **every** test accepts it.

For a finite collection, associativity lets us regroup without changing membership:

$$
(E_1\cup E_2)\cup E_3=E_1\cup(E_2\cup E_3),
$$

and likewise for intersection. We can therefore omit unnecessary parentheses. The next two pages introduce one large symbol for the entire operation.

The practical distinction is easy to remember. A union is a choice of routes into an event; an intersection is a checklist of requirements. Adding another route can enlarge the union. Adding another requirement can shrink the intersection.

## Check your understanding

A scholarship requires a passing exam, a submitted application, and eligibility. Should the successful event be a union or an intersection? What would the union describe?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Translate the requirements</summary>

Success requires all three conditions, so it is their intersection. The union would include anyone who satisfies at least one condition—including someone who submitted an application but failed the exam. The same event names can produce very different sets depending on the logical connector.
