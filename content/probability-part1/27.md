# One die, three event operations

The same two event sets produce three different answers depending on the question. A union widens acceptance, an intersection narrows it, and a complement reverses one event's test within the sample space.

## Set up the source example

Roll a standard six-sided die and define

$$
S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\},
$$

$$
A=\{2,4,6\}\quad\text{(even)},
\qquad
B=\{1,2,3\}\quad\text{(less than 4)}.
$$

Before calculating, make a prediction: which faces satisfy at least one condition, which satisfy both, and which fail the evenness condition?

## Check every face once

| Face | In $A$? | In $B$? | In $A\cup B$? | In $A\cap B$? | In $A^c$? |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| 2 | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| 3 | No | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| 4 | Yes | No | Yes | No | No |
| 5 | No | No | No | No | Yes |
| 6 | Yes | No | Yes | No | No |

Therefore the three requested answers are

$$
A\cup B=\{1,2,3,4,6\},
$$

$$
A\cap B=\{2\},
$$

$$
\overline A=A^c=\{1,3,5\}.
$$

The face 2 enters the union once, even though it satisfies both conditions. The face 5 enters the complement of $A$ despite belonging to neither $A$ nor $B$; the complement depends on $S$ and $A$, not on $B$.

## Add a probability model only when needed

The event sets above are correct whether the die is fair or biased. If it is fair, each face has probability $1/6$, giving

$$
P(A\cup B)=\frac56,\quad P(A\cap B)=\frac16,\quad P(A^c)=\frac36=\frac12.
$$

Simply adding $P(A)+P(B)=1/2+1/2$ would count the face 2 twice. Correcting the overlap gives

$$
P(A\cup B)=\frac12+\frac12-\frac16=\frac56.
$$

On a biased die, the set operations stay the same, but probabilities must add the actual weights of the selected faces rather than divide their counts by six.

## Check your understanding

Find $B^c$ and $A\cap B^c$. Explain their meanings in words.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Reveal the answer</summary>

$B^c=\{4,5,6\}$ means “not less than 4,” or “at least 4.” Then $A\cap B^c=\{4,6\}$ means “even and at least 4.” It is the part of $A$ outside $B$.
